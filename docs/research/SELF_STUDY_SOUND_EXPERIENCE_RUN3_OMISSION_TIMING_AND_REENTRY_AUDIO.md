## Research question

**Inside a learned pachislot sound baseline, when do silence, omission, attenuation or timing deviation become meaningful prediction error rather than missing/broken feedback—and how should interruption/re-entry avoid accidentally impersonating an authored audio anomaly?**

This continues Sound Run 2 and directly accepts the Visual & Mechanism Run 3 handoff. Run 2 established that routine sounds should usually confirm prediction and that rare deviations can spend attention authority. Visual Run 3 adds a critical mobile boundary: Safari interruption, viewport disturbance or touch occlusion must not destroy the learned machine map. Sound therefore needs to distinguish an intentional deviation inside continuous play from an accidental gap caused by suspension/resume or device behavior.

---

# 1. Why this theme was selected

Sound Run 2 left a high-value unresolved question:

- a stable repeated action-sound relation can make a missing or delayed sound highly salient;
- real pachislot sound design uses timing, short silence and omission as meaningful presentation devices;
- however, the same absence can also be produced by browser suspension, audio-context resumption, muted output, interruption or an implementation fault;
- if the player cannot distinguish authored anomaly from broken response, audio loses causal authority instead of gaining meaning.

The research problem is therefore not simply "can silence attract attention?" It is:

`WHEN DOES ABSENCE BELONG TO THE MACHINE'S LANGUAGE?`

---

# 2. What was not understood before this study

Before Run 3, Sound & Experience did not have a sufficiently evidence-based answer to:

1. Whether an omitted expected action sound produces a measurable prediction-error response.
2. How strongly expectation reliability matters for omission perception.
3. Whether a repeated rule can remain represented even when an expected sound is absent.
4. How interruption and environmental change affect resumption of an ongoing task.
5. Whether adding sound at re-entry necessarily helps, or can itself become distraction.
6. How these findings connect to real pachislot use of silence / delay without inventing undisclosed machine behavior.
7. What safeguards are needed so an authored silence does not become indistinguishable from audio failure.

# 3. Investigation

## SOURCED FACT — omitted expected self-generated sounds can evoke an omission response

Tast (2024) studied self-generated sounds in which button presses produced a tone with either high or weaker probability. An omission-related N1 response was observed when the action strongly predicted the sound (80% sound occurrence) but not when predictability was weaker (50%). This supports a key boundary: **an omission becomes a strong prediction violation only after a sufficiently reliable action→sound expectation has been learned.**

Source:
- Tast, V. (2024), *Suppression and omission effects in auditory predictive processing—Two of the same?*, European Journal of Neuroscience.
  https://doi.org/10.1111/ejn.16393
  PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38764129/

## SOURCED FACT — ordered auditory regularity leaves information about a sound even when that sound is omitted

Hauswald et al. (2024) reported carrier-frequency-specific neural activity during omissions within ordered tone sequences. Their results support the idea that a learned auditory sequence can generate a representation of an upcoming event even when the physical sound does not occur.

This is not proof that a pachislot player consciously perceives every omission. It is evidence that regular auditory structure can support event-specific predictions rather than treating silence as an informational void.

Source:
- Hauswald, A. et al. (2024), *Carrier-frequency specific omission-related neural activity in ordered sound sequences is independent of omission-predictability*, European Journal of Neuroscience.
  https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38711271/

## SOURCED FACT — contextual cues can materially aid resumption after interruption

Hodgetts & Jones (2006) found that contextual cues encoded around interruption could facilitate recovery of the suspended task goal, while altering those cues after interruption could impede resumption. Their interpretation supports memory-for-goals accounts in which the task context at suspension and retrieval matters.

Source:
- Hodgetts, H. M. & Jones, D. M. (2006), *Contextual cues aid recovery from interruption: the role of associative activation*, Journal of Experimental Psychology: Learning, Memory, and Cognition 32(5), 1120–1132.
  https://doi.org/10.1037/0278-7393.32.5.1120
  Repository abstract: https://orca.cardiff.ac.uk/id/eprint/33005/

## SOURCED FACT — sound can worsen interruption recovery when it acts as competing distraction

Hodgetts, Vachon & Tremblay (2014) used a dynamic radar task and found that interruption impaired decision efficiency and resumption, with the cost larger and longer-lasting in the presence of background sound. This warns against assuming that a conspicuous re-entry sound automatically helps the user reorient.

Source:
- Hodgetts, H. M., Vachon, F. & Tremblay, S. (2014), *Background Sound Impairs Interruption Recovery in Dynamic Task Situations: Procedural Conflict?*, Applied Cognitive Psychology 28(1), 10–21.
  https://doi.org/10.1002/acp.2952

## SOURCED FACT — maintaining access to primary-task cues can reduce interruption disruption

Ratwani & Trafton (2010) compared interruption modalities and found that an auditory interruption with the visual primary task still visible was least disruptive in their experiment. Their eye-movement evidence suggested that maintaining an associative link to the task interface was important for resumption.

Source:
- Ratwani, R. M. & Trafton, J. G. (2010), *An Eye Movement Analysis of the Effect of Interruption Modality on Primary Task Resumption*, Human Factors 52(3).
  https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21077561/

## SOURCED FACT — real pachislot sound production treats timing and silence as authored variables

The existing Chappy5 Sound Reality Baseline records an interview with a current pachislot sound creator describing adjustment of presentation "間" at sub-second resolution, including short silent intervals and deliberate timing deviations. The same baseline also records real-machine examples in which start-sound delay, silence or other deviations have role/state meaning.

# 4. Direct observations from the evidence

## OBSERVATION 1 — absence only works after presence has earned trust

Across omission studies and the real-machine baseline, the useful unit is not "silence" by itself. The useful unit is:

`STABLE EXPECTED EVENT → EXPECTED MOMENT → ABSENCE / ATTENUATION / DELAY`

Without a learned normal relation, the listener has no strong basis for interpreting the absence as a deviation.

## OBSERVATION 2 — reliability determines whether the player can assign meaning to the anomaly

If the same action sometimes produces sound and sometimes does not for no clear reason, the machine teaches uncertainty about its own feedback path. The player may stop treating omission as information and instead classify it as normal unreliability.

## OBSERVATION 3 — interruption is dangerous because it can counterfeit authored silence

A browser/app interruption can create:

- a real gap in continuous BGM;
- missed or delayed action feedback;
- changed phase relationship between visual state and audio state;
- a restart transient or delayed audio-context wakeup.

Those artifacts can resemble exactly the authored tools—silence, delay, attenuation—that pachislot sound uses for meaning.

## OBSERVATION 4 — re-entry audio can also be harmful if it acts like an alert unrelated to the suspended task

Interruption research does not support a simplistic "play a sound when returning" rule. Competing sound can extend resumption cost. The useful target is restoration of the same task context, not introduction of a new attention claim.

# 5. Interpretation

## INTERPRETATION — meaningful omission requires a trusted causal baseline

A sound omission should be treated as a **second-order event**. Its meaning depends on a first-order event having become reliable.

`ACTION / STATE → EXPECTED AUDIO CONSEQUENCE`

Only after this relation is stable can:

`EXPECTED AUDIO CONSEQUENCE → MISSING / WEAK / LATE`

become an interpretable deviation.

Therefore authored silence is not a cheap way to create suspense. It spends the credibility accumulated by routine audio.

## INTERPRETATION — missing feedback and meaningful silence are separated by causal recoverability

A useful authored omission should still leave the player able to answer, soon enough:

- did my action register?
- is the machine still in the same state?
- was the absence itself intentional information?
- what event resolves this anomaly?

If none of these can be resolved, the silence behaves like a fault rather than an authored clue.

This extends Machine Run 3's attention-contract principle: a claim is incomplete until the player can learn how it resolves.

## INTERPRETATION — re-entry should restore baseline before spending anomaly authority

After interruption, the player's first auditory problem is not "what rare thing happened?" but "am I still in the same machine/event context?"

A future production system—if Human later approves one—would therefore need to distinguish:

`RESUME / CONTEXT RESTORATION`

from

`AUTHORED IN-GAME AUDIO DEVIATION`.

This is a research boundary, not a concrete implementation instruction.

## INTERPRETATION — continuity can be an information surface, but only when interruption handling protects it

Case Study 02 showed that sustained BGM continuity can itself carry meaning. That means technical interruption is more than a quality defect: it can corrupt the semantics of the machine if continuity/stoppage is part of the player's learned evidence.

In such systems, "audio resumed eventually" is not sufficient. The experience must preserve which changes belong to game truth and which belong to platform truth.

## INTERPRETATION — attenuation may be safer than total omission in some contexts, but this cannot be generalized yet

Attenuation can preserve action ownership while still creating difference, whereas total omission removes the expected confirmation channel. However, the reviewed evidence does not establish a universal hierarchy among silence, attenuation and delay for pachislot use. This remains unresolved and must not be converted into a preset rule.

# 6. What changed from the previous understanding

Run 2 model:

`SOURCE LEGIBILITY = FAMILY COHERENCE × SUFFICIENT EVENT CONTRAST × TEMPORAL OWNERSHIP × MASKING SURVIVAL × REPETITION/ATTENTION FITNESS`

Run 3 adds a reliability dimension:

`MEANINGFUL ABSENCE = TRUSTED BASELINE × PRECISE EXPECTATION × CAUSAL CONTEXT × RESOLVABLE DEVIATION`

and an interruption boundary:

`PLATFORM GAP MUST NOT MASQUERADE AS GAME TRUTH`.

Key changes:

- **Silence is not an asset; it is a violation of an earned expectation.**
- **An unreliable baseline destroys the informational value of omission.**
- **Audio interruption can corrupt game semantics, not merely continuity.**
- **Re-entry should restore context before asking for new attention.**
- **A meaningful omission needs a learnable resolution path.**

---

# 7. Reusable principles

## REUSABLE PRINCIPLE 1

**`ABSENCE ONLY HAS AUTHORITY AFTER PRESENCE HAS EARNED TRUST.`**

An omitted/delayed/attenuated sound is meaningful only against a sufficiently stable expected baseline.

## REUSABLE PRINCIPLE 2

**`AUDIO ANOMALY IS A SECOND-ORDER EVENT.`**

First establish the normal action/state→sound relation; only then can deviation from that relation carry information.

## REUSABLE PRINCIPLE 3

**`UNRELIABLE FEEDBACK DESTROYS THE SEMANTICS OF SILENCE.`**

If expected sound is inconsistently missing for technical or arbitrary reasons, the player cannot safely learn intentional omission.

## REUSABLE PRINCIPLE 4

**`PLATFORM SILENCE MUST NOT IMPERSONATE GAME SILENCE.`**

Interruption, suspension, audio-context wakeup or output loss must be conceptually separated from authored in-game omission.

## REUSABLE PRINCIPLE 5

**`RE-ENTRY SHOULD RESTORE CONTEXT BEFORE IT SPENDS ATTENTION AUTHORITY.`**

A return from interruption should first help the player recover the known machine/event context rather than behave like a new alert or reward event.

## REUSABLE PRINCIPLE 6

**`MEANINGFUL SILENCE NEEDS A RETURN PATH.`**

The player should be able to learn how the anomaly resolves and when ordinary audio responsibility returns.

## REUSABLE PRINCIPLE 7

**`CONTINUITY CAN BE A SIGNAL SURFACE.`**

When sustained audio itself is a reference, a stoppage may carry meaning; therefore technical continuity errors can become semantic errors.

## REUSABLE PRINCIPLE 8

**`DO NOT CONFUSE RESUMPTION FEEDBACK WITH CELEBRATORY RESET.`**

A re-entry cue that demands fresh orienting can increase recovery cost even if it is technically informative.

## REUSABLE PRINCIPLE 9

**`TEST OMISSION AGAINST FAILURE ATTRIBUTION.`**

Future Human QA should distinguish whether an absent/delayed sound is interpreted as intentional machine information, unregistered input, muted/broken audio, lag or unknown failure.

# 8. Limits and misuse warnings

- Neural omission responses do not directly specify conscious pachislot experience or ideal sound timing.
- Laboratory tones and button-press paradigms are not pachislot cabinets.
- Interruption studies do not authorize a particular iPhone resume sound or implementation.
- Online video cannot validate speaker frequency response, physical low-frequency behavior or real hall masking.
- 新ハナビ evidence is used only as completed real-machine contrast evidence; no melody, sample, signature sound or undisclosed behavior is copied.
- No exact milliseconds, gain values, fade durations or probability values are derived here.
- These principles must not be used to justify making routine action feedback ambiguous in the name of "mystery".

1. **Topic reason** — Sound Run 2 and Visual Run 3 both exposed a conflict between meaningful deviation and accidental missing feedback.
2. **Prior unknown** — no evidence-based boundary existed between authored silence and broken audio response.
3. **Investigation** — auditory omission prediction, action-generated sound expectation, interruption recovery/context cues, background-sound distraction, existing pachislot sound baseline and completed 新ハナビ Sound evidence.
4. **New learning** — omission effects depend on a learned expectation; contextual stability matters for resumption; extra sound can impair recovery; continuous audio can itself become an information surface.
5. **Changed understanding** — silence is a second-order deviation that consumes trust earned by the normal audio baseline.
6. **Reusable principles** — stable baseline first; platform silence must not impersonate game silence; re-entry restores context; meaningful silence needs a return path.
7. **Limits/misuse** — no exact timing/mix rules, no copied pachislot sounds, no assumption that laboratory omission responses equal conscious game excitement.
8. **Remaining unknowns** — target-device resume behavior, Human attribution boundary, redundancy requirements and long-session false-anomaly rate.
9. **Next worthwhile Sound study** — how action confirmation can remain perceptually immediate when actual audio output latency/jitter varies across iPhone/Safari, without compensating by detaching sound from visible/mechanical causality.
10. **Possible future Chappy5 value** — if Human later authorizes production, these principles could protect causal ownership and make sparse audio deviations meaningful without creating failure anxiety or mobile-platform artifacts that masquerade as game information.
