## 1. Cabinet first impression — screen-first, but not screen-only

### SOURCED FACT

Major machine references classify the machine as a 2016 A+ART machine using real BIG and ART `マギカ☆ラッシュ`; ART is approximately 1.5 coins/G and normally begins from 50G+α. The machine therefore must present persistent state, rare-role aftermath, CZ, real BONUS and ART/extension information in addition to the physical reel result.

Sources:
- https://1geki.jp/slot/s_madomagi2/
- https://hazuse.com/machine/pachislot/SX0136/

### DIRECT VISUAL OBSERVATION

Front photographs consistently show a vertically layered cabinet:

`large upper ornamental/lamp region`
→ `large landscape LCD`
→ `compact dedicated physical reel window`
→ `operation deck / 3 STOP row`
→ `large lower illuminated graphic panel`

The LCD is visually much larger than the physical reel window. The reels are nevertheless not overlaid by the LCD; they sit in a distinct framed opening below it. The three STOP buttons sit immediately below the reel area, while the lever/MAX BET zone remains to the player's left and a large secondary PUSH control is located to the right.

The cabinet is identifiable even in a static front photograph through a pale/white body, strong pink-toned framing, a large central jewel-like translucent top element, dark upper grille-like fields, luminous side edges and a broad lower illuminated panel.

### INTERPRETATION

This is a **screen-first / body-map-stable** cabinet. The LCD receives most of the narrative/state-information area, but the physical reel cavity and conventional controls remain spatially independent. The player can therefore change cognitive task without relearning where the primary inputs are.

The large top translucent element and luminous perimeter appear to carry hall-distance identity while the screen/reels carry seated play information. This means recognizability does not require a giant moving mechanism.

### REUSABLE PRINCIPLE

`SCREEN-FIRST DOES NOT HAVE TO MEAN REEL-IRRELEVANT.`

`HALL-DISTANCE IDENTITY CAN BE STATIC WHILE PLAY-STATE INFORMATION REMAINS LOCAL AND DYNAMIC.`

## 2. Physical reel window — small relative to LCD, but protected as a work surface

### SOURCED FACT

Game & Reel Run 1 established that ordinary play uses a BAR landmark and intermittent cherry/watermelon follow branches; ART navigation changes STOP responsibility to order compliance. The visible reel result therefore still has practical work to do even though full internal resolution may continue after STOP3.

See:
- `docs/research/CASE_STUDY_03_MADOKA_MAGICA_2_GAME_REEL_RUN1.md`

### DIRECT VISUAL OBSERVATION

The reel area is a compact horizontal band centered below the LCD. It has its own surrounding frame/bezel, with the physical strips visibly recessed behind the front opening. The upper/lower edges of the opening occlude the reel circumference, making the strips read as rotating bodies rather than three flat icon columns.

The reel symbols remain relatively large within that compact window. The three STOP controls below map directly to left/middle/right reel positions.

### INTERPRETATION

The reel opening appears to have measurable front-to-back separation from the front bezel, but no authoritative depth dimension was found. `DEPTH = INTERPRETATION`, not sourced specification.

The critical mechanism lesson is not “make reels large.” It is to preserve a **dedicated readable work surface** even when another display is much larger. The reel strip is small in total cabinet share but visually fenced off from the LCD.

This arrangement supports Game & Reel Run 1's finding that motor duty is intermittent. Most games do not require New-Hanabi-level reel study; when a cherry/watermelon branch or navigation appears, the eyes can drop to an already stable, high-contrast physical zone.

### REUSABLE PRINCIPLE

`A SMALLER PHYSICAL WORK SURFACE CAN REMAIN PRIMARY FOR ACTION IF ITS BOUNDARY, SCALE AND BODY MAPPING ARE STABLE.`

`DISPLAY AREA AND ACTION IMPORTANCE ARE NOT THE SAME THING.`

## 3. Operation deck — state changes preserve the learned body coordinates

### DIRECT VISUAL OBSERVATION

Real-machine photographs show:
- lever at lower-left;
- MAX BET control above/right of the lever zone;
- three round STOP buttons aligned horizontally below the reels;
- a large rectangular PUSH control to the right of the reel window;
- additional small controls/status display to the left of the reels;
- medal/input hardware to the right side of the deck.

The primary 3 STOP row does not move or change physical geometry between normal, CZ, BONUS or ART.

### INTERPRETATION

The machine can ask for at least three different input contracts while preserving one physical map:

`ordinary role coverage`
→ `ART navigation / order compliance`
→ `BONUS-related aiming or confirmation`

The LCD can change the instruction while the hand stays anchored to the same three positions. The large PUSH control provides a secondary interaction channel without replacing the reel-stop row.

### REUSABLE PRINCIPLE

`STATE COMPLEXITY CAN LIVE ABOVE A STABLE PHYSICAL BODY MAP.`

`TRANSITIONS SHOULD CHANGE CONTEXT BEFORE THEY CHANGE COORDINATES.`

This directly supports the Game & Reel principle:
`STOP RESPONSIBILITY CAN SHIFT WITHOUT MOVING THE PLAYER'S BODY MAP.`

## 4. The LCD is not simply decoration — it holds unresolved consequence after STOP3

### SOURCED FACT

Normal play has documented low/high/super-high internal states. Public stage information includes ordinary stages and higher-expectation/precursor stages; examples published by NANA PRESS/P-WORLD include ordinary home/school/after-school-type stages, a high-state suggestion stage, a witch-exploration precursor stage, and a special CZ-related stage.

Sources:
- https://nana-press.com/kaiseki/machine/50/910/
- https://www.p-world.co.jp/machine/database/8129

Game & Reel Run 1 also established that visible role identification and complete event resolution are separated: a rare-role stopping form can still feed simultaneous BONUS, hidden-state movement, CZ/direct ART or later reward-state information.

### INTERPRETATION

This gives the LCD a specific responsibility that New Hanabi did not need to the same degree. STOP3 can finish **what physically stopped**, while the screen continues **what that event means for the current state**.

A useful visual timeline is therefore:

`REELS CLASSIFY EVENT`
→ `LCD/STATE PRESENTATION HOLDS CONSEQUENCE OPEN`
→ `FOLLOW-UP STATE / CZ / BONUS / ART RESOLVES IT`

The screen should not be interpreted as competing with the reels on every game. In this architecture it carries information that cannot be fully expressed by the final three-reel tableau alone.

### REUSABLE PRINCIPLE

`STOP3 CAN CLOSE ROLE IDENTITY WHILE PRESENTATION KEEPS OUTCOME OPEN.`

`SECONDARY PRESENTATION EARNS ITS AREA WHEN IT CARRIES INFORMATION THE PHYSICAL RESULT CANNOT YET RESOLVE.`

## 5. NORMAL REST VISUAL — identity remains present before escalation

### DIRECT VISUAL OBSERVATION

In static real-machine photographs, the cabinet retains visual identity without an active jackpot sequence:
- large fixed top translucent/jewel-like centerpiece;
- dark upper grille-like fields framing it;
- illuminated perimeter/side channels;
- side translucent graphic/light regions;
- lit controls;
- fixed lower illuminated panel;
- LCD occupying the largest active information surface;
- reel cavity and STOP row continuously visible.

No evidence was found that a large mechanical object must continuously move during ordinary play for the machine to remain identifiable.

### INTERPRETATION

The REST state is not visually empty. It is a stable composition of fixed silhouette + light + screen + reels + deck. High-intensity sequences can therefore change **content, brightness, motion density and timing** while the cabinet's physical identity remains intact.

This is the opposite of treating normal as “everything off until a strong effect.” It also protects escalation: permanent maximum movement would consume the contrast needed for CZ/BONUS/ART transitions.

### REUSABLE PRINCIPLE

`REST VISUAL IS AN ACTIVE BASELINE, NOT AN ABSENCE OF PRESENTATION.`

`STATIC FORM CAN CARRY IDENTITY SO DYNAMIC CHANGE CAN BE RESERVED FOR GAME MEANING.`

## 6. Normal → precursor/CZ — screen changes context, not the player's workplace

### SOURCED FACT

Published stage descriptions explicitly associate different LCD stages with ordinary play, higher-state suggestion and precursor/CZ expectation. CZ `魔女の結界` is a separate play state with role-dependent ART conversion.

Sources:
- https://nana-press.com/kaiseki/machine/50/910/
- https://hazuse.com/machine/pachislot/SX0136/

### INTERPRETATION

The visual escalation in this family is primarily a **context-layer escalation**. Scene/background/content on the large LCD can increase expectation while the reel window, STOP row and lever remain spatially constant.

That stability matters because a rare role can first be identified on the reel, then become relevant to the LCD state. If the visual transition also displaced or obscured the reel workspace, the player would pay a reorientation cost exactly when the role needs to be read.

### REUSABLE PRINCIPLE

`A STATE TRANSITION SHOULD RAISE INFORMATION DENSITY WITHOUT NEEDLESSLY REBUILDING THE PLAYER'S MOTOR MAP.`

## 7. BONUS — foreground reward layer with explicit display interaction

### SOURCED FACT

P-WORLD's archived system description states that BIG provides about 150 coins and, at BIG start, the player can choose the notification type/BGM through the LCD touch interface. Public descriptions distinguish chance-notification, full-notification and late-notification styles. During ordinary play the BIG can feed ART lottery; during ART the bonus can feed the ongoing ART reward economy.

Source:
- https://www.p-world.co.jp/machine/database/8129

### INTERPRETATION

This is important for cabinet integration: the large LCD is not only a passive movie screen. At BONUS entry it can temporarily become a **direct choice surface**, while the physical reels and STOP controls remain the base gambling input below it.

Real BONUS therefore becomes a foreground state that can momentarily expand screen interaction without requiring a new hardware map.

When BONUS occurs inside ART, the presentation challenge is to communicate “additional reward inside an existing persistent state,” not “the old state vanished and an unrelated game started.” The public game structure supports this interpretation because ART value can persist/be reinforced across the BONUS event.

### REUSABLE PRINCIPLE

`A TEMPORARY REWARD LAYER MAY TAKE VISUAL FOREGROUND WITHOUT ERASING THE PERSISTENT STATE IT ENRICHES.`

`ADDITIVE REWARDS SHOULD VISUALLY RETURN TO THE VALUE CONTRACT THEY MODIFIED.`

## 8. ART / extension — navigation can change STOP responsibility without moving the reels

### SOURCED FACT

ART `マギカ☆ラッシュ` is a persistent game-count state and uses navigation; special extension states modify the value of familiar outcomes rather than replacing the physical three-reel control system.

Sources:
- https://1geki.jp/slot/s_madomagi2/
- `docs/research/CASE_STUDY_03_MADOKA_MAGICA_2_GAME_REEL_RUN1.md`

### INTERPRETATION

For Visual QA, navigation has one essential job: make the required STOP responsibility unmistakable **while leaving left/middle/right spatial correspondence intact**.

The cabinet already provides a strong physical mapping: reel above button. A screen-heavy ART state should therefore add instruction above that map, not create a second competing map elsewhere.

Extension states can become visually denser because the per-game reward meaning is denser, but the stable reel/deck area provides a re-anchor point when that phase ends.

### REUSABLE PRINCIPLE

`NAVIGATION SHOULD ANNOTATE THE BODY MAP, NOT REPLACE IT.`

`HIGH-STATE VISUAL DENSITY NEEDS A STABLE PHYSICAL RE-ANCHOR.`

## 9. Hall identity without giant moving mechanisms

### DIRECT VISUAL OBSERVATION

Front and hall photographs show that the cabinet remains recognizable through fixed architecture:
- unusual top-center translucent form;
- bright side perimeter lighting;
- white/pink shell contrast;
- dark upper fields;
- large landscape screen;
- centered compact reel cavity;
- luminous lower panel.

The silhouette and luminous zones remain legible even when the LCD content itself changes.

### INTERPRETATION

A noisy hall requires two kinds of recognition:

`DISTANCE IDENTITY` — silhouette, large light zones, major contrast blocks;

`SEATED PLAY IDENTITY` — screen language, reel workspace, button positions, state cues.

The first need not animate constantly. A stable luminous silhouette can get the player to the machine; dynamic screen/lamp behavior can then communicate game state at seated distance.

### REUSABLE PRINCIPLE

`DISTANCE PRESENCE AND SEATED INFORMATION SHOULD BE DESIGNED AS DIFFERENT SCALES.`

`A CABINET DOES NOT NEED TO MOVE ITS LARGEST OBJECT TO PROVE THAT IT IS ACTIVE.`

## 11. Contrast with Case Study 01 / 02

### INTERPRETATION

**スマスロ北斗の拳** taught that a large screen can coexist with physical reels when the screen carries future-state interpretation and the fixed cabinet/body map remains trusted.

**新ハナビ** taught that the reels themselves can be the dominant interpretation surface and that quiet static form can reserve dynamic change for meaningful deviations.

**まどマギ2** adds a third architecture:

`LARGE LCD = persistent context / unresolved consequence / state transition`

`PHYSICAL REELS = role classification / loss-prevention / nav execution`

`FIXED DECK = motor continuity across normal / CZ / BONUS / ART`

`STATIC LUMINOUS CABINET = hall identity`

The key learning is therefore not “large LCD versus reels.” It is **which layer owns which truth, and whether those truths hand off cleanly over time**.

### REUSABLE PRINCIPLE

`VISUAL HIERARCHY SHOULD FOLLOW INFORMATION OWNERSHIP, NOT PIXEL AREA.`

## 13. Visual & Mechanism Run 1 synthesis

Case Study 03 demonstrates a machine where **the LCD is the dominant visual area but not the only truth surface**.

The real cabinet preserves a stable chain:

`SCREEN CONTEXT`
→ `PHYSICAL REEL EVENT`
→ `FIXED 3STOP BODY MAP`
→ `SCREEN/STATE CONSEQUENCE`
→ `REWARD/STATE TRANSITION`
→ `RETURN TO THE SAME PHYSICAL ANCHOR`

The strongest cross-case abstraction from this run is:

`SCREEN-FIRST DOES NOT HAVE TO MEAN REEL-IRRELEVANT.`

and, more specifically:

`THE SCREEN MAY HOLD CONSEQUENCE OPEN AFTER THE REELS CLOSE ROLE IDENTITY, PROVIDED THE PLAYER NEVER LOSES THE PHYSICAL ANCHOR FOR THE NEXT ACTION.`

No implementation approval follows from this research.
