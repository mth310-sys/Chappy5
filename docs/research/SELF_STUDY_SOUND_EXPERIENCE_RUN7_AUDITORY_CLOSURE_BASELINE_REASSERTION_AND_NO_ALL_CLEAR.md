# 1. Why this topic is next

Machine Run 7 separated `RESPONSIBILITY RESOLVED` from `ATTENTION SET RELEASED`.

Game & Reel Run 7 then established that closure can be responsibility-specific: one role duty may close while a new inference duty opens.

Visual & Mechanism Run 7 added that the surface which closes the question should remain legible until the next grammar is secure, and warned against adding a new visual `ALL CLEAR` message.

The Sound-side unresolved question is therefore:

**When temporary auditory priority correctly opened responsibility, how can audio release that priority without introducing a second mandatory “closure sound” that itself becomes another thing the player must monitor?**

---

# 2. Prior unknown

Sound Run 6 established:

`OPEN -> HOLD -> RELEASE`

and:

- responsibility, not novelty, earns interruption authority;
- timing deviation can reopen a question without level escalation;
- omission can carry meaning only after continuity becomes predictable;
- audio may reopen reel attention but should not pre-empt reel interpretation;
- good interruption requires an exit condition.

What remained underspecified was **what counts as sufficient RELEASE evidence**.

A simplistic implementation would add an explicit sound meaning “temporary duty finished.” That can be counterproductive because it creates a new target for monitoring and may falsely close a duty that visual/reel evidence still leaves unresolved.

# 3. Closed real-machine anchor — 新ハナビ

## 3.1 Temporary procedure audio does not need permanent ownership

### SOURCED FACT

Published 新ハナビ analysis documents temporary RT procedures in which prediction/audio cues alter stopping responsibility in 花火チャレンジ / 花火GAME, while ordinary play continues to use the same lever and three STOP buttons.

Sources retained from the closed Case Study:
- https://nana-press.com/kaiseki/machine/134/3869/
- https://nana-press.com/kaiseki/machine/134/3873/
- https://1geki.jp/slot/s_shinhanabi/64/

### OBSERVATION

The cue's usefulness is bounded by the procedure it opens. Once that local action is performed and the game proceeds, the player does not benefit from having to keep listening for a second confirmation merely to know that the same temporary cue is no longer governing the next action.

### INTERPRETATION

Audio priority can expire through **loss of applicability**, not necessarily through a dedicated closure event.

### REUSABLE PRINCIPLE

`A TEMPORARY AUDIO RULE MAY CLOSE BECAUSE ITS ACTION LAW NO LONGER APPLIES.`

`DO NOT REQUIRE A SECOND SOUND TO CONFIRM THAT THE FIRST SOUND STOPPED MATTERING.`

---

## 3.2 BGM continuity / interruption demonstrates that absence can change meaning

### SOURCED FACT

Published 新ハナビ material documents BGM cessation during RT as bonus information. Continuity therefore functions as a learned state surface, and an interruption may carry rule value.

Source:
- https://1geki.jp/slot/s_shinhanabi/64/

Auditory neuroscience literature also shows that offsets and omissions of expected sounds are perceptually/neuronally processed events rather than literal “nothing.” Sound offsets elicit measurable responses, especially under attention, and omission responses depend on learned predictability.

Research anchors:
- Yamashiro et al., `Attention-dependent sound offset-related brain potentials`, Psychophysiology, 2016. PMID 26757414.
- `The sound of silence: Omission responses and how the brain predicts in the absence of sound`, Neuroscience & Biobehavioral Reviews, 2025. PMID 41354114.

### INTERPRETATION

This creates an important asymmetry:

- an **authored omission** may legitimately reopen or change responsibility when a continuous baseline has earned predictive meaning;
- but the **return of ordinary baseline audio** does not automatically need its own celebratory or confirmation marker.

Once the special rule has logically resolved, familiar routine timing/continuity can simply regain authority.

### REUSABLE PRINCIPLE

`AUDITORY ABSENCE CAN BE AN EVENT; AUDITORY RETURN DOES NOT ALWAYS NEED TO BE ONE.`

`BASELINE REASSERTION IS STRONGEST WHEN THE PLAYER CAN STOP CHECKING FOR IT.`

# 4. Closure audio must not overrule unresolved reel evidence

### CLOSED CASE ANCHOR

新ハナビ's published game structure repeatedly leaves the stopped reels as the proof surface. Audio/timing cues may redirect attention or procedure, but the visible stop form still carries role/inference meaning.

### INTERPRETATION

If reel evidence still leaves an inference open, a cheerful/definitive “done” cue would create a cross-modal contradiction:

`AUDIO SAYS CLOSED / REEL SAYS QUESTION STILL OPEN`

That is not a harmless presentation mismatch. It teaches the player to distrust one channel or to privilege an arbitrary hierarchy.

Therefore Sound closure must be subordinate to logical closure established by Game/Reel state and preserved by Visual evidence.

### REUSABLE PRINCIPLE

`AUDIO MUST NOT CLOSE A QUESTION THE REELS STILL KEEP OPEN.`

`CLOSURE AUTHORITY BELONGS TO THE EVENT LOGIC; AUDIO MAY SUPPORT IT, NOT OVERRULE IT.`

`THE PROOF CHANNEL SHOULD DETERMINE WHETHER RELEASE IS LEGITIMATE.`

# 5. Three legitimate auditory closure modes

This run distinguishes three non-exclusive ways temporary auditory priority can end.

## MODE A — NATURAL EXPIRY

The cue opened a single local action duty. The duty is performed; the next game/action law no longer references that cue.

No new sound is required.

`CUE -> DUTY -> ACTION -> APPLICABILITY ENDS -> BASELINE`

Best when the player can infer closure from the action/state itself.

## MODE B — BASELINE REASSERTION

A temporary audio condition ends and the familiar ordinary sound grammar resumes in its usual causal place.

The ordinary sound is not an “all clear” message. It is simply the old machine law becoming valid again.

`SPECIAL AUDIO GRAMMAR -> LOGICAL RESOLUTION -> FAMILIAR BASELINE GRAMMAR RETURNS`

This is analogous to Visual Run 7's `RESTORE THE GRAMMAR, NOT AN ALL CLEAR MESSAGE.`

## MODE C — RESOLUTION-BOUND FEEDBACK

An event already requires local result feedback because the player performed a judged action (e.g., technical intervention). That feedback may simultaneously mark the local duty's completion.

Crucially, it is not an extra closure sound layered after the existing result; it is the **same causal feedback** doing two compatible jobs.

`ACTION -> JUDGMENT/RESULT FEEDBACK -> LOCAL DUTY CLOSED`

### REUSABLE PRINCIPLE

`THE BEST AUDITORY CLOSURE OFTEN REUSES CAUSAL FEEDBACK THAT ALREADY HAD A JOB.`

`CLOSURE SHOULD NOT CREATE A NEW EVENT WHEN AN EXISTING EVENT ALREADY PROVES COMPLETION.`

# 6. Why a dedicated “all clear” sound is risky

## 6.1 It adds a new monitoring obligation

If the player learns that a temporary responsibility is not safely closed until a closure tone arrives, the machine has created a two-cue protocol:

`OPEN CUE -> ACTION -> WAIT FOR CLOSE CUE`

This can turn a short local intervention into prolonged defensive listening.

### REUSABLE PRINCIPLE

`IF RELEASE REQUIRES LISTENING FOR RELEASE, MONITORING HAS NOT ACTUALLY RELEASED.`

## 6.2 It can generate task-set after-effects

### SOURCED FACT

Prospective-memory/task-switching studies show that responding to special target cues can slow subsequent ongoing-task performance even without ongoing strategic monitoring, and that overlap between the temporary and ongoing task influences the persistence of this after-effect.

Sources:
- Meier & Rey-Mermet, `Beyond monitoring: after-effects of responding to prospective memory targets`, Consciousness and Cognition, 2012. PMID 23064406.
- Meier et al., `After-effects without monitoring costs: The impact of prospective memory instructions on task switching performance`, 2017. PMID 28477841.

### INTERPRETATION

These are not pachislot timing studies and do not justify numerical cooldowns. They support a narrower concern: **a temporary task can remain behaviorally active after the target event has been handled**.

Adding a closure cue does not guarantee that the task set is cognitively gone. It may instead prolong the cue-response episode or become another target.

### REUSABLE PRINCIPLE

`AN EXPLICIT CLOSURE SIGNAL DOES NOT GUARANTEE ATTENTION-SET CLOSURE.`

`MEASURE RETURN TO BASELINE BEHAVIOR, NOT THE PRESENCE OF A RELEASE SOUND.`

# 7. Baseline reassertion must be causally trustworthy

A familiar routine sound can only regain baseline authority if:

1. it occurs at the familiar causal boundary;
2. it has the same routine semantic status as before;
3. no unresolved state still makes that sound ambiguous;
4. it does not require a one-off “special return variant” to be noticed;
5. visual/reel state also permits the ordinary action grammar.

If the ordinary sound returns early while the special responsibility remains active, it becomes false reassurance.

### REUSABLE PRINCIPLE

`BASELINE AUDIO MAY REASSERT AUTHORITY ONLY WHEN BASELINE ACTION LAW IS ACTUALLY VALID AGAIN.`

`FAMILIAR SOUND + WRONG STATE = FALSE CALM.`

`RESTORING A SOUND BED IS NOT THE SAME AS RESTORING THE PLAYER'S GRAMMAR.`

---

# 8. Closure should be responsibility-specific

Game & Reel Run 7 showed that one duty can close while another opens. Audio must follow the same rule.

Example abstract structure:

`ROLE-HANDLING DUTY CLOSES -> INFERENCE DUTY OPENS`

A local success/settling cue may legitimately close the motor/technical duty while leaving the stopped-reel interpretation open.

Therefore Sound must not use a global semantic category equivalent to `EVERYTHING RESOLVED` unless the whole event logic actually supports it.

### REUSABLE PRINCIPLE

`AUDITORY CLOSURE IS DUTY-SPECIFIC, NOT A GLOBAL CALM FLAG.`

`ONE SOUND MAY CLOSE MOTOR DUTY WITHOUT CLOSING INFERENCE DUTY.`

`DO NOT COLLAPSE MULTIPLE RESPONSIBILITIES INTO ONE “SUCCESS” SEMANTIC.`

---

# 9. Closure should reduce channel demand

The target experience is not silence for its own sake. It is a reduction in required listening.

Healthy release:

- player no longer waits for an audio confirmation;
- routine action-acceptance sounds become background again;
- meaningful future deviations remain detectable;
- reels remain the primary proof surface;
- ordinary motor rhythm returns without a new checklist.

Unhealthy release:

- player waits one extra beat “to make sure”;
- checks whether BGM/ambient bed really returned;
- treats every dropout as suspicious;
- continues searching for the previous cue;
- trusts a closure sound over contradictory reel evidence.

### REUSABLE PRINCIPLE

`GOOD RELEASE REMOVES AN AUDIO CHECK.`

`A RELEASE THAT ADDS A VERIFICATION STEP IS NOT RELEASE.`

`LOW-MONITORING BASELINE IS THE OUTCOME; SILENCE IS ONLY ONE POSSIBLE ACOUSTIC CONDITION.`

# 10. Integrated Sound model

Previous model:

`OPEN -> HOLD -> RELEASE`

Run 7 refines RELEASE into:

`LOGICAL RESOLUTION -> CLOSURE EVIDENCE -> AUDIO PRIORITY EXPIRES -> BASELINE GRAMMAR REASSERTS -> LISTENING DUTY COMPRESSES`

Full model:

`PREDICTABLE BASELINE -> LEGITIMATE AUDITORY DEVIATION -> RESPONSIBILITY OPEN -> MINIMAL HOLD -> ACTION / INFERENCE -> LOGICAL RESOLUTION -> EVIDENCE PERSISTS -> AUDIO PRIORITY EXPIRES -> BASELINE REASSERTION -> LOW-MONITORING LISTENING`

Central rule:

`GOOD AUDITORY CLOSURE RESTORES LISTENING GRAMMAR WITHOUT ADDING A NEW THING TO LISTEN FOR.`

Short form:

`DO NOT SOUND THE ALL CLEAR; MAKE THE OLD RULE TRUSTWORTHY AGAIN.`

# 11. Changed understanding

Before this run, `RELEASE` could be misread as an audio event that tells the player the temporary state ended.

The stronger formulation is:

**Release is a reduction in auditory responsibility, not necessarily an audible event.**

The best closure may be:

- the natural end of cue applicability;
- an existing result feedback that already proves the local task complete;
- ordinary machine audio simply resuming its former causal role after the game state genuinely returns.

This aligns Sound with Visual Run 7: the system should restore grammar rather than add an “all clear” layer.

# 13. Limits / misuse risks

- Cognitive task-switching and prospective-memory studies are not pachislot specification sources; no reaction-time values, cooldown durations or exact cue timings are imported.
- Sound-offset/omission ERP evidence does not prove conscious detection of a particular pachislot audio transition.
- Published 新ハナビ material establishes documented cue/procedure relationships, not proprietary waveform design, speaker routing, SPL, EQ, exact delay values, mixing or hidden control logic.
- No third-party sound, melody, voice, sample, signature jingle or cabinet-specific audio asset is reproduced.
- This study does not authorize audio design for an original Chappy5 machine.

# 14. Remaining unknowns

1. In real repeated play, how many games does post-cue checking persist after a temporary auditory responsibility logically resolves?
2. Can ordinary baseline audio reassert authority without an explicit close cue under high visual/reel workload?
3. When local technical feedback closes motor duty but reel evidence opens inference duty, does the player correctly separate those two responsibilities?
4. What forms of audio dropout are interpreted as authored omission versus technical failure in an iPhone/browser context?
5. How should future QA detect false calm: the player stops listening because the baseline is trustworthy versus because they have given up on the audio channel?

# 15. Next worthwhile study

Playtest & QA Director should test the distinction:

**After a responsibility-bearing sound correctly triggers a changed action, does the player return to ordinary play because the game grammar is genuinely restored, or do they continue waiting/checking for an explicit auditory closure?**

Future Human Trial, only when authorized, should compare:

- correct next-action recovery;
- unnecessary post-event listening/checking;
- false alarms in the following ordinary games;
- reel interpretation accuracy;
- whether a local skill/result cue is mistaken for global resolution;
- whether ordinary routine timing becomes background again.

Do not prime the Human by teaching an “all clear” category before measuring spontaneous behavior.
