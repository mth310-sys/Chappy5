## 0. Why this machine is selected

Case Study 03 is deliberately selected to cover a missing architecture identified in Machine Reality research: **A+ART**.

This machine combines:
- real BONUS as an independent reward event;
- ART `マギカ☆ラッシュ` as a longer game-count reward state;
- CZ / rare-role routes into ART;
- ART game-count extension / special extension zones;
- state-dependent value of small roles and rare roles;
- normal play, bonus play and ART play sharing the same physical lever/reels/STOP inputs while assigning them different meanings.

This makes it structurally different from both earlier case studies.

The selection is not an endorsement of the third-party IP or protected presentation. Chappy5 must not copy characters, artwork, music, voices, proprietary animation, branding, protected reel art or signature effects.

## 3. Why A+ART is a necessary third lineage

Case Study 01 asks largely:

`How does future state projection make the next game valuable?`

Case Study 02 asks largely:

`How does present reel interpretation make the current game valuable?`

Case Study 03 should ask:

`How do independently valuable BONUS and a persistent ART state coexist without one making the other feel like transport?`

A+ART also lets the five Directors study a new integration problem:

`NORMAL → BONUS / CZ / DIRECT ART → ART → GAME-COUNT EXTENSION / BONUS DURING ART → ART RESUME / END → NORMAL`

This is neither a shallow bonus-only loop nor a modern upper-state ladder.

## 4. Phase A — shared real-machine research questions

### Machine Director
- Why did this product architecture exist in 2016 and what does it preserve/modernize from its predecessor where sourced?
- Why does a player sit down: IP attraction, setting expectation, bonus expectation, CZ/ART route, extension-state attraction, long-session motive?
- How are normal, BONUS, ART and extension zones valued independently?
- Does real BONUS interrupt ART, reinforce ART, or serve as a parallel reward layer?
- What is the post-BONUS / post-ART return contract?
- Can a 3-hour session remain meaningful without rare extension states?

### Game & Reel Director
- Actual reel array, valid lines, symbol roles and bonus combinations.
- Small-role / rare-role structure and loss-prevention routine.
- Internal role versus observed reel form where documented.
- Normal stopping routine, bonus detection and bonus alignment.
- How role value changes between normal / CZ / BONUS / ART.
- How ART game-count extension is triggered and how physical reel stopping remains relevant.
- Setting differences: bonus, ART direct entry, weak-role samples, CZ routes and other documented inference channels.
- No undisclosed stop-control invention.

### Visual & Mechanism Director
- Real cabinet silhouette, depth, screen/reel hierarchy and operation deck.
- How dedicated cabinet ornament/lamps coexist with a large LCD and physical reels.
- REST visual during normal play versus CZ, BONUS, ART and extension states.
- Whether reels remain visually readable during screen-heavy sequences.
- Mechanism/light behavior and what remains physically static.
- Explicitly label inferred materials/depth as interpretation.

### Sound & Experience Director
- Baseline BET/lever/reel/STOP/payout/replay sound contract.
- Normal-stage BGM/ambient behavior and repetition burden.
- Rare-role / precursor / CZ / bonus-entry audio hierarchy.
- BONUS audio as reward versus ART audio as sustain.
- How audio changes when BONUS occurs inside ART.
- How long ART avoids permanent maximum intensity.
- Speaker/low-frequency behavior only where documented or directly observable.
- Do not reproduce protected music, voices or signature sound samples.

### Playtest & QA Director
- First 5 minutes: what must a beginner watch/aim/understand?
- Normal 100G and 500G dry-spell structure.
- CZ failure truth and residue.
- BONUS-only session satisfaction without ART.
- ART entry satisfaction without major extension.
- BONUS inside ART: additive reward or attention overload?
- 3-hour fatigue from screen attention, rare-role vigilance and game-count management.
- Separate actual enjoyment from IP attachment, payout relief and setting/expectation-value motive.

## 6. Phase B — smartphone app study, deferred but defined

Once Phase A is formally completed by all five fixed Directors, the same machine moves to its smartphone app.

Study app-specific translation rather than just “reproduction quality”:
- full-cabinet framing versus cropped playfield;
- reel size/readability and stop-button hit targets;
- touch input replacing physical lever/STOP travel;
- cabinet depth/material/light translated into a flat display;
- speaker/cabinet vibration losses and any software substitute;
- UI overlays, data counter, menus and service-layer information;
- auto play / speed controls / forced-state tools if present;
- timing/animation compression or altered wait behavior;
- audio omissions or licensing differences;
- device performance, resolution and readability;
- what real-machine information becomes easier/harder to perceive.

Do not assume the app is a perfect reference model. Treat it as a **translation product**.

## 7. Phase C — translation study, deferred

After app study:

`REAL MACHINE PROPERTY → APP PRESERVATION / LOSS / SUBSTITUTE → PLAYER EXPERIENCE EFFECT`

Examples of categories to compare:
- physical depth → shading/perspective/cropping;
- tactile STOP → touch target + audiovisual response;
- cabinet sound field → phone speaker/headphone mix;
- seated gaze path → portrait-phone gaze path;
- hall-distance identity → icon/title/loading/store identity;
- real wait/fatigue → app speed/auto convenience;
- physical reel readability → pixel scale/frame rate/zoom.

The purpose is to learn digital translation principles, not to copy the third-party app UI.
