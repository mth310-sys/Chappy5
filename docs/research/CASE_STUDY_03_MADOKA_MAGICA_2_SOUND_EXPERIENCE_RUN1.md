## 1. The key A+ART audio problem: reward foreground versus persistent-state background

### SOURCED FACT

The machine is a real BONUS + ART machine. Major references describe BIG as a 150-coin real bonus and `マギカ☆ラッシュ` as a 50G+α, approximately 1.5 coins/G ART. ART can be entered through CZ, rare-role, bonus and ceiling routes.

Sources:
- P-WORLD machine database: https://www.p-world.co.jp/machine/database/8129
- Ichigeki machine summary: https://1geki.jp/slot/s_madomagi2/
- Ichigeki ART summary: https://1geki.jp/slot/s_madomagi2/81/

### INTERPRETATION

The real acoustic challenge is not a single vertical `normal < chance < AT < upper` loudness ladder.

There are at least two different reward clocks:

1. `BIG` — a short, self-contained foreground reward.
2. `ART` — a persistent background contract that may last hundreds of games and must remain sustainable.

A BIG during ART is therefore not simply “one more level upward.” It temporarily occupies the foreground and then must hand auditory ownership back to the still-existing ART state.

### REUSABLE PRINCIPLE

`SHORT REWARD MAY TAKE THE FOREGROUND WITHOUT ERASING THE PERSISTENT AUDIO CONTEXT IT INTERRUPTS.`

For A+ART study, always ask two separate questions:
- What sound says **reward now**?
- What sound says **the longer state still continues after this reward**?

## 2. BIG explicitly gives the player control over its audio experience

### SOURCED FACT

After BIG entry, the player can select the bonus notification type and BGM from the LCD. Published references list three notification types:
- chance notification: a cut-in creates a red-7 aiming opportunity;
- complete notification: a door-style notification can announce ART;
- post notification: the final two games reveal the result through a screen interaction.

P-WORLD additionally documents selectable BGM at BIG start.

Sources:
- P-WORLD: https://www.p-world.co.jp/machine/database/8129
- Ichigeki BIG overview: https://1geki.jp/slot/s_madomagi2/61/
- Ichigeki BIG detail: https://1geki.jp/slot/s_madomagi2/62/
- NanaPress bonus overview: https://nana-press.com/kaiseki/machine/50/918/

### INTERPRETATION

This is more than cosmetic customization. The three modes redistribute **when uncertainty is resolved**:

- chance notification keeps attention on intermittent reel opportunities;
- complete notification converts a future reward into a sudden lever/reel-timing announcement;
- post notification defers the answer toward the end of the bonus.

The player is therefore choosing an **attention schedule**, not merely a skin.

### REUSABLE PRINCIPLE

`AUDIO CUSTOMIZATION CAN CHANGE WHEN THE PLAYER RECEIVES CERTAINTY, NOT JUST WHAT THE MACHINE SOUNDS LIKE.`

This is a useful distinction from soundtrack selection alone.

## 3. Complete-notification impact demonstrates a dangerous but effective dynamic-range extreme

### FIELD REPORT

A long-term player report describes the complete-notification golden-door event as accompanied by an extremely loud impact, strong enough to make the player physically jump and draw attention from the adjacent seat. The same report says the player disliked the startling impact but still selected the mode because the visual/reward moment was attractive.

Source:
- のり子の下手スロ, field report: https://noriko-slot.com/zissen-madomagi22567

### INTERPRETATION

This is important because “memorable” and “comfortable” are not synonyms.

A single high-dynamic-range event can be strongly memorable precisely because the surrounding bonus routine is not continuously at that peak. But a player can simultaneously value the information/reward and dislike the sensory penalty.

### REUSABLE PRINCIPLE

`MEMORABILITY MUST BE EVALUATED SEPARATELY FROM STARTLE COST.`

and

`A RARE PEAK BORROWS ITS POWER FROM THE HEADROOM LEFT BY ROUTINE AUDIO.`

Playtest should later test whether a cue feels rewarding, startling, fatiguing, or all three.

## 4. NORMAL REST must leave acoustic headroom for consequence that is unresolved after STOP3

### SOURCED FACT

The Game & Reel Run 1 established that visible rare-role identity can resolve on the reels while its consequence remains open: simultaneous BONUS, hidden-state movement, CZ/direct ART and later reward are not always known at STOP3.

Published normal-play material shows multiple subsequent presentation routes after ordinary/rare-role play, including expectation-building sequences, CZ entry and battle outcomes.

Sources:
- Case Study 03 Game & Reel Run 1 (repository)
- Ichigeki normal presentation: https://1geki.jp/slot/s_madomagi2/53/
- NanaPress CZ overview: https://nana-press.com/kaiseki/machine/50/913/

### INTERPRETATION

The real machine's screen-heavy design means audio can continue to carry a question **after the physical reel result exists**.

This creates a three-step acoustic ownership model:

`MOTOR/ROLE → CONSEQUENCE QUESTION → RESOLUTION`

The STOP sound should not need to become the strongest sound merely because it is the last physical button press. A later presentation event can legitimately carry more semantic weight if STOP3 only resolves role identity.

### REUSABLE PRINCIPLE

`STOP3 MAY CLOSE THE MOTOR QUESTION WHILE AUDIO KEEPS THE CONSEQUENCE QUESTION OPEN.`

This complements the Visual principle `STOP3 CAN CLOSE ROLE IDENTITY WHILE PRESENTATION KEEPS OUTCOME OPEN.`

## 5. CZ is a temporary duty/intensity envelope, not a new permanent baseline

### SOURCED FACT

`魔女の結界` is documented as a finite CZ consisting of a 12G exploration section followed by a battle section, with role-dependent ART expectation and multiple CZ variants. It is therefore a short evaluative state inserted inside the longer normal-play session.

Source:
- NanaPress CZ overview: https://nana-press.com/kaiseki/machine/50/913/
- P-WORLD: https://www.p-world.co.jp/machine/database/8129

### INTERPRETATION

CZ sound should be read as a **bounded attention envelope**. Its job is to temporarily increase listening/visual priority, then clearly release that priority after success or failure.

A common research mistake would be to catalogue only the entry jingle and success sound. The more important repeated-play question is whether the player can hear/feel:

`NORMAL → CZ CLAIM → CZ SUSTAIN → BATTLE RESOLUTION → ART OR RELEASE → NORMAL/OTHER STATE`

### REUSABLE PRINCIPLE

`TEMPORARY HIGH-DUTY AUDIO NEEDS A CLEAR RELEASE EDGE.`

If a failed CZ leaves the normal state acoustically “still tense,” repeated failure can create residue fatigue even without high volume.

## 6. ART navigation makes sound a motor instruction, not only atmosphere

### SOURCED FACT

Published ART instructions state that play is basically the same as normal play, but when a navigation instruction appears the player follows it. ART is game-count based and can continue for extended periods.

Sources:
- P-WORLD ART instructions: https://www.p-world.co.jp/machine/database/8129
- Ichigeki ART overview: https://1geki.jp/slot/s_madomagi2/81/

### INTERPRETATION

During ART, audio has at least two simultaneous responsibilities:

1. maintain state identity/reward continuity;
2. support time-critical physical STOP compliance when navigation appears.

Those responsibilities can conflict. If BGM and reward effects occupy all perceptual space, navigation becomes harder to parse. If navigation is too dominant every game, the 50G+α state becomes tiring.

The crucial experience distinction is therefore **continuous state sound versus intermittent action cue**.

### REUSABLE PRINCIPLE

`PERSISTENT BGM MAY OWN MOOD; INTERMITTENT CUES MUST OWN ACTION TIMING.`

and

`WHEN AUDIO CHANGES PLAYER INPUT DUTY, ITS SEMANTIC PRIORITY SHOULD EXCEED DECORATIVE LAYERS WITHOUT REQUIRING PERMANENT MAXIMUM LOUDNESS.`

## 7. ART extension zones should spend intensity locally, then return it

### SOURCED FACT

ART contains extension opportunities including `マギカ☆クエスト` and `ワルプルギスの夜`; published summaries describe them as higher-value temporary zones inside/attached to the continuing ART reward. `ワルプルギスの夜` is documented with continuation management and a minimum initial run, while `マギカ☆クエスト` ties role timing to extension outcomes.

Sources:
- P-WORLD: https://www.p-world.co.jp/machine/database/8129
- Ichigeki ART: https://1geki.jp/slot/s_madomagi2/81/
- strategy detail: https://crankyseven.com/madokamagica-two-pc.htm

### INTERPRETATION

These states justify increased acoustic urgency because individual games have temporarily increased consequence density. But the important long-session behavior is the **return to ordinary ART**.

If every special zone permanently raises the post-zone baseline, a long ART becomes an endless escalation with no perceptual recovery.

### REUSABLE PRINCIPLE

`INTENSITY IS A TEMPORARY RESOURCE; SPECIAL STATES SHOULD RETURN IT WHEN THEIR LOCAL QUESTION ENDS.`

This is structurally different from a design that treats every reward as permission to make all subsequent audio permanently larger.

---

## 8. Bonus inside ART is an addition-and-return problem

### SOURCED FACT

Because the bonus is a real bonus, its timing changes its benefit. Published references document that BONUS during ART or related states can add reward value such as extension-zone opportunity rather than discarding the ART context.

Sources:
- Ichigeki BIG detail: https://1geki.jp/slot/s_madomagi2/62/
- P-WORLD: https://www.p-world.co.jp/machine/database/8129

### INTERPRETATION

The desired experiential grammar is not:

`ART AUDIO → BIG AUDIO → UNKNOWN NEW BASELINE`

but:

`ART HOME → BIG FOREGROUND → BIG RESOLUTION → ART RE-ANCHOR`

The player should not have to re-learn where they are after every bonus interruption.

### REUSABLE PRINCIPLE

`A PARALLEL REWARD SHOULD HAVE AN AUDIBLE RETURN ADDRESS.`

The same idea can later be tested visually and in app translation: can the player recognize that the persistent state survived the foreground reward?

## 9. Sparse versus dense is the wrong binary for this machine

### INTERPRETATION

Compared with Case Study 02, this machine is substantially more screen/presentation heavy. Yet that does not imply audio should be continuously dense.

The more useful axis is **semantic concurrency**:
- routine input confirmation;
- role cue;
- hidden-consequence cue;
- state BGM;
- navigation instruction;
- reward confirmation;
- voice/presentation layer.

Fatigue grows when too many of these make simultaneous claims, even if each individual element is well-made.

### REUSABLE PRINCIPLE

`DENSE PRESENTATION CAN REMAIN READABLE IF SEMANTIC OWNERSHIP CHANGES CLEANLY OVER TIME.`

and the previously established baseline rule remains valid:

`ATTENTION HIERARCHY > LOUDNESS HIERARCHY.`

## 10. Long-form / repeated-play evidence and limitations

### SOURCED / FIELD EVIDENCE

Long-form public play material exists for the machine, including multi-segment practical-play programs and long player session reports. They confirm that a single session may cycle repeatedly through long normal stretches, isolated BIGs, failed CZs, ART, BIG inside ART and extension zones; one farewell-session field report describes an ART run followed later by a near-ceiling dry stretch and a final BIG that failed to reach ART.

Sources:
- BASHtv long practical-play video listing: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=sGUC3Mow1P8
- farewell long-session field report: https://noriko-slot.com/zissen-madomagi22577
- practical teaching feature: https://pachiseven.jp/articles/detail/4173

### INTERPRETATION

This supports evaluating audio over **session topology**, not only event clips. The important fatigue test is whether the machine creates recoverable valleys between events and whether ordinary operation remains tolerable when the desired state does not arrive.

### UNRESOLVED

This pass does **not** claim a time-coded 100G acoustic event-density measurement. Available indexed long-form sources were sufficient to establish repeated-state topology but not to defensibly count every BET/lever/STOP/BGM event from raw uninterrupted footage.

Playtest/next Sound pass should add time-coded direct observation if a sufficiently clean full-machine recording is accessible.

## 11. Speaker, low-frequency and cabinet-transmission claims remain unresolved

### UNRESOLVED

Visual Run 1 deliberately did not identify upper grille-like regions as speakers merely from appearance. This Sound run likewise found no sufficiently authoritative public source in the reviewed material for:
- exact driver count/location;
- crossover or frequency response;
- cabinet low-frequency transducer specification;
- deliberate physical vibration mechanism;
- measured sound-pressure behavior.

Do not infer these from photos or from a phone/video recording.

### REUSABLE PRINCIPLE

`DO NOT CONVERT RECORDED LOUDNESS INTO A CABINET ACOUSTIC SPECIFICATION.`

Video microphone AGC, hall noise, editing and playback device make such inference unreliable.

## 14. Handoff to Playtest & QA Director

Prioritize real repeated-play reconstruction and test these questions against beginner/expert reports and long sessions:

1. During a 100G / 500G dry stretch, does ordinary presentation remain tolerable, or is the player mainly waiting for relief?
2. After a rare role, how long does the player remain acoustically/visually vigilant before the consequence question closes?
3. On CZ failure, how quickly does attention return to NORMAL REST?
4. Does BIG alone feel like a meaningful reward when it fails to produce ART?
5. Compare the three BIG notification modes as different **certainty schedules**, not just preferences.
6. Separate the pleasure of complete-notification impact from startle/fatigue cost.
7. During long ART, distinguish sustainable state BGM from intermittent navigation and extension-zone demand.
8. Check `BIG inside ART → ART return`: does the player immediately know the persistent state survived?
9. Identify fatigue from semantic concurrency: navigation + BGM + voice + screen event + reward cue at once.
10. Do not use the smartphone app yet; Phase B starts only after all five Phase A Directors complete the real-machine baseline.
