# 1. Why this topic is the next Sound question

Visual & Mechanism Run 5 established that a stopped reel is an external memory surface: STOP1 and STOP2 leave visible evidence that helps the player reduce the live hypothesis set. Skilled play may require fewer visual checks, but the machine must not remove evidence simply because the player has learned where and when to look.

The Sound handoff therefore asks a precise question:

**When can routine audio confirm action/state cheaply enough that the player keeps eyes on reel evidence, and when does audio become a second mandatory monitoring channel that creates defensive listening?**

This run does not ask how to make sound more exciting. It asks how audio can reduce avoidable gaze switching without replacing reel reading or demanding continuous auditory vigilance.

---

# 2. Prior unknown

Previous Sound runs established:

- routine audio should become predictable;
- deviation can carry meaning only after baseline trust exists;
- STOP feedback is a causal chain rather than one isolated SE;
- platform delay/dropout must not impersonate machine-authored timing;
- habituation/tolerance is not proof of comfort or responsiveness.

The missing distinction was **offloading versus duplication**.

It was not yet clear when an auditory cue genuinely removes visual work and when it merely duplicates the same information so that the player now feels obliged to watch **and** listen.

# 3. Investigation

## 3.1 Auditory cues can reduce visual workload, but modality choice is conditional

### SOURCED FACT

Brown, Newsome & Glinert (CHI 1989) experimentally compared auditory and visual cues in a visual-search task and reported that auditory cues could replace traditionally visual cueing and reduce visual workload in the tested context.

Source:
- Brown, M. L., Newsome, S. L., Glinert, E. P., `An experiment into the use of auditory cues to reduce visual workload`, CHI 1989.
- https://doi.org/10.1145/67450.67515

A later continuous visual-tracking experiment found simple auditory alerts less disruptive to the ongoing visual task than visual alerts, with lower dual-task workload in the no-noise condition. The authors also note that even simple interruptions produced measurable disruption after the alert.

Sources:
- `Simple auditory and visual interruptions of a continuous visual tracking task: modality effects and time course of interference`, Ergonomics 64(7), 2021.
- https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33428536/

### INTERPRETATION

Audio can protect a visual precision task when it carries **simple, low-ambiguity confirmation** that otherwise would require an extra visual check.

This does not mean “move reel information into audio.” It means a player may not need to look away from stopped-reel evidence merely to confirm that an action was accepted or that an already-known state continues.

The important boundary is whether hearing the cue **reduces a required visual check** or merely adds another required check.

### REUSABLE PRINCIPLE

`GOOD AUDITORY OFFLOADING REMOVES A CHECK; BAD MULTIMODALITY ADDS A CHECK.`

`USE AUDIO TO PROTECT THE VISUAL PRECISION TASK, NOT TO RECREATE THE VISUAL PRECISION TASK IN SOUND.`

---

## 3.2 Redundant multimodal feedback is not automatically lower workload

### SOURCED FACT

Seagull, Wickens & Loeb studied visual, auditory and redundant patient-monitoring displays and explicitly framed redundant auditory displays as a possible way to offload visual workload, while testing the attention/workload consequences rather than assuming redundancy is always beneficial.

Source:
- Seagull, F. J., Wickens, C. D., Loeb, R. G., `When is Less More? Attention and Workload in Auditory, Visual, and Redundant Patient-Monitoring Conditions`, Proceedings of the Human Factors and Ergonomics Society Annual Meeting, 2001.
- https://doi.org/10.1177/154193120104501817

A multimodal-feedback experiment by Vitense, Jacko & Emery found that some bimodal combinations improved performance while lowering self-reported mental demand; the result depended on the combination rather than on “more modalities” as a universal rule.

Source:
- Vitense, H. S., Jacko, J. A., Emery, V. K., `Multimodal feedback: an assessment of performance and mental workload`, Ergonomics 46(1-3).
- https://doi.org/10.1080/00140130303534

A 2013 meta-analysis of 68 studies on interruptions and multimodal interfaces likewise found modality effects depend on signal complexity, workload and task conditions; redundant auditory-visual presentation was beneficial in some conditions, not a universal default.

Source:
- Lu, S. A. et al., `Supporting Interruption Management and Multimodal Interface Design`, Human Factors 55(4), 2013.
- https://doi.org/10.1177/0018720813476298

### INTERPRETATION

For repeated reel play, `visual + audio` should not be interpreted as “double certainty.” If both channels must be consciously inspected to know whether the machine behaved correctly, the player pays two monitoring costs.

A useful redundancy pattern is asymmetric:

- one channel carries the primary evidence;
- another channel cheaply confirms ownership/state;
- conscious cross-checking occurs mainly when the baseline deviates.

For reel-centered play, stopped reels remain the primary evidence surface. Routine audio should normally allow the player **not** to inspect a secondary visual acknowledgment—not require them to audit the audio as well.

### REUSABLE PRINCIPLE

`REDUNDANCY SHOULD CREATE OPTIONAL CONFIRMATION, NOT DUAL-MODAL OBLIGATION.`

`ONE CHANNEL MAY CONFIRM ANOTHER; BOTH SHOULD NOT REQUIRE FULL-TIME POLICING.`

---

## 3.3 Audio can orient attention toward a visual target; that makes false orientation costly

### SOURCED FACT

Naka & Yamauchi (2024) report that auditory spatial cues can facilitate visual search by drawing spatial attention toward visual targets under workload conditions.

Source:
- Naka, K., Yamauchi, K., `How close laterally should auditory cue be toward visual target to facilitate visual search under workload condition?`, Mechanical Engineering Journal 11(6), 2024.
- https://doi.org/10.1299/mej.24-00246

### INTERPRETATION

Any audio that makes the player redirect gaze has spent attention authority. If a cue repeatedly causes the player to look at the reel window, side display, lamp or instruction area without changing the needed interpretation/action, it trains defensive checking rather than mastery.

Therefore “the player looked where we wanted” is not automatically a success metric. The cue must have justified the gaze shift.

### REUSABLE PRINCIPLE

`AN AUDIO CUE THAT MOVES THE EYES HAS SPENT ATTENTION AUTHORITY.`

`GAZE REDIRECTION SHOULD PURCHASE INFORMATION, ACTION CHANGE, OR CONFIDENCE — NOT MERELY CONFIRM THAT NOTHING CHANGED.`

---

## 3.4 Repeated low-value alerts can change trust and monitoring behavior

### SOURCED FACT

Human-factors research on alarm systems consistently separates correct detection from trust/compliance/reliance behavior. Ferraro & Mouloua (2021) found that automated-alert error type changes operator monitoring and trust-related behavior.

Source:
- Ferraro, J. C., Mouloua, M., `Automated Alert Failures and their Impact on Operator Performance and Trust`, HFES Annual Meeting, 2021.
- https://doi.org/10.1177/1071181321651290

An integrative review of monitor alarm fatigue reports that excessive false alarms contribute to desensitization, mistrust and reduced response.

Source:
- `Monitor Alarm Fatigue: An Integrative Review`, Biomedical Instrumentation & Technology 46(4).
- https://doi.org/10.2345/0899-8205-46.4.268

Meyer, Wiczorek & Günzler distinguish two different responses to alerts: **compliance** (acting when an alert occurs) and **reliance** (withholding action when no alert occurs).

Source:
- `Measures of reliance and compliance in aided visual scanning`, Human Factors 56(5), 2014.
- https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25141592/

### INTERPRETATION

Pachislot routine audio is not a medical alarm system, and those findings cannot be imported as numeric thresholds. But the behavioral distinction is valuable.

If a player learns “when this audio is absent I can safely keep reading the reels,” audio may reduce unnecessary monitoring. If cue reliability is weak—or if harmless audio events repeatedly impersonate meaningful deviations—the player may instead:

- look every time;
- listen defensively every game;
- distrust silence;
- or eventually ignore a cue that was supposed to matter.

The Sound question is therefore not just `Was the cue audible?` but `What monitoring policy did repeated exposure teach?`

### REUSABLE PRINCIPLE

`AUDIO TEACHES A MONITORING POLICY.`

`LOW-VALUE FALSE ORIENTING CAN TURN OPTIONAL LISTENING INTO DEFENSIVE LISTENING.`

`RELIABLE SILENCE CAN BE AS IMPORTANT AS RELIABLE SOUND.`

`AUDIO SHOULD REPORT WHAT THE EYES SHOULD NOT HAVE TO LEAVE THE REELS TO CHECK.`

`AUDIO SHOULD NOT REPORT EVERYTHING THE REELS ARE ALREADY BETTER AT PROVING.`

# 5. New learning — auditory offloading needs a monitoring-cost test

## INTERPRETATION

The useful question for each repeated audio category is not simply whether it has information. It is:

1. What visual check does this sound remove?
2. If the player ignores the sound during ordinary play, can they still play safely/honestly?
3. If the sound deviates, does it deserve a gaze shift or action change?
4. Does repeated exposure reduce checking, or make the player audit the channel defensively?
5. Can the player return attention to reels immediately after the audio responsibility resolves?

This produces a research-only distinction:

`AUDITORY OFFLOADING`
= sound carries cheap confirmation so visual precision can remain primary.

`AUDITORY DUPLICATION`
= sound repeats visible information without reducing any obligation.

`DEFENSIVE LISTENING`
= the player continuously monitors sound because missing a subtle cue may cost information/value or because reliability is uncertain.

### REUSABLE PRINCIPLE

`THE BEST ROUTINE AUDIO OFTEN SAVES A GLANCE RATHER THAN EARNS ONE.`

`A SOUND SHOULD EITHER REMOVE WORK OR JUSTIFY NEW WORK.`

`IF IT DOES NEITHER, IT SPENDS ATTENTION WITHOUT BUYING PLAY VALUE.`

# 6. Reel-reading primacy and skill

## INTERPRETATION

Game & Reel Run 5 frames mastery as shrinking the live hypothesis set. Visual Run 5 frames stopped reels as persistent external memory. Sound should support those mechanisms without taking over their proof function.

A useful separation is:

- **audio confirms transaction/state**;
- **reels prove the visual consequence**;
- **the player performs the interpretation**.

If audio announces the conclusion before the player can use partial-stop evidence, it can collapse discovery into notification. If audio is too ambiguous, the player must monitor both sound and reels continuously.

### REUSABLE PRINCIPLES

`DO NOT LET CONFIRMATION PREEMPT DISCOVERY.`

`THE REEL SHOULD REMAIN THE PROOF SURFACE WHEN REEL READING IS THE SKILL.`

`AUDIO MAY REDUCE GAZE SWITCHING WITHOUT REDUCING PLAYER INFERENCE.`

`SKILL SUPPORT IS NOT THE SAME AS SKILL SUBSTITUTION.`

# 7. Changed understanding

Previous Sound research emphasized:

`routine audio -> meaningful deviation`

and then:

`action -> causal audio chain -> reliable attribution`.

Run 5 adds a **resource-allocation layer**:

`ROUTINE AUDIO -> LOW-COST CONFIRMATION -> FEWER AVOIDABLE VISUAL CHECKS -> REEL EVIDENCE REMAINS PRIMARY -> DEVIATION EARNS ORIENTING -> ATTENTION RELEASE`

The central change is that clarity is not measured by the number of channels carrying the same truth. Clarity can improve when one channel becomes safely ignorable because another carries the relevant confirmation more efficiently.

Research-only conceptual model:

`OFFLOADING VALUE ≈ REMOVED VISUAL CHECKS + PRESERVED REEL EVIDENCE - NEW LISTENING OBLIGATION - FALSE ORIENTING`

This is an interpretation, not a scientific or production equation.

# 8. Limits / misuse prevention

- This run does not specify any Chappy5 sound, waveform, timbre, pitch, duration, loudness, spatialization or mix.
- Human-factors alarm/monitoring studies are used for principles about attention/trust, not to claim pachislot-specific thresholds.
- Auditory-alert benefits reported in quiet/noise-controlled experiments cannot be assumed to survive pachislot-hall noise or iPhone speakers without testing.
- Do not infer that “audio is better than visual.” Modality suitability depends on task, information complexity and context.
- Do not move reach-eye, role-denial or stop-control proof entirely into audio merely to reduce visual load.
- Do not deliberately create hard-to-ignore frequent cues under the label of accessibility.
- Do not treat habituation, ignored cues or reduced gaze as proof of mastery without checking detection/prediction accuracy.
- 新ハナビ remains a reference anchor only; protected sounds/melodies/samples/signature behavior are not copied.

## 10.1 Topic reason
Visual Run 5 showed that mastery depends on persistent reel evidence. Sound therefore needed to determine whether audio can reduce unnecessary gaze switching without becoming a second precision task.

## 10.2 Prior unknown
The project had not separated useful multimodal offloading from mere audiovisual duplication or defensive listening.

## 10.3 Investigation
Reviewed completed 新ハナビ Sound evidence plus human-factors work on auditory visual-load reduction, multimodal workload, attentional cueing, alarm trust/reliance and repeated-alert fatigue.

## 10.4 New learning
Audio is most valuable to reel-centered play when it removes a low-value visual confirmation while leaving the reel as primary proof. Redundancy is useful only when it lowers monitoring cost; otherwise it can create dual-channel obligation.

## 10.5 Changed understanding
The objective is no longer “make every important action audible.” The stronger model is “make ordinary confirmation cheap enough to become safely backgrounded, and reserve conscious listening for changes that deserve a model/action update.”

## 10.7 Limits / misuse
