## 3. Investigation

### SOURCED FACT — agency and causal grouping

Kawabe, Roseboom & Nishida (2013) report that action-effect agency can be understood through general cross-modal causal grouping. Their experiments show that how action- and effect-related signals are grouped across modalities changes perceived causality / agency. Source: Proceedings of the Royal Society B, DOI `10.1098/rspb.2013.0991`, PMID `23740784`.

Wen (2019) reviews action-feedback delay and concludes that delay often reduces reported sense of agency, but that different agency measures and processing levels respond differently; delay alone is not a complete causal-attribution model. Source: Consciousness and Cognition 73:102759, DOI `10.1016/j.concog.2019.05.007`, PMID `31173998`.

Osumi et al. (2019) experimentally separate delay detection from agency attribution: the time window for agency judgment was longer than the time window for detecting delay, supporting a two-step account in which detecting a sensory discrepancy and assigning agency are not the same judgment. Source: PLOS ONE 14(7):e0219222, DOI `10.1371/journal.pone.0219222`, PMID `31287829`.

A 2021 audiomotor temporal-recalibration study found that exposure to delayed feedback shifted the decision criterion for self-agency without changing perceptual sensitivity, showing that learned timing history can bias source judgment even when sensory discrimination itself is unchanged. Source: Frontiers in Psychology 12:580441, DOI `10.3389/fpsyg.2021.580441`.

Recent multisensory causal-inference literature continues to distinguish **integration** from **segregation**: the observer estimates whether signals share a common cause, and persistent discrepancies can recalibrate later expectations. This supports treating source authority as learned and revisable rather than as a one-frame visual/audio property.

### OBSERVATION — repository evidence across the two completed real-machine cases

Case Study 01 and Case Study 02 use very different information architectures, yet both rely on stable ordinary relationships before deviations become interpretable.

For 北斗, state-related meaning is not established by one isolated flash or sound; players interpret sequences of role outcomes, presentation, persistence, transition and resolution against a learned normal baseline.

For 新ハナビ, stop position, slip, stopped form, flash/sound and subsequent bonus/RT behavior are interpreted as belonging to the same machine law because the ordinary action -> reel -> result loop is repeatedly stable. A single unexplained display/audio discontinuity outside that law is not equivalent evidence.

The common structure is therefore not “multiple channels fire together.” It is **multiple observations remain compatible with one learned causal model of the machine**.

## 4. New learning — add SOURCE AUTHORITY to the research-only contract

The ATTENTION CONTRACT should not add another effect channel. It should add a question that sits across all channels:

### `SOURCE AUTHORITY`

**What evidence lets the Human assign the observed change to the machine's current causal story rather than to input rejection, device/browser behavior, rendering/audio discontinuity, interruption/resume, or unexplained noise?**

A research record can therefore become:

1. `CLAIM OPENER` — what observable evidence justifies extra attention?
2. `RESPONSIBILITY` — what must the player now watch, predict, aim, remember or decide?
3. `SOURCE AUTHORITY` — why should this evidence be attributed to the machine rather than the platform/noise?
4. `CROSS-CHANNEL SUPPORT` — which reel / visual / mechanism / audio observations remain compatible with the same causal story?
5. `RESOLUTION EVIDENCE` — what proves the responsibility ended or changed?
6. `RELEASE / RE-ANCHOR` — how does ordinary play become safely ordinary and causally trustworthy again?

This remains an analysis template. It prescribes no event, timing, effect, feature, or original machine behavior.

## 5. Changed understanding

Previous model:

`coherent channels -> event feels machine-authored`

Revised model:

`stable learned machine law + compatible cross-channel evidence + explainable transition + trustworthy return -> event can earn machine-source authority`

Cross-channel simultaneity can help grouping, but simultaneity is not sufficient. If all channels glitch together because the browser resumes, they may be highly synchronized and still correctly feel platform-caused.

Likewise, a real machine event need not be multimodally loud. A quiet reel result can possess strong source authority when it follows a stable action/reel law and is resolved consistently.

## 6. Reusable principles

`DETECTION, MEANING AND SOURCE ATTRIBUTION ARE THREE DIFFERENT ACCEPTANCE QUESTIONS.`

`SOURCE AUTHORITY IS EARNED BY A STABLE CAUSAL LAW, NOT BY EFFECT SIZE.`

`CROSS-CHANNEL AGREEMENT SHOULD SUPPORT ONE CAUSAL STORY; IT SHOULD NOT MERELY ADD MORE SIGNALS.`

`SYNCHRONY CAN SUPPORT CAUSAL GROUPING, BUT SYNCHRONY ALONE CANNOT PROVE MACHINE ORIGIN.`

`A PLATFORM DISCONTINUITY MUST NOT BE ALLOWED TO COUNTERFEIT A MACHINE CLAIM.`

`BASELINE RELIABILITY IS PART OF EVENT SEMANTICS.`

`RE-ANCHORING MUST RESTORE CAUSAL TRUST, NOT ONLY VISUAL OR AUDIO NORMALITY.`

`CONFIDENT MISATTRIBUTION IS A MACHINE-LEVEL FAILURE EVEN WHEN EVERY LOCAL SUBSYSTEM IS FUNCTIONING.`

`A GOOD REALITY STUDY RECORDS WHY AN EVENT BELONGS TO THE MACHINE, NOT ONLY WHAT THE EVENT LOOKS OR SOUNDS LIKE.`

## 7. Limits / misuse

- Sense-of-agency research is not pachislot-specific and cannot provide production timing thresholds.
- Do not infer that every machine event requires redundant visual + audio + reel confirmation.
- Do not convert `SOURCE AUTHORITY` into a requirement for louder or more explicit feedback.
- Do not treat subjective confidence as proof of correct attribution.
- Do not use cross-modal synchrony as a universal pass condition; common platform glitches can also be synchronous.
- Do not invent hidden stop-control, state, lottery or presentation rules for any real machine.
- Do not rank or refine SIGNAL/FORGE/VAULT.
- This study does not authorize a prototype, browser test, timing experiment, asset, or original-machine specification.

## 8. Remaining unknowns

- How should source authority be tested when two legitimate machine responsibilities overlap?
- Can a player correctly attribute a quiet reel anomaly while a louder but irrelevant platform event occurs nearby?
- How much ordinary-loop reliability is needed before omission/delay can safely carry machine meaning?
- How should re-entry after Safari interruption distinguish “machine continuity restored” from “new machine event” without teaching the Human what to expect?
- Does expertise improve source attribution, or can experts become overconfident and reinterpret platform artifacts as meaningful machine anomalies?
- Which cross-channel contradictions are useful information and which destroy the common-cause model?
