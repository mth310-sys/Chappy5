## Research question

**How can repeated machine sounds remain instantly source-identifiable while still belonging to one coherent machine body and staying comfortable across hundreds of repetitions?**

This directly continues Run 1. Run 1 established that physical body presence is not equivalent to bass or loudness. Run 2 asks how multiple repeated sources can be differentiated without turning the machine into unrelated samples, and how repeated exposure changes attention, segregation and fatigue.

---

# 1. Why this theme was selected

Run 1 left an important conflict unresolved:

- BET / lever / reel motion / STOP1-3 / settlement / payout / information / reward must be distinguishable quickly;
- mechanically related events should still sound like one physical machine;
- repeated sounds must not consume excessive attention or become irritating across 100G, 500G and multi-hour play;
- strong informational cues must remain legible when several layers overlap.

A naive solution is to make every event maximally different in pitch, spectrum, envelope and loudness. Auditory-stream research warns that sufficiently large differences promote perceptual segregation. That may improve local identification while destroying the impression that the events belong to one machine. The design problem is therefore not maximum separation; it is **controlled differentiation inside a stable source family**.

---

# 2. What was not understood before this study

Before Run 2, Sound & Experience did not have a sufficiently evidence-based answer to:

1. Which sound differences make sequential events perceptually split into separate auditory streams?
2. How can timbre/envelope differences aid identification without making sources feel unrelated?
3. Can predictability help listeners organize repeated machine sounds?
4. What happens to attention when salient repeated cues recur many times?
5. Does habituation mean a repeated sound has become comfortable, or merely that the listener has learned to ignore it?
6. How should urgency/importance relate to annoyance?
7. What principles survive without importing recognizable pachislot samples or signature melodies?

---

# 3. What was investigated

## SOURCED FACT — large acoustic differences can split one sequence into multiple perceived streams

Moore & Gockel's review of auditory stream formation reports that small differences between sequential sounds favor fusion into one stream, while sufficiently large differences favor segregation. Frequency separation is important, but timbre, spectral shape, temporal envelope, modulation and other cues also affect streaming. Timbre differences can promote segregation even when center frequency is held constant.

Source:
- Moore, B. C. J. & Gockel, H. E. (2012), *Properties of auditory stream formation*, Philosophical Transactions of the Royal Society B.
  https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3282308/

## SOURCED FACT — attack/envelope and spectral structure participate in source segregation

The same review summarizes experiments in which differences in temporal envelope and spectral shape changed stream segregation. Fast/slow onset relationships and timbral differences affected whether alternating sounds were heard as one or multiple streams.

A 2023 JASA study further showed that abrupt changes in timbral properties, including attack/decay envelope and modulation, can reset or increase segregation depending on direction and context.

Sources:
- https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3282308/
- Roberts & Haywood (2023), *Asymmetric effects of sudden changes in timbre on auditory stream segregation*.
  https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37462404/

## SOURCED FACT — temporal coherence contributes to auditory grouping

Reviews of auditory scene analysis describe temporal coherence as one mechanism contributing to whether acoustic components are grouped as one source or separated. Spectral/location cues matter, but timing relationships also participate in source formation.

Sources:
- *Neural correlates of auditory scene analysis and perception*.
  https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4176604/
- *Behind the Scenes of Auditory Perception*.
  https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2901988/

## SOURCED FACT — predictability can support auditory scene organization

Bendixen's review describes growing evidence that listeners exploit predictable regularities in sound sequences when organizing auditory scenes. Predictability is not the only source-segregation mechanism, but repeated regular structure can influence how ongoing sound is parsed.

Source:
- Bendixen (2014), *Predictability effects in auditory scene analysis: a review*.
  https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/neuroscience/articles/10.3389/fnins.2014.00060/full

## SOURCED FACT — natural/source-related auditory mappings can improve referent identification

Auditory-interface research distinguishes abstract beeps from sounds whose acoustic structure has a meaningful relation to their source/referent. In a driving-interface experiment, different classes of auditory display differed in mapping accuracy and response time. Earlier auditory-icon work argued that sounds carrying information about the nature of their producing event can communicate system events through everyday source-like relationships.

Sources:
- McKeown & Isherwood (2007), *Mapping Candidate Within-Vehicle Auditory Displays to Their Referents*.
  https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1518/001872007X200067
- Gaver, *Auditory Icons: Using Sound in Computer Interfaces*.
  https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1207/s15327051hci0202_3

These studies are not pachislot-specific and do not license literal real-world recordings. The reusable point is that **source/event relationship can carry identity**, not that Chappy5 should copy physical-machine samples.

## SOURCED FACT — warning salience and annoyance are related to parameters and context

Vehicle-alert studies found that perceived urgency and annoyance vary with alert parameters such as density/pulse characteristics and with context. Increasing attention demand is therefore not free; a sound can gain urgency while also gaining annoyance, and appropriateness depends on the event context.

Sources:
- Marshall, Lee & Austria (2001), *Annoyance and Urgency of Auditory Alerts for in-Vehicle Information Systems*.
  https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/154193120104502314
- Marshall, Lee & Austria (2007), *Alerts for In-Vehicle Information Systems: Annoyance, Urgency, and Appropriateness*.
  https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1518/001872007779598145

## SOURCED FACT — repeated salient auditory structure can habituate, but not all structures habituate equally

A study of structural features in radio listening reported that repeated jingles and production effects showed habituation under some conditions, while voice changes continued to elicit orienting responses across repetitions. Separate work with irrelevant ringtones found initial disruption to short-term memory and gradual recovery with repeated exposure.

Sources:
- Potter et al., *The Impact of Cognitive Load on the Cardiac Orienting Response to Auditory Structural Features during Natural Radio Listening Situations*.
  https://doi.org/10.5334/joc.43
- Röer, Bell & Buchner (2014), *Please silence your cell phone: Your ringtone captures other people's attention*.
  https://doaj.org/article/05768450ad9c4689b2503fdb63fd6450

Habituation is therefore not equivalent to “the sound became pleasant.” It may reflect reduced orienting to a predictable repeated event.

## SOURCED FACT — competing spectral content can reduce detectability

Masking research shows that detection of one auditory signal can deteriorate when competing sound occupies overlapping spectral regions. The exact result depends on stimulus, level, temporal structure and listener, but there is no basis for assuming that several individually clear sounds remain equally clear when layered.

Relevant sources:
- Shimokura & Soeta (2026), *Reaction Time to Amplitude-Modulated Tones Under Spectral Masking*.
  https://doi.org/10.3390/app16083814
- General auditory scene/segregation review:
  https://pubs.asha.org/doi/10.1044/2017_JSLHR-H-17-0041

# 4. New learning

## INTERPRETATION — source identity needs a family resemblance plus event-specific contrast

The evidence changes the problem from “make each event unique” to a two-level identity model:

`MACHINE FAMILY IDENTITY` — stable relationships that imply one body / one acoustic environment / one causal system.

`EVENT IDENTITY` — limited differences that let the player distinguish BET, reel motion, STOP, settlement, information and reward.

If every event is maximally distant in spectrum/envelope/pitch/space, auditory streaming may encourage the player to hear several unrelated sources. If every event is too similar, identification and masking fail.

The target is therefore **minimum sufficient contrast inside a coherent family**, not maximum contrast.

## INTERPRETATION — temporal ownership is part of source identity

Run 1 treated timing mainly as causality. Run 2 adds that stable temporal relationships can also help auditory grouping. A STOP-related transient that repeatedly occurs at the same causal phase can become identifiable partly because of *when* it belongs, not only because of timbre.

This means event identity need not be carried entirely by loudness or radically different sound color.

## INTERPRETATION — predictability should reduce monitoring cost for routine sounds

Routine action sounds can become part of a predictable baseline. Their job is not to reorient the player's attention every game. A rare meaningful deviation can then violate the learned baseline and regain attention authority.

This aligns Sound with Machine Run 2's attention architecture:

`routine sound = confirms predicted machine state`

`meaningful deviation = updates belief/action/value`

A baseline sound that repeatedly demands orienting wastes attention authority even if it remains memorable.

## INTERPRETATION — habituation is not a quality PASS

A listener may stop orienting toward an annoying repetitive sound simply because it has become predictable. Therefore “people no longer notice it after 100G” does not prove comfort or good design.

Future QA must distinguish at least:
- source-identification accuracy;
- task relevance/meaning recognition;
- subjective annoyance/fatigue;
- unwanted attentional capture;
- detectability of rare deviations;
- whether routine cues can be safely ignored without losing required information.

## INTERPRETATION — repeated sounds need an attention budget, not merely a loudness budget

Traditional mix thinking can focus on clipping/headroom/frequency masking. The research adds a cognitive dimension: every salient onset can also spend **orienting authority**.

A sound may be technically audible yet still be over-authored because it repeatedly demands attention that the game does not require.

## INTERPRETATION — semantic importance should determine separation strength

Not every layer needs equal segregation. Mechanically related routine events may benefit from family cohesion, while critical informational transitions may require stronger differentiation.

This suggests a future research model:

`similar source family + controlled contrast + event importance + repetition rate`

rather than assigning every event its own unrelated timbre.

No concrete sound design or mix numbers are authorized by this interpretation.

# 5. What changed from the previous understanding

Previous Run 1 model emphasized:

`physical plausibility + body context + output-device survival`.

Run 2 adds:

`SOURCE LEGIBILITY = FAMILY COHERENCE × SUFFICIENT EVENT CONTRAST × TEMPORAL OWNERSHIP × MASKING SURVIVAL × REPETITION/ATTENTION FITNESS`

Key changes:

- **Difference is not automatically clarity.** Too much difference can fragment one machine into separate streams.
- **Sameness is not automatically coherence.** Too little difference can make event identity and masking unacceptable.
- **Habituation is not comfort.** Reduced orienting after repetition can coexist with annoyance or loss of useful information.
- **Routine sound should usually confirm prediction, not repeatedly interrupt it.**
- **Event identity can be distributed across timbre, envelope, timing, context and source relationship rather than concentrated in loudness.**

# 6. Reusable principles for future Chappy5 work

## REUSABLE PRINCIPLE 1

**`DIFFERENTIATE EVENTS WITHOUT DIVORCING THEM FROM THE MACHINE FAMILY.`**

Mechanical/event sounds should have enough contrast for recognition while retaining shared acoustic relationships that support one-machine identity.

## REUSABLE PRINCIPLE 2

**`MAXIMUM TIMBRAL DISTANCE IS NOT MAXIMUM CLARITY.`**

Very large differences can promote perceptual segregation into unrelated streams. Use the least differentiation that reliably communicates the required distinction.

## REUSABLE PRINCIPLE 3

**`ROUTINE AUDIO SHOULD CONFIRM PREDICTION MORE OFTEN THAN IT DEMANDS ORIENTING.`**

Frequently repeated baseline sounds should support action/state ownership without repeatedly behaving like alerts.

## REUSABLE PRINCIPLE 4

**`HABITUATION IS NOT A COMFORT PASS.`**

Reduced attention after repetition may indicate learning or suppression. Evaluate annoyance, fatigue, identification and rare-signal detection separately.

## REUSABLE PRINCIPLE 5

**`ATTENTION AUTHORITY IS AN AUDIO RESOURCE.`**

A highly salient onset spends player attention even when it does not consume much mix headroom. Reserve repeated high-salience events for information that merits reorientation.

## REUSABLE PRINCIPLE 6

**`EVENT IDENTITY CAN LIVE IN WHEN AND WHY, NOT ONLY IN WHAT TIMBRE.`**

Stable causal phase/context can help distinguish event roles and reduce the need for extreme spectral/loudness separation.

## REUSABLE PRINCIPLE 7

**`TEST SOUNDS IN COMPETITION, NOT ONLY IN SOLO.`**

An individually readable sound can fail when reels, body, information and BGM overlap. Future acceptance must include simultaneous and near-simultaneous event conditions.

## REUSABLE PRINCIPLE 8

**`RARE DEVIATIONS MUST REMAIN LEGIBLE AFTER THE BASELINE HAS HABITUATED.`**

The purpose of a predictable baseline is partly to make meaningful deviations easier to notice, not to desensitize the player to the entire audio system.

# 7. Conditions where these principles do not apply / misuse risks

1. Auditory-stream experiments often use simplified tones/sequences, not pachislot cabinets. Do not convert their thresholds into fixed Chappy5 frequency distances.
2. A reward/celebration event may intentionally break routine family coherence. The principle is controlled exception, not mandatory uniformity.
3. Source-family cohesion must not be used to make critical warnings or state changes ambiguous.
4. Habituation is context-dependent; 100G or 3-hour conclusions require actual Human play later, not laboratory extrapolation.
5. Do not create artificial “auditory icons” by copying recognizable real-machine sounds. Reuse only the source/event mapping principle.
6. Do not solve masking by making everything louder. Spectral, temporal and priority structure must be considered.
7. Do not assume headphones and iPhone speaker create the same stream segregation or source impression.
8. Do not make every rare cue startling merely to guarantee detection; urgency and annoyance can rise together.
9. Do not treat silence as automatically comfortable. Missing expected feedback can damage action ownership if the action normally requires confirmation.

- How routine-event detectability changes after 100G, 500G and 3h on target iPhone.

- How many simultaneous/near-simultaneous layers can remain semantically legible before masking/informational overload occurs.

- Whether rare deviations remain detectable after long adaptation to a stable baseline.
- How to measure annoyance separately from excitement, urgency and memorability in a pachislot-specific Human trial.

# 9. Next worthwhile study

Highest-value next Sound & Experience question:

**How should silence, omission, attenuation and timing deviation be interpreted inside a learned machine sound baseline—when do they create meaningful prediction error, and when do they merely feel like missing/broken feedback?**

Reason: Run 2 establishes that routine sound builds a predictable source family. The next deeper unknown is the informational value of breaking that family by *not* sounding, delaying, suppressing or attenuating an expected event. This directly connects sparse presentation, anticipation, surprise, reel-reading and long-session attention without requiring louder effects.
