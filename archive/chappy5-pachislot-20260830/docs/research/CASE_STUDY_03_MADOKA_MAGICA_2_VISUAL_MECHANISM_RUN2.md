## 1. Run 2 question: where does the player's eye physically live?

### SOURCED FACT

K-Navi describes the machine as using the BAZOOKA II cabinet and a full-face touch LCD. P-WORLD documents a real BIG of about 150 coins and ART `マギカ☆ラッシュ` of 50G+α, meaning the player repeatedly alternates between reel classification, screen-state interpretation and persistent-state reading.

### DIRECT REAL-MACHINE PHOTO OBSERVATION

Front cabinet photos consistently show three horizontal visual zones that matter at seated distance:

1. a large landscape LCD occupying the largest active surface;
2. a recessed three-reel window immediately below it;
3. a three-button STOP row immediately below the reel window.

The LCD and reel window are not co-planar in appearance. The reel opening has a surrounding bezel and visible occlusion at top and bottom. The STOP row is physically closer to the player and follows the left/middle/right mapping of the reel cylinders.

### INTERPRETATION

The effective gaze path is short and mostly vertical: `LCD meaning -> reel evidence -> STOP action -> LCD consequence`. The machine does not ask the eyes to traverse distant side devices for the ordinary loop. The large LCD is dominant in area, but the reel zone is physically protected as a narrow work surface.

This suggests that the important ergonomic quantity is not raw screen-to-reel distance in millimeters but **gaze travel plus re-acquisition cost**: after reading the LCD, how quickly can the player reacquire the exact reel landmark needed for the next stop?

### REUSABLE PRINCIPLE

`A SCREEN-FIRST CABINET CAN STILL BE REEL-OPERABLE WHEN GAZE TRAVEL IS SHORT, VERTICAL AND REPEATABLE.`

`VISUAL HIERARCHY SHOULD BE MEASURED BY RE-ACQUISITION COST, NOT AREA ALONE.`

## 2. Reel-window readability: depth helps separate “information screen” from “physical result”

### SOURCED FACT

Game & Reel Run 2 established the real-machine routine as a branch structure: left BAR landmark first, then precision rises only on cherry/watermelon branches; ART may additionally impose stop-order responsibility. The physical reel result therefore remains a functional input/result surface.

### DIRECT REAL-MACHINE PHOTO OBSERVATION

The three reel strips appear behind a distinct front opening. Top/bottom masking hides the circumference, and the visible symbols occupy a relatively large share of each reel aperture despite the reel zone being much smaller than the LCD.

### INTERPRETATION

The apparent recess creates a material category boundary: LCD = emissive narrative/state plane; reels = mechanical result plane. Exact millimeter depth was not found and remains unresolved.

The practical value of depth is not decorative realism by itself. The recess and bezel reduce visual blending between animated LCD content and the symbols the player must classify. This is especially useful because Run 2 established that attention precision is intermittent rather than constant.

### REUSABLE PRINCIPLE

`PHYSICAL DEPTH CAN FUNCTION AS INFORMATION SEPARATION.`

`WHEN A SMALL WORK SURFACE SITS UNDER A LARGE DISPLAY, BORDER + RECESS + OCCLUSION CAN PROTECT TASK READABILITY.`

### UNRESOLVED

- authoritative reel-window depth;
- exact reel-diameter/strip geometry for this cabinet;
- measured seated viewing angle and symbol angular size.

## 3. Operation deck: hand travel is compressed around the reel axis

### DIRECT REAL-MACHINE PHOTO OBSERVATION

The lever is placed at lower left. A MAX BET control sits in the same left-side operating region. Three STOP buttons are centered directly beneath the reel window. A large PUSH control is placed to the right of the reels. Small status/utility controls occupy the left of the reel region, while medal/input hardware is on the right.

### INTERPRETATION

Primary repetitive actions stay clustered around the center-left lower front of the cabinet. The rare/secondary PUSH action is deliberately displaced rightward and physically larger/different, so it does not masquerade as a fourth reel stop.

The control map therefore distinguishes two interaction classes:

- `high-frequency motor loop`: BET / lever / STOP1-3;
- `contextual confirmation/interaction`: large PUSH / touch LCD.

The full-face touch LCD adds a third interaction plane without replacing the physical loop.

### REUSABLE PRINCIPLE

`FREQUENT INPUTS SHOULD FORM A TIGHT MOTOR CLUSTER; EXCEPTIONAL INPUTS SHOULD BE SPATIALLY AND TACTILELY DISTINCT.`

`A TOUCH DISPLAY CAN ADD CONTEXTUAL INTERACTION WITHOUT DISSOLVING THE PRIMARY REEL BODY MAP.`

### UNRESOLVED

- exact button pitch, lever throw and operating force;
- documented anti-fatigue geometry for this specific cabinet.

## 4. Cabinet silhouette: static architecture does most of the hall-distance work

### DIRECT REAL-MACHINE PHOTO OBSERVATION

Real-machine photos show a pale body with strong pink-toned molding, luminous side channels, dark upper grille-like fields, a large central top translucent/jewel-like element, a large landscape LCD, a compact reel window and a wide illuminated lower panel.

The cabinet remains recognizable in photographs where no large mechanism is moving.

### INTERPRETATION

The machine's hall-distance identity is carried by a **static landmark stack**:

`bright top center landmark + dark flanking masses + luminous side rails + large horizontal screen + bright lower panel`.

This allows dynamic presentation to be spent on meaning rather than on proving every second that the cabinet is alive. It also creates a useful two-scale composition: from several meters away the top/side/lower silhouette identifies the unit; once seated, the LCD/reel/STOP triangle carries play information.

### REUSABLE PRINCIPLE

`DISTANCE IDENTITY AND SEATED TASK HIERARCHY ARE DIFFERENT SCALES.`

`A CABINET DOES NOT NEED CONSTANT LARGE MECHANICAL MOTION IF STATIC LANDMARKS ALREADY OWN THE SILHOUETTE.`

## 5. Materials and translucency: only visible behavior is claimed

### DIRECT REAL-MACHINE PHOTO OBSERVATION

Several perimeter and upper-center parts transmit colored light rather than reading as opaque painted panels. The dark upper areas visually resemble perforated/grille fields. Metallic-looking trim is visible around some control and reel areas.

### INTERPRETATION

- upper-center and perimeter pieces appear to be translucent or light-guiding molded parts;
- some trim appears metallic/chrome-like;
- dark upper fields may house acoustic openings or ventilation, but their exact function cannot be proven from front imagery alone.

These are appearance-level interpretations, not material specifications.

### REUSABLE PRINCIPLE

`MATERIAL CLAIMS MUST FOLLOW OBSERVED OPTICAL BEHAVIOR, NOT STYLE LABELS.`

`TRANSLUCENT STRUCTURE IS USEFUL BECAUSE THE SAME PHYSICAL FORM CAN READ QUIETLY AT REST AND BECOME LEGIBLE WHEN LIT.`

## 6. REST VISUAL: the machine is not “off” during ordinary play

### DIRECT REAL-MACHINE PHOTO OBSERVATION

At idle/ordinary states the cabinet still presents fixed top geometry, side-lightable forms, lit controls, a visible reel cavity and a large active LCD. The lower panel remains a broad illuminated identity surface.

### INTERPRETATION

The normal REST state is a **stable visual floor**, not a void. Its job is to maintain orientation while leaving contrast budget for rare-role aftermath, CZ, BONUS and ART.

Because the physical landmarks remain fixed, the screen can change scene or expectation without forcing the player to search for the reels or controls again.

### REUSABLE PRINCIPLE

`REST VISUAL SHOULD PRESERVE ORIENTATION WHILE RESERVING MOTION/BRIGHTNESS HEADROOM FOR MEANINGFUL DEVIATION.`

`IF THE PLAYER MUST RE-LOCATE THE REELS AFTER EVERY SCREEN CHANGE, THE CABINET HIERARCHY HAS FAILED.`

## 7. Full-touch LCD: information plane can temporarily become an input plane

### SOURCED FACT

K-Navi explicitly notes a full-face touch LCD. NANA PRESS and 1geki document touch interaction at BONUS end, including a screen/voice response used for setting inference; sources also note that PUSH can be used for the same BONUS-end check in some descriptions.

### INTERPRETATION

This means the large LCD has at least two roles across the session:

- `presentation/state surface` during the ordinary loop;
- `direct interaction surface` at selected moments.

The key ergonomic point is that touch does not permanently replace the three STOP controls. The screen becomes actionable only when the context warrants it.

### REUSABLE PRINCIPLE

`A LARGE DISPLAY CAN TEMPORARILY BECOME AN INPUT SURFACE WITHOUT BECOMING THE PRIMARY MOTOR LOOP.`

`CONTEXTUAL TOUCH IS STRONGER WHEN PHYSICAL REEL INPUT REMAINS STABLE AND UNAMBIGUOUS.`

## 8. BONUS / ART visual re-anchor: returning to the same place is part of continuity

### SOURCED FACT

P-WORLD documents BIG and the persistent 50G+α ART state. Game & Reel Run 2 further established that BONUS can occur during ART and return value to the ongoing ART contract, while ART-end preparation remains a playable boundary.

### INTERPRETATION

For Visual & Mechanism, the continuity requirement is not merely “the correct screen appears after BONUS.” A successful return must restore three things quickly:

1. where the reels are;
2. which physical controls own the next action;
3. which screen region now communicates persistent ART state.

The cabinet is well suited to this because the reel opening and STOP row never physically disappear. Temporary foreground content can occupy the LCD while the player's motor map stays visible underneath.

### REUSABLE PRINCIPLE

`PHYSICAL ANCHORS CAN MAKE TEMPORARY VISUAL TAKEOVERS CHEAPER TO EXIT.`

`VISUAL RE-ANCHOR = RESTORED STATE LOCATION + RESTORED ACTION LOCATION + RESTORED ATTENTION PRIORITY.`

This complements Game & Reel Run 2:
`A TEMPORARY REAL BONUS INSIDE A PERSISTENT STATE MUST HAVE A CLEAR RETURN ADDRESS.`

## 9. What remains deliberately quiet so reels can still be read

### DIRECT REAL-MACHINE PHOTO OBSERVATION

The reel window itself is not visually overloaded with a second independent display layer in front of the symbols. The main animated content is concentrated above the reel opening. The primary STOP row is visually simple and directly aligned below the reels.

### INTERPRETATION

The cabinet spends ornamental complexity around the perimeter, top and lower panel rather than inside the narrow reel work zone. This is important: the machine can be visually rich overall while keeping the precise task surface comparatively disciplined.

### REUSABLE PRINCIPLE

`VISUAL RICHNESS SHOULD SURROUND A PRECISION TASK BEFORE IT INVADES IT.`

`WHEN REEL READING MATTERS INTERMITTENTLY, THE REEL ZONE NEEDS A LOW-NOISE BASELINE EVEN IF THE CABINET IS OTHERWISE EXPRESSIVE.`

## 10. Run 2 synthesis

Run 1 established `SCREEN-FIRST DOES NOT HAVE TO MEAN REEL-IRRELEVANT.` Run 2 sharpens that into an ergonomic/mechanical model:

`STATIC HALL LANDMARKS`
→ `LARGE LCD FOR STATE/MEANING`
→ `RECESSED REEL WORK SURFACE FOR PHYSICAL EVIDENCE`
→ `DIRECTLY MAPPED STOP ROW FOR ACTION`
→ `TEMPORARY TOUCH/PUSH LAYER WHEN CONTEXT REQUIRES`
→ `RETURN TO THE SAME PHYSICAL ANCHORS`

The central reusable finding is:

`A COMPLEX SCREEN-FIRST MACHINE CAN STAY LEARNABLE IF ITS PHYSICAL ANCHORS DO NOT NEGOTIATE WITH EVERY STATE CHANGE.`

A second finding is:

`DEPTH, BORDER AND CONTROL ALIGNMENT ARE NOT COSMETIC; THEY REDUCE GAZE AND RE-ANCHOR COST.`

These are abstract structural principles only. They do not authorize copying this cabinet's protected visual design.
