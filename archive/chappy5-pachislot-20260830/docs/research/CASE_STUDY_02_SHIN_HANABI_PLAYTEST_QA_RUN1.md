## Playtest & QA Director — Deep Dive Run 1

> Stage: `Stage 1 — Reality Baseline Study`
> Human Gate: `HUMAN_GATE_1_NOT_READY`
> Production: `DENIED_UNTIL_HUMAN_MACHINE_APPROVED`
> Scope: repeated real-machine play reconstruction, not original-concept desk testing

## 0. QA stance

This run evaluates whether the reel-centered product survives repeated play, dry spells and uneven player skill. It does not treat a memorable reach eye, a lucky bonus, or a successful high-skill REG as proof that the whole session is good.

Evidence is separated into:
- `SOURCED FACT` — official/manual/major analysis publication;
- `VIDEO / FIELD OBSERVATION` — player behavior visible or explicitly described in real-machine play material;
- `INTERPRETATION` — Playtest & QA reconstruction from the evidence;
- `UNRESOLVED` — not established strongly enough to promote.

No undisclosed reel-control table is inferred here.

## 2. First five minutes — beginner reconstruction

### What the beginner is actually doing
A realistic beginner is not reading 3,500 reach eyes. The first usable loop is much narrower:

`aim a visible landmark → STOP → check whether ice/cherry requires attention → finish the other reels → notice whether light/sound differs → repeat`

The basic procedure can be learned before the deep language. That is important: entry does not require mastery.

### Friction observed/reconstructed
However, the machine asks for visual timing earlier than an AT machine that permits free stopping for long stretches. Even if the beginner route is safe, three simultaneous learning burdens appear quickly:
1. where to aim;
2. when a role requires follow-up;
3. which visual/audio deviation actually matters.

`遅れ` is especially instructive. Published rules make it meaningful (cherry or bonus), but a new player who has not yet internalized ordinary start timing may not consciously perceive it. Therefore:

**QA finding:** `A CUE CAN BE FAIR BY RULE AND STILL BE INVISIBLE TO A NOVICE.`

The correct beginner test is not “was delay explained?” but “after five minutes of ordinary spins, can the player notice one without being told that this exact game was delayed?”

### Reusable principle
`ENTRY ROUTINE BEFORE EXPERT LANGUAGE`

A deep reel machine should expose one low-risk repeatable aiming routine first. Advanced stop-order play can then become optional expansion rather than mandatory literacy.

## 3. Normal 100G — intrinsic reel-play test

Over ~100 ordinary games, the key QA question is whether attention repeatedly returns to the physical reels voluntarily.

### INTERPRETATION — three layers of attention
A beginner tends to prioritize:
- aiming landmark;
- obvious role follow-up;
- obvious flash/blackout/mini-LCD change.

An intermediate player adds:
- slip distance/position relation;
- role denial after two reels;
- whether to change stop order on a cue;
- early bonus suspicion.

An expert can add:
- deliberately selected aiming lens;
- two-reel certainty/contradiction;
- nuanced role/bonus possibilities;
- setting samples and RT behavior.

The important QA result is that **the machine does not merely become easier with mastery; it can become more information-dense.** The same three STOPs can carry more questions later than they did in the first five minutes.

### Reusable principle
`MASTERY SHOULD ADD QUESTIONS, NOT ONLY REMOVE DIFFICULTY`

If mastery only automates the beginner routine, long-term play becomes rote. If mastery reveals new classifications and optional routes, repetition can deepen.

## 4. 500G bonus drought — do not confuse relief with quality

A 500G drought is the critical contrast with 北斗. There is no CZ milestone or upper-state ladder to promise that the next hundred games are structurally different.

### What still exists during a drought
The player still encounters:
- frequent ordinary roles;
- role-follow decisions;
- occasional delay/blackout/flash/mini-LCD deviations;
- stop-position/slip observations;
- alternative stop-order opportunities when chosen;
- setting-sample accumulation for players who care about it.

So:

`NO BONUS ≠ NO INPUT DECISION ≠ NO OBSERVATION`

But this must not be overclaimed. A player can still be bored, annoyed by repeated aiming, or economically frustrated.

### QA separation
When a bonus finally arrives after a long drought, measure three reactions separately:
1. `RELIEF` — finally the drought ended;
2. `DISCOVERY SATISFACTION` — I recognized the result myself;
3. `BASE-GAME SATISFACTION` — the preceding 100G were themselves worth playing.

A strong celebration can inflate (1) and make weak normal play appear better than it is. Case Study 02 must not make that mistake.

### Reusable test
`DRY-SPELL WITHOUT PAYOFF TEST`

Review a 100G slice from inside a 500G bonus drought with the eventual bonus removed from the evaluation. Ask whether enough voluntary watching/aiming/predicting remains to justify the slice by itself.

## 5. Missed vs recognized reach eye

### INTERPRETATION
A recognized reach eye can produce reward before explicit notification because the player resolves the machine's question personally.

A missed reach eye creates a very different experience: the machine may already be in a valuable state while the player does not know it. For a beginner this can be mystery; for an expert it is avoidable information loss.

This produces an unusual QA axis:

`SYSTEM STATE` versus `PLAYER-KNOWN STATE`

For reel-centered machines, testing only internal state transitions is insufficient. QA must also test whether intended player classes can perceive those transitions.

### Reusable principle
`PLAYER-KNOWN STATE IS A REAL QA VARIABLE`

A correct machine can still provide a poor experience if crucial readable truth is available only to a tiny expertise tier without a viable beginner path.

---

## 6. Bonus detection — reward starts before payout

Published play guides and reach-eye material support multiple ways for a player to suspect/recognize bonus before formal confirmation. Therefore entry impact should be decomposed:

`SUSPICION → SELF-CONFIRMATION → SYMBOL ALIGNMENT / EXPLICIT CONFIRMATION → PAYOUT PHASE`

For experts, `SELF-CONFIRMATION` may be the emotional peak. For beginners, explicit confirmation may remain primary.

### QA implication
Do not judge bonus entry only by lamp/flash/BGM strength. Measure whether the machine gives skilled players room to arrive at the truth themselves without making beginners unable to understand that they won.

## 7. REG — skill reward and skill tax coexist

REG is the clearest QA risk in the case study.

### SOURCED FACT
Three difficulty routes exist, and published payout references differ materially among them. The advanced route asks for repeated 2-frame ice aiming across all reels.

### INTERPRETATION
This is genuine agency because execution affects outcome quality. It is also genuine pressure because the player can attribute lost value to their own mistake.

The novice/intermediate routes are important because they prevent the machine from requiring the hardest technique merely to complete the bonus. But the economic gap means “optional” does not mean emotionally neutral.

### QA framework
Test technical intervention on two independent axes:
- `ACCESS`: can a weaker player complete the state without confusion?
- `REGRET`: after choosing a lower-difficulty route or missing an advanced attempt, how strongly does the product communicate lost value?

### Reusable principle
`SKILL REWARD SHOULD SCALE PRIDE FASTER THAN REGRET`

The product may acknowledge mastery strongly, but repeated failure feedback should not turn a reward state into a public self-critique session.

## 8. BIG → 花火チャレンジ → 花火GAME — same hardware, changing responsibility

### SOURCED FACT
`花火チャレンジ` is up to 20G and uses JAC-IN avoidance to extend the phase. `花火GAME` then runs 20G after JAC-IN. BGM stopping is published as bonus information in both phases.

### QA reconstruction
The reward state changes what the player monitors without replacing the core body grammar:
- normal: aim / classify / discover;
- challenge: also protect/extend RT at the correct instruction window;
- game: continue role/bonus observation inside sustained RT;
- BGM stop: learned state sensor;
- end: return to normal reels.

This is important because the bonus aftermath remains participatory without inventing a new control surface.

### Reusable principle
`REWARD SHOULD CHANGE RESPONSIBILITY BEFORE IT CHANGES CONTROLS`

---

## 9. Return to normal — loss of privilege or return to product?

A decisive test for this lineage is the first 20–50 games after RT ends.

If normal play is truly the product, RT ending should feel like:
`reward phase ended → core investigation resumed`.

If the player experiences:
`real game ended → now waiting for access again`,
then the reel-centered thesis has failed.

### QA test
`POST-REWARD BASELINE RECOVERY TEST`

Observe the first 30 normal games after RT. Count/describe:
- voluntary reel focus;
- changed aiming route;
- response to first small deviation;
- whether the player immediately disengages and only waits for the next bonus.

## 10. Three-hour fatigue model

No source opened in this run provides controlled eye/hand fatigue measurements, so physical fatigue remains `UNRESOLVED`. Online edited video cannot prove a 3-hour comfort threshold.

However, the workload is identifiable.

### Likely fatigue sources — INTERPRETATION
- repeated visual targeting on the same reel landmark;
- role-follow concentration, especially ice;
- advanced REG repeated timing pressure;
- challenge-phase JAC-IN avoidance responsibility;
- baseline BET/lever/STOP sounds exposed by sparse presentation;
- economic frustration during droughts.

### Likely anti-fatigue factors — INTERPRETATION
- optional change of aiming lens/stop order for experts;
- frequent resolution at the third STOP rather than multi-minute scripted waits;
- cancellable/compact presentation;
- reward phases that preserve familiar controls;
- increasing pattern literacy reducing conscious effort on known forms.

### QA principle
`DEPTH AND FATIGUE CAN COME FROM THE SAME REPETITION`

Repeated aiming is the source of mastery and also a possible source of eye/hand fatigue. It cannot be classified as automatically good because experts value it.

## 11. Beginner vs expert: same three STOPs, different game

| Moment | Beginner watches/does | Skilled player may additionally watch/do |
|---|---|---|
| Lever | start routine | timing deviation / `遅れ` |
| 1st STOP | hit landmark | aimed-vs-stopped relation, alternative route classification |
| 2nd STOP | follow obvious role | role denial, 2-reel certainty, control hypothesis kept as field-level only |
| 3rd STOP | see whether role paid | reach-eye resolution / contradiction / bonus suspicion |
| REG | choose feasible route | optimize repeated intervention |
| RT | follow displayed requirement | extend efficiently, use BGM/flash as sensors, setting samples |
| Return | resume basic routine | deliberately change lens and search different information |

This is the strongest Playtest finding of Run 1:

**The same hardware can contain multiple practical games at different mastery levels without changing mode architecture.**

That is reusable as a design principle; the specific symbols, reach eyes, flashes and branded presentation are not.

## 13. Contradictions and cautions

1. High published full-strategy payout does not establish a beginner-friendly experience; it assumes execution.
2. Expert enthusiasm for alternate stops/reach-eye discovery does not prove novice readability.
3. A sparse presentation set can preserve signal sensitivity, but it can also expose repetitive baseline sound/aiming fatigue.
4. “All-normal-play” specialist video being watchable proves content exists for knowledgeable observers, not that 500G no-bonus play is universally satisfying.
5. Community/specialist stop-control descriptions are not manufacturer disclosure and must remain FIELD/INFERRED.
6. 2026スマスロ ハナビ material must not be substituted for 2021 新ハナビ procedure/numbers.

## 14. Unresolved after Run 1

- controlled novice data: time to reliably hear `遅れ`;
- actual miss rates for ice, REG advanced intervention and RT JAC-IN avoidance by skill tier;
- no-cut 3-hour footage with enough audio fidelity to assess baseline cue fatigue;
- controlled comparison of beginner/intermediate/expert perceived regret after technical-intervention misses;
- measured physical eye/hand fatigue and STOP ergonomics;
- exact proportion of 500G drought attention spent on reels versus peripheral display;
- whether beginner use of `たまやチャレンジ` acts as a bridge into reel knowledge or becomes a substitute for learning the reels;
- whether expert route switching measurably reduces repetition fatigue.

These remain research questions, not invitations to prototype.

## 15. Playtest & QA verdict for Case Study 02 Run 1

`DEEP_DIVE_RUN_1_COMPLETE`

The strongest evidence-supported interpretation is that 新ハナビ can sustain repeated play without an AT/CZ ladder because the basic three-STOP cycle changes informational value with mastery. Bonus, REG technique and RT do not discard that grammar; they reassign responsibility to it and then return the player to the same core surface.

The largest reality risk is equally clear: the same repeated precision that creates mastery can create intimidation, regret and fatigue. Therefore a reel-centered machine must be evaluated across skill tiers and dry spells, not only through expert highlight play.
