## Integrated answer
Shared Phase B question:

**DID THE PHONE PORT PRESERVE THE PLAYER'S REPEATED ACTION/ATTENTION CONTRACT, OR DID IT MOSTLY PRESERVE OUTCOMES WHILE USING CONVENIENCE FEATURES TO BYPASS THE COST OF THE ORIGINAL LOOP?**

Machine verdict:

**BOTH ARE POSSIBLE, AND THEY MUST BE TESTED AS DIFFERENT CONTRACTS.**

The official simulator's manual path can preserve much of the player-facing causal geography — reels, three sequential STOP decisions, direct notification, BONUS transition and the post-BONUS 32G question — while necessarily losing cabinet mass, physical button travel, reel depth, lamp optics and cabinet acoustics. Optional wait cut compresses elapsed time without necessarily delegating the actions. Auto play changes authorship of the repeated loop. Therefore manual, wait-cut and auto are not one fidelity scale.

The strongest cross-specialist finding is:

`LOGICAL STATE ≠ ACTION OWNERSHIP ≠ WALL-CLOCK EXPOSURE ≠ PHYSICAL/SENSORY EMBODIMENT`.

A port can preserve the first while altering the other three.

## Cross-specialist synthesis

### 1. Information geography survives more readily than physical matter
Visual and Game agree that phone translation should first preserve where the player looks and acts: truth signal, reels, STOP sequence and state orientation. Decorative cabinet detail cannot compensate for unreadable reels or displaced action hierarchy.

### 2. Causal timing is more portable than physical timbre/body
Sound and Visual converge on event law: input → response → reel/state change → notification. Cabinet resonance, lamp spill and physical control feel are not portable by simple reproduction. Their functional responsibilities need separate mobile substitutes and separate QA.

### 3. Three inputs are not automatically three-STOP rhythm
Playtest and Game agree that retaining three touch events does not prove preservation of the embodied reel loop. Manual touch must still support `anticipate → STOP1 → read → STOP2 → STOP3 → resolve`. Exact hitboxes, latency, occlusion and aiming quality remain unresolved without direct app execution.

### 4. Wait cut and auto are categorically different
Wait cut changes cadence. Auto changes action ownership. High-speed automation may preserve outcome uncertainty while converting repeated reel play into surveillance. Convenience must not be credited as intrinsic loop quality.

### 5. The 32G horizon has multiple exposure dimensions
The same 32 logical games can be `played`, `accelerated-played`, or `observed`. QA must distinguish logical games, wall-clock time, owned actions, listening exposure and vigilance exposure.

### 6. Simulator laboratory tools are valuable but contaminate natural-play evidence
Setting selection, forced roles and save/restart can isolate states. They cannot prove natural frequency, anticipation buildup or hidden real-machine law. `STATE ACCESS TEST ≠ NATURAL EXPERIENCE TEST`.

### 7. Mobile creates new fatigue rather than merely reducing cabinet effort
Potential fatigue shifts toward finger/hand repetition, posture, screen vigilance, audio cadence, heat/battery/frame pacing and automation boredom. These require real-device evidence.

## Reusable principles retained
- Preserve information geography before decorative fidelity when physical scale is lost.
- Preserve causal event timing before attempting to imitate cabinet acoustic mass.
- Treat manual, wait-cut and auto as separate experience contracts.
- Do not credit skippability as engagement.
- Do not credit forced-state access as natural-play fidelity.
- Evaluate resolution horizons by logical count, elapsed time, action ownership and attention cost.
- When every pixel emits light, signal salience must come from REST contrast, stable location and timing rather than glow alone.
- A port may reproduce outcomes while failing to reproduce player authorship; these require separate PASS criteria.

## Unresolved requiring direct device evidence
