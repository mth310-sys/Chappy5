## Purpose

Run 1 established reel-first visual hierarchy and restrained presentation. Run 2 established physical depth allocation, hand/gaze plane separation, peripheral energy, static optical life and the two-resolution cabinet test.

Run 3 does **not** design a Chappy5 cabinet and does not reproduce protected HANABI artwork, exact flash patterns, ornament shapes, logos or panel graphics. It closes a narrower reality question:

**How does a comparatively restrained cabinet route attention between identity, reels, instructions and state change without making every surface compete every game?**

This run therefore concentrates on:

- front-face visual zoning at REST;
- upper panel / reel / lower-panel hierarchy;
- the mini-display as a secondary information layer rather than a substitute reel game;
- RT instruction visibility and how the same hardware changes responsibility;
- blackout / flash as changes to the reel-reading field itself;
- operation-deck visibility versus motor predictability;
- what remains visually stable across normal → bonus → RT → normal;
- what can and cannot be concluded from resale photos and online footage.

## 1. REST VISUAL is zoned, not blank

### Direct observation

At a powered but non-peak presentation state, the face remains visually active through large static zones:

1. saturated crown / shoulder mass;
2. bilateral dark upper grille cavities;
3. upper identity / bonus-reference area;
4. large central identity field;
5. bright recessed three-reel rectangle;
6. narrow information / count / mini-display band;
7. projecting operation deck;
8. fixed horizontal STOP row;
9. broad lower identity panel.

No single zone needs a large actuator to make the cabinet recognizable.

### Interpretation

The cabinet spends visual energy at **different spatial frequencies**:

- large color/material blocks for aisle recognition;
- medium panel divisions for cabinet organization;
- fine reel symbols / stop relationships for seated play.

This supports:

`REST VISUAL SHOULD BE STRUCTURED ENOUGH TO IDENTIFY THE MACHINE, QUIET ENOUGH TO LET DEVIATION REGISTER.`

REST does not mean visually empty. It means the normal hierarchy is stable enough that blackout, flash, instruction and bonus-state changes have a reference state to deviate from.

---

## 2. The central reel rectangle is a protected precision zone

### Direct observation

Across front photographs, the reels form the brightest, most regular high-contrast rectangle in the central face. Decorative graphics and large identity lettering surround it rather than crossing through the reel aperture. The reel cylinders sit physically behind a front cover / bezel plane.

### Sourced fact

The manufacturer explicitly calls out changed reel arrangement and blackout rules as new play elements. Major analysis documents blackout count as role information rather than a purely cosmetic effect.

### Interpretation

The reel window is therefore not simply one display among several. It is both:

- a physical moving mechanism;
- an information surface whose illumination state can itself carry rules.

Reusable principle:

`PROTECT THE PRIMARY PRECISION ZONE FROM BRANDING NOISE.`

A cabinet can be highly branded around the work surface while keeping the exact area used for slip / symbol / line discrimination visually coherent.

---

## 3. Blackout is a change to the work surface, not an overlay on it

### Sourced fact

Published analysis for 新ハナビ documents different role-denial rules by blackout count. The official product page explicitly advertises changed blackout rules.

### Visual interpretation

This matters mechanically because the event modifies the illumination of the reels the player is already reading. It does not require a new opaque animation to cover the reels and explain that something happened.

`CHANGE THE CONDITION OF THE WORK SURFACE BEFORE COVERING THE WORK SURFACE.`

This extends Run 1's `SUBTRACTION IS A LIGHT EVENT`:

- baseline reel illumination is useful information infrastructure;
- removing light can be meaningful because the baseline is stable;
- the player can continue reading the same physical reels while also reading the light-state deviation.

### Boundary

No full proprietary flash/blackout sequence is reproduced here. Exact lamp electronics, optical diffusion, LED count and timing remain unresolved.

## 4. The mini-display adds an interpretation layer without becoming the primary playfield

### Manufacturer fact

Universal explicitly describes `たまやチャレンジ` as a **mini-LCD** feature added to the familiar game structure.

### Sourced behavior

Major analysis documents `たまやチャレンジ` as a percentage-based bonus-expectation display connected to prior reel/flash conditions.

### Direct observation

The display/information band is materially smaller than the three-reel field and sits below it. It does not physically replace the reels during normal operation.

### Interpretation

The display is best understood as an **annotation channel** on top of a reel-first game, not as a second full-size game competing for permanent gaze dominance.

Reusable principle:

`SECONDARY DISPLAY SHOULD ADD INTERPRETATION WITHOUT DEMOTING THE PRIMARY PHYSICAL GAME.`

The important general lesson is hierarchy, not the copied percentage presentation.

---

## 5. Instruction can temporarily outrank identity without outranking the reels forever

### Sourced fact

During 花火チャレンジ, analysis sources document reverse-order navigation / aiming in one situation and normal-order JAC IN navigation in another. 1geki specifically describes an upper-panel arrow instruction for the reverse-order procedure.

### Interpretation

This creates a useful distinction between **persistent hierarchy** and **temporary task hierarchy**.

At rest:

`identity context → reels → information band → controls`

During a technical instruction:

`instruction cue → reels / required target → controls`

After resolution, the machine can fall back to the ordinary hierarchy.

Reusable principle:

`TEMPORARY TASK PRIORITY SHOULD EXPIRE CLEANLY.`

A state instruction can become visually important for a few seconds without permanently turning the cabinet into an instruction screen.

This also aligns with Game & Reel's `REEL RESPONSIBILITY SHOULD OPEN AND CLOSE WITH INFORMATION NEED`.

## 6. Same controls, different visual responsibility

### Direct observation

The cabinet keeps the same physical lever, BET controls and three STOP buttons across normal play, bonus and RT. There is no evidence of a new large physical input device appearing for reward states.

### Sourced behavior

RT changes the player's required stop order / aiming duty depending on navigation and phase, while other games can be played with lower visual duty. 花火GAME keeps panel flash / warning-sound rules and reel observation.

### Interpretation

The mechanism remains fixed while **the reason to look at it changes**.

`STABLE HARDWARE CAN SUPPORT VARIABLE ATTENTION CONTRACTS.`

This is different from saying all states should look the same. The visual system may alter BGM, panel instruction, flash, lighting and information, but the learned body map survives.

## 7. Operation deck: visual restraint is enabled by motor predictability

### Direct observation

The projecting operation deck is physically prominent in oblique view, but the three STOP targets form a simple repeated horizontal row. The lever remains isolated to the player's left. The control geometry is therefore easy to locate as a body map even when the eye stays on the reels.

### Interpretation

A control does not need to flash aggressively every game merely to remain operable if its physical location is predictable.

`MOTOR CERTAINTY CAN REDUCE VISUAL PROMPTING.`

This extends `HAND PLANE != GAZE PLANE`. Visual silence around repeated controls is possible when tactile/spatial consistency carries part of the interface burden.

### Unresolved

This cannot establish switch force, travel, surface texture, tactile differentiation or the exact degree to which skilled players operate without gaze relocation. Those need physical human testing.

---

## 8. Lower panel carries identity while the upper center carries play

### Direct observation

The broad lower panel is a large luminous/graphic identity surface. It is visually substantial from aisle distance but is physically below the main reel-reading area and below the STOP row.

### Interpretation

This allows a large, colorful, hall-readable identity surface to exist without sitting behind every stopped symbol.

`BRAND AREA CAN BE LARGE WITHOUT OCCUPYING THE DECISION AREA.`

The protected-design details are not reusable; the allocation principle is.

The cabinet therefore achieves hall identity through **where** large graphics live, not only through how much animation they contain.

---

## 9. Restrained does not mean low-contrast

### Direct observation

The cabinet uses strong red, dark grille voids, bright reels, reflective side trim and a broad luminous lower panel. These are not muted materials.

### Interpretation

The restraint is temporal / attentional, not necessarily chromatic.

A useful distinction is:

`STATIC IDENTITY CONTRAST != PER-GAME ATTENTION CLAIM.`

A machine may be visually loud as an object while still being quiet as a repeated decision interface. This is one reason a normal/reach-eye machine can remain identifiable in a noisy hall without a giant moving mechanism.

---

## 10. Bonus / RT transition changes framing more than anatomy

### Sourced fact

The core flow remains bonus → 花火チャレンジ → 花火GAME → normal, with technical responsibility and RT rules changing by phase. Official/major analysis does not describe a new cabinet anatomy appearing for these states.

### Observation / interpretation

The same physical reel window, operation deck, panels, lamps and speakers are repeatedly recontextualized.

The reusable mechanical lesson is:

`STATE ESCALATION CAN REALLOCATE EXISTING SURFACES BEFORE ADDING NEW SURFACES.`

This aligns with Machine Run 2's human-cost model: extra physical spectacle is not free if it competes with the fine reel work the player is still required to perform.

## 11. Hall identity without giant movement — integrated explanation

新ハナビ's comparatively restrained cabinet remains identifiable through several layers that do not depend on a large moving役物:

### Coarse / aisle layer
- saturated upper mass;
- dark bilateral grille cavities;
- reflective vertical framing;
- large upper and lower identity panels;
- bright central reel block;
- broad luminance contrast.

### Seated / game layer
- recessed physical reels;
- regular three-column symbol field;
- fixed STOP row;
- stable hand/gaze separation;
- blackout / flash changes in the existing reel-light field;
- small secondary display / instruction surfaces.

### Temporal layer
- baseline visual stability;
- occasional subtraction / flash / instruction;
- bonus/RT reframing;
- clean return to ordinary reel reading.

Integrated principle:

`HALL PRESENCE CAN COME FROM STATIC MASSING + MATERIAL CONTRAST + LIGHT TOPOLOGY; GAME PRESENCE CAN COME FROM PRECISE CHANGE INSIDE A STABLE BODY.`

This does not prescribe a Chappy5 design. It records why giant movement is not the only route to real-machine presence.

## 12. Direct observation vs interpretation boundary

### Sourced / directly observable
- cabinet is officially `アーチャー` and machine is bonus＋RT;
- mini-LCD `たまやチャレンジ` exists;
- reel arrangement and blackout rules were changed as advertised features;
- front photos show a recessed physical three-reel aperture;
- oblique photos show a projecting operation deck and forward STOP plane;
- upper left/right grille cavities are visibly present;
- large upper / lower identity panels and peripheral luminous surfaces are visibly present;
- RT sources document visual/order instructions and panel-flash use.

### Interpretation only
- exact perceived material categories such as chrome-like / resin-like / smoked-cover appearance;
- exact depth ordering beyond what parallax visibly supports;
- which surfaces are intended primarily for identity versus state information unless behavior is documented;
- whether skilled players reliably stop without looking at the controls;
- whether side illumination materially harms reel discrimination in a dark hall;
- any assumed stereo field or woofer location.

## 14. Unresolved / contradiction register

1. Exact external cabinet dimensions, reel aperture dimensions, reel diameter, cover-to-reel depth and operation-deck angles remain unverified by primary manufacturer specification.
2. Exact material chemistry/coatings of red shell, reflective trim, transparent/translucent parts and reel cover remain unverified.
3. Exact OEM speaker count, channel routing, woofer location and low-frequency mechanical coupling remain unverified.
4. Exact side-lamp state table, color mapping, lamp count, diffusion construction and drive electronics remain unverified.
5. Resale photos cannot prove original hall brightness because camera exposure, ambient light and modifications are uncontrolled.
6. Exact STOP-button lamp semantics and whether every visible control lamp has a state meaning remain unresolved.
7. Long-session glare, reflected hall-light interference, peripheral-light distraction and reel-cover eye fatigue remain unresolved without controlled human observation.
8. Physical lever / button force, travel, texture and seated reach geometry remain unresolved.
9. Long-form online footage is useful for event-frequency context but cannot reliably reproduce absolute luminance or material reflectance because camera auto-exposure compresses the real dynamic range.
10. No undisclosed internal mechanism, hidden actuator, LED topology or protected flash timing is inferred.
