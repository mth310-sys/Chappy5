## 2. What phone translation can preserve best: event grammar and timing relationships

### REAL-MACHINE FACT

The real-machine Run 1 records several information-bearing acoustic relationships: ordinary repeated action timing; silence or reel-start timing deviation as exceptional information; direct BONUS notification; BONUS preview/changed STOP-sound persistence across STOP1/2/3; and music changes that alter future expectation.

Reference:
- `CASE_STUDY_04_OKIDOKI_GOLD_SOUND_EXPERIENCE_RUN1.md`

### INTERPRETATION

A phone cannot reproduce the cabinet as an acoustic body, but it can preserve a large part of the **event grammar** if the following relationships remain stable:

`INPUT → expected feedback latency`

`LEVER/START → reel-start timing`

`STOP1 → STOP2 → STOP3 → persistence/closure`

`DIRECT SIGNAL → certainty`

`BONUS ENTRY → sustained reward context`

`BONUS END → 32G vigilance horizon`

The most portable asset is therefore not the original sound waveform. It is the temporal law connecting action, state and acoustic response.

### REUSABLE TRANSLATION PRINCIPLE

**WHEN PHYSICAL ACOUSTIC MASS IS LOST, PRESERVE CAUSAL AUDIO TIMING BEFORE PRESERVING TIMBRE.**

A phone translation can remain legible even with different spectral output if the player still hears the right event at the right causal boundary.

## 3. Cabinet/body presence does not survive automatically

### INTERPRETATION

A real cabinet can contribute speaker separation, panel vibration, enclosed-volume coloration, mechanical reel/lever/button noise and room/hall reflections. An iPhone collapses those into a small device speaker or headphones. This changes not only fidelity but the *source model*: real-machine audio can feel emitted by multiple physical locations inside a machine; phone audio generally arrives from one compact handheld object or directly at the ears.

The app may therefore preserve informational sound while losing part of `MACHINE BODY PRESENCE`.

A translation should be evaluated across three separate questions:

1. `EVENT LEGIBILITY` — can the player tell what happened?
2. `ACTION CAUSALITY` — does the sound feel caused by the touch/STOP/state transition?
3. `BODY PRESENCE` — does it still feel like a machine responding rather than a generic mobile UI?

### REUSABLE TRANSLATION PRINCIPLE

**INFORMATIONAL AUDIO FIDELITY AND PHYSICAL-BODY AUDIO FIDELITY ARE DIFFERENT PASS CONDITIONS.**

Do not infer the second from the first.

### UNRESOLVED

No direct controlled comparison of real cabinet speaker output versus this specific app on iPhone speaker/headphones was available in this run. Exact frequency response, stereo handling, limiter/compression behavior, low-frequency substitution and output latency remain unresolved.

## 4. Direct notification on phone: louder is not the only way to remain dominant

### REAL-MACHINE FACT

The real machine uses a low-density HOME in which a direct truth signal can close a long period of uncertainty. Real-machine Sound Run 1 also found that silence/timing deviation can be high-salience precisely because the baseline is trusted.

### INTERPRETATION

On phone, hall noise and cabinet-distance cues disappear or change, but the app also competes with a smaller sound field and with device-level constraints. Dominance of the direct notification should therefore be evaluated by:

- baseline restraint;
- attack/timing contrast;
- temporary reduction of competing layers;
- stable causal pairing with the visual truth signal;
- short duration where possible;
- reliable audibility at normal device volume.

The correct question is not `did the app make the notification louder than the cabinet?` but `did the notification still close uncertainty immediately without forcing the whole baseline upward?`

### REUSABLE TRANSLATION PRINCIPLE

**A PHONE TRUTH SIGNAL SHOULD WIN BY CONTRAST, TIMING AND MIX PRIORITY BEFORE IT WINS BY RAW LEVEL.**

This is the audio analogue of the Visual app finding that every phone pixel can emit light, so lamp salience must be rebuilt through contrast and timing rather than emission alone.

## 5. Touch input removes some natural mechanical sound, increasing the burden on synthetic feedback

### INTERPRETATION

Physical BET/lever/STOP operation supplies tactile and incidental acoustic evidence before/alongside electronic SE. Touch input removes most of that natural confirmation. Therefore the app's electronic action feedback carries additional responsibility: it must confirm acceptance and preserve the action rhythm without becoming decorative mobile-UI noise.

This creates a translation shift:

`REAL MACHINE: physical action + mechanical/tactile evidence + electronic audio`

`PHONE: touch event + visual response + electronic audio (+ device haptics where present/used)`

### REUSABLE TRANSLATION PRINCIPLE

**WHEN PHYSICAL ACTION NOISE DISAPPEARS, ACTION-CONFIRMATION AUDIO BECOMES MORE IMPORTANT EVEN IF THE GAME RULE IS UNCHANGED.**

But stronger feedback does not mean longer or louder feedback; low latency and causal precision may matter more.

### UNRESOLVED

Direct app execution was not available in this run, so exact touch-to-audio latency, whether system/device haptics are used, and whether rapid three-STOP touch produces perceptibly stable audio spacing remain `UNRESOLVED` rather than inferred.

## 6. Wait cut: time compression can preserve action ownership while changing acoustic cadence

### SOURCED APP FACT

The official app offers `ウェイトカット` as a simulator option.

Source:
- https://apps.apple.com/jp/app/id6443791490

### INTERPRETATION

Game & Reel established `TIME COMPRESSION ≠ ACTION DELEGATION`. Sound must make the same distinction.

With wait cut, the player can remain the actor while the intervals between actions/reel phases shrink. This means the **sequence** of action-linked sounds may survive while the **breathing room between them** changes.

Potential experience change:

`ordinary cadence → compressed cadence → less acoustic release between repeated games`.

A sound that is comfortable at real-machine pacing may become fatiguing when repeated substantially faster, even without any volume increase.

### REUSABLE TRANSLATION PRINCIPLES

**AUDIO FATIGUE MUST BE RE-TESTED WHEN TIME IS COMPRESSED, EVEN IF THE ASSET SET IS UNCHANGED.**

**CADENCE IS PART OF AN AUDIO ASSET'S EFFECTIVE INTENSITY.**

Do not validate wait-cut audio by checking only that every event still fires.

## 7. Auto play: automation can remove the meaning of action-linked timing

### SOURCED APP FACT

The official app offers auto play; the launch release explicitly advertises high-speed auto as a simulator convenience.

Sources:
- https://www.dreamnews.jp/press/0000273931
- https://apps.apple.com/jp/app/id6443791490

### PUBLIC APP-STORE USER REPORT

A 2024 App Store review reports normal/high-speed auto, arbitrary stopping in some contexts, BONUS cut in high-speed mode, and restrictions on forced-role use during some sections. This is a user report, not publisher specification.

Source:
- https://apps.apple.com/jp/app/id6443791490

### INTERPRETATION

Real-machine Sound Run 1 identified the player's three STOPs as a clock that reveals how long an altered sound condition persists. Under auto play, the same audio sequence may still occur, but the human is no longer *causing* the boundaries that reveal it.

Therefore two experiences that are acoustically similar in waveform can be experientially different:

`MANUAL: I press STOP2 → the altered state is still present → expectation updates.`

`AUTO: STOP2 occurs for me → I observe the altered state continue.`

This is not a minor control difference. It changes audio from `ACTION-REVEALED INFORMATION` to `OBSERVED INFORMATION`.

### REUSABLE TRANSLATION PRINCIPLE

**AUTOMATION CAN PRESERVE AUDIO EVENT ORDER WHILE DESTROYING ACTION-LINKED AUDIO MEANING.**

When evaluating an app/simulator, mark whether information is `player-revealed` or merely `system-presented`.

## 8. High-speed auto may create a new acoustic mode rather than a faster version of manual play

### SOURCED APP FACT

The publisher advertises a high-speed auto mode as an app-specific convenience.

Source:
- https://www.dreamnews.jp/press/0000273931

### INTERPRETATION

If high-speed auto materially compresses or skips phases, the correct fidelity target is not necessarily `play every real-machine sound faster`. That can create an unintelligible or fatiguing stream. A simulator may need an explicit `ACCELERATED OBSERVATION MODE` whose sound responsibility is narrower: preserve critical truth/transition information while reducing low-value repetitive action confirmation.

This is an interpretation, not a claim that the current app implements such filtering.

### REUSABLE TRANSLATION PRINCIPLE

**ACCELERATED OBSERVATION IS A DIFFERENT LISTENING CONTRACT FROM MANUAL PLAY.**

Do not grade it against manual-play acoustic density without accounting for its different purpose.

## 9. 32G on phone: logical horizon may survive while vigilance cost changes

### INTERPRETATION

The app can preserve the logical 32-game horizon, but wait cut/auto can materially reduce how long that horizon occupies the player's body and attention. The real-machine experience `32 repetitions with player-owned inputs` can become `a much shorter observation interval`.

Thus Sound QA should separate:

- `LOGICAL HORIZON` — the game still resolves within 32G;
- `TEMPORAL EXPOSURE` — how much wall-clock time passes;
- `ACTION EXPOSURE` — how many human actions are required;
- `LISTENING EXPOSURE` — how many repeated baseline events the player actually hears/attends to.

### REUSABLE TRANSLATION PRINCIPLE

**THE SAME 32G RULE CAN PRODUCE A DIFFERENT EXPERIENCE WHEN LISTENING EXPOSURE IS COMPRESSED OR DELEGATED.**

App convenience can improve usability while simultaneously removing part of the studied real-machine tension rhythm. That is a translation trade-off, not automatically a defect.

## 10. Speaker versus headphones: the listening frame changes

### INTERPRETATION

Phone speaker and headphones create opposite risks.

`PHONE SPEAKER` may weaken low-frequency/body cues and reduce source separation, making short action feedback and notification transients carry more of the identity burden.

`HEADPHONES` may increase detail and isolation far beyond normal hall listening, making repeated high-frequency transients, voice cues or sharp action sounds more fatiguing than expected.

Therefore app audio cannot be judged by one output path only.

### REUSABLE TRANSLATION PRINCIPLE

**MOBILE AUDIO QA NEEDS AT LEAST TWO LISTENING FRAMES: DEVICE SPEAKER AND HEADPHONES.**

The goal is not identical sound between them; it is preserved hierarchy and acceptable fatigue in both.

### UNRESOLVED

No controlled direct listening of this app was performed in this run. All statements here are translation requirements, not claims about current mix quality.

---

## 11. Sparse presentation remains valuable on phone, but silence has new competitors

### INTERPRETATION

On a real cabinet, silence or omission is judged against a stable machine baseline. On phone, device context introduces new ambiguity: system volume, silent mode behavior, external notifications, background audio policies, headphones, and user environment can all affect whether `absence` is perceived as designed information or simply missing output.

Therefore a mobile translation that uses silence as information must maintain strong local visual/temporal corroboration and predictable ordinary audio behavior.

### REUSABLE TRANSLATION PRINCIPLE

**MOBILE SILENCE IS INFORMATION ONLY WHEN THE USER CAN DISTINGUISH DESIGNED ABSENCE FROM DEVICE-LEVEL ABSENCE.**

This does not mean every silence cue needs a visual duplicate; it means the app must not make ordinary audio reliability ambiguous.

### UNRESOLVED

The app's behavior under iOS silent-mode/ringer settings, interruptions, route changes and background/resume was not directly tested.

## 15. Handoff to Playtest & QA

Playtest & QA should not ask only whether `the app sounds like 沖ドキ！GOLD`.

Required experience comparisons where direct device evidence becomes available:

- first five minutes manual: does each touch/action receive low-latency, non-fatiguing confirmation?
- manual 100G: does the baseline remain tolerable on phone speaker and headphones?
- wait-cut 100G: does faster cadence increase fatigue even with the same nominal events?
- auto/high-speed 100G: which action-linked sounds remain, which are suppressed, and does the user still monitor them?
- direct notification: does it remain decisive without excessive level?
- BONUS: are action-required cues still dominant over music/atmosphere?
- manual 32G versus accelerated 32G: compare logical, temporal, action and listening exposure separately;
- chain/end: does the app give acoustic release before the next vigilance horizon?
- interruption/silent-mode/route change where practical: can designed silence be mistaken for device-level silence?
- extended phone use: separate `audio fatigue`, `vigilance fatigue`, `screen/touch fatigue`, and `automation boredom`.

Do not use forced-role access to infer natural frequency or emotional buildup.
