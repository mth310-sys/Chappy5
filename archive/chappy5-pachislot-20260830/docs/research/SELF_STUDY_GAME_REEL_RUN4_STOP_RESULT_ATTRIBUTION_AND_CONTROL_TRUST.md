## 1. Why this topic now

Machine Director Run 4 added `SOURCE AUTHORITY` to the cross-Director Attention Contract and handed Game & Reel a concrete boundary problem:

**When a player sees a surprising stopped result, what evidence lets them distinguish (a) their own aiming/timing error, (b) the stop order / action route they chose, (c) a legitimate internal-role / state / reel-control consequence, and (d) a platform / input / render artifact — without exposing or inventing undisclosed stop-control tables?**

This also continues Game & Reel Run 3, which identified failure attribution as the next high-value problem after reel-grammar recovery.

No Case Study is reopened. 新ハナビ remains a completed real-machine reference because its documented relation among aiming, stop order, slip, role denial and technical intervention makes the attribution problem concrete without requiring proprietary control reconstruction.

## 2. Prior unknown

Earlier work established that meaningful reel agency requires a stable action→consequence law, that internal role and observed stop form are different, and that mastery should compress the possibility space. It did not yet establish how a player can tell *why* a surprising result occurred.

We did not have a sufficiently explicit separation among:

- `EXECUTION ERROR` — the intended action route was correct but the actual press/aim was inaccurate;
- `ROUTE EFFECT` — a different stop order or observation route legitimately changed what could be seen;
- `MACHINE-LAW EFFECT` — the stop is a valid consequence of internal role/state/control under the chosen action;
- `PLATFORM ARTIFACT` — input registration, frame/timing discontinuity, rendering or resume behavior corrupted the apparent action→result relation;
- `INTERPRETATION ERROR` — the machine acted consistently but the player misread the stopped form.

Without this separation, a technically deep reel system can still feel arbitrary.

## 3. Investigation

### SOURCED FACT — real 新ハナビ play explicitly ties valid interpretation to procedure conditions

P-WORLD documents a standard normal procedure in which the player first aims a cherry-covering landmark on the left reel, may stop the right reel freely, and only needs to aim ice on the middle reel when the partial stop form leaves ice relevant. It also documents reach-eye examples whose validity depends on conditions such as 3-BET and particular stop orders. The same page documents a delayed reel-start cue as `cherry or bonus` and flash patterns whose corresponding-role denial can indicate bonus.

Source:
- https://www.p-world.co.jp/machine/database/9401

### INTERPRETATION

This means a stopped form is not self-explanatory outside its action context. A player who remembers only the final three-reel picture but forgets the stop order / BET / cue condition may classify a legitimate result as impossible.

The minimum observable event record for attribution is therefore not:

`FINAL STOP FORM`

but closer to:

`PRE-GAME STATE / CUE + INTENDED ROUTE + ACTUAL STOP ORDER + APPROXIMATE AIM + PARTIAL STOPS + FINAL STOP + RESULT`

This still does not reveal the hidden manufacturer control table.

### SOURCED FACT — published first-stop material can expose local control evidence without exposing the whole algorithm

The completed 新ハナビ Game & Reel Case Study records that 一撃 publishes first-stop control material separated by LEFT / MIDDLE / RIGHT first stop and by bonus-established conditions, with examples of press position and resulting slip count. The prior study deliberately left complete second-/third-stop priority logic unresolved.

Reference already preserved in Case Study 02:
- https://1geki.jp/slot/s_shinhanabi/47/
- https://1geki.jp/slot/s_shinhanabi/48/
- https://1geki.jp/slot/s_shinhanabi/49/
- `docs/research/CASE_STUDY_02_SHIN_HANABI_GAME_REEL_RUN4.md`

### INTERPRETATION

This provides a useful evidence boundary: local documented behavior can prove that *state-sensitive control exists* and that a remembered aim/slip relation can carry information, while the unpublished remainder must stay unresolved.

Therefore, attribution does not require omniscience. It requires enough public / directly observable invariants to reject the wrong causal class.

### SOURCED FACT — 新ハナビ technical intervention makes execution quality economically real

P-WORLD documents BIG technical intervention and REG procedures whose difficulty changes with the selected first-stop route; published complete-strategy return differs materially from nominal return. Industry coverage at introduction likewise described level-based REG technical intervention and a high-difficulty middle-first procedure.

Sources:
- https://www.p-world.co.jp/machine/database/9401
- https://news.p-world.co.jp/articles/16848/greenbelt
- https://news.p-world.co.jp/articles/17236/greenbelt

### INTERPRETATION

A skill machine therefore needs to let the player separate at least:

`I selected the harder route`
from
`I executed the selected route badly`
from
`the machine produced a legitimate low-value result`

If these collapse into one opaque failure, the published skill/value relationship may be mathematically real yet experientially unlearnable.

### SOURCED FACT — action ownership depends on expected action-effect relations, not simply zero delay

Haering & Kiesel found that repeated experience with a consistent delayed action-effect relation changes which timing feels self-caused: agency judgments followed the learned expected timing rather than a universal rule that shorter is always more self-authored.

Source:
- Haering & Kiesel, *Was it me when it happened too early? Experience of delayed effects shapes sense of agency*, Cognition (2015), DOI 10.1016/j.cognition.2014.11.012
- https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25490127/

### SOURCED FACT — self/other attribution participates in motor correction

Asai reported that when fake visual movement was spatiotemporally close enough to be misattributed as self-generated, participants attempted to control it and movement error increased; reducing feedback so that self/other attribution became difficult removed that additional error increase.

Source:
- Asai, *Feedback control of one's own action: Self-other sensory attribution in motor control*, Consciousness and Cognition (2015), DOI 10.1016/j.concog.2015.11.002
- https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26587957/

### SOURCED FACT — agency combines predictive and postdictive cues under uncertainty

Rashidi et al. describe sense of agency as integrating predictive and postdictive cues, with their weighting depending on cue reliability. Under uncertainty about action-effect timing, predictive signals contributed to later agency judgment.

Source:
- Rashidi et al., *Right parietotemporal activity predicts sense of agency under uncertain delays of sensory outcomes*, Journal of Neurophysiology (2021), DOI 10.1152/jn.00640.2020
- https://journals.physiology.org/doi/abs/10.1152/jn.00640.2020

### SOURCED FACT — cross-modal grouping can change causal/agency perception

Kawabe et al. showed that grouping of action and effect across modalities can alter perceived causal linkage and sense of agency. Additional synchronized signals can change whether events are grouped as one action-effect pair.

Source:
- Kawabe et al., *The sense of agency is action-effect causality perception based on cross-modal grouping* (2013)
- https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23740784/

### INTERPRETATION

For reel play, this supports a stronger model than `input registered = agency solved`.

A player attributes a stop to themselves / their chosen route when several cues agree:

- they remember what they intended to do;
- the control acknowledged the intended stop in a learned timing relation;
- the reel response is compatible with a known action class;
- the visible consequence fits the partial-stop history;
- other channels do not contradict the causal story;
- repeated similar actions produce learnably similar classes of result.

A single giant confirmation flash cannot substitute for this causal consistency.

## 4. New learning

### INTERPRETATION — failure attribution needs an evidence ladder

The useful question after a surprising stop is not immediately “what hidden role was drawn?” The player can often classify the source progressively without hidden-table access.

Proposed research-only ladder:

1. `ACTION ACKNOWLEDGED?`
   - Did the intended STOP register at the expected action boundary?
   - If not, platform/input attribution remains plausible.

2. `ROUTE AS INTENDED?`
   - Was the actual first/second/third-stop order the route the player intended?
   - If not, route effect precedes any interpretation of the final pattern.

3. `AIM PLAUSIBLE?`
   - Does the player know approximately where they aimed / whether the required precision window was hit?
   - If not, execution error remains plausible.

4. `PARTIAL-STOP HISTORY COHERENT?`
   - Did STOP1/STOP2 create a legal/expected observation path under known rules?
   - If yes, a surprising STOP3 can be interpreted against that path rather than in isolation.

5. `KNOWN MACHINE LAW SATISFIED OR CONTRADICTED?`
   - Does a sourced public law (role denial, stop-order-conditioned reach-eye rule, documented slip example, technical-intervention rule) explain the result?
   - If yes, no hidden table needs to be invented.

6. `REPEATABILITY / PLATFORM HEALTH?`
   - If ordinary action→feedback relations are simultaneously unstable, platform artifact probability rises and the reel result should not be used as evidence about hidden game truth.

This is not an implementation spec. It is a research decomposition for future Human acceptance testing.

### INTERPRETATION — partial stops are attribution evidence, not merely suspense

STOP1 and STOP2 have an additional responsibility beyond narrowing the result space: they preserve causal breadcrumbs. If the final result is surprising but the player can reconstruct how the first two stops changed the legal possibilities, the result is easier to attribute to machine law or their own route.

A system that shows only an opaque final result destroys this evidence even if the internal control is correct.

### INTERPRETATION — legitimate uncertainty and arbitrary uncertainty are different

A player does not need to know the full internal role table to trust the machine. They need enough stable invariants that unknown outcomes fall inside a credible law.

`LEGITIMATE UNKNOWN = I DO NOT KNOW WHICH HIDDEN CAUSE APPLIED, BUT THE VISIBLE RESULT FITS THE MACHINE'S LEARNED RULE FAMILY.`

`ARBITRARY UNKNOWN = I CANNOT TELL WHETHER MY INPUT, THE GAME LAW, OR THE PLATFORM PRODUCED THIS RESULT.`

This distinction is central to real reel depth.

### INTERPRETATION — skill punishment becomes friction when causal source is opaque

Missing value after a difficult technical action can feel fair when the player can identify:

`I chose this route → this was the precision duty → this is the observed miss/success consequence.`

It becomes friction when the player cannot tell whether they missed, the input was late, the frame stuttered, or the machine ignored them. Economic consequence amplifies the need for attribution precision.

### INTERPRETATION — source authority should survive surprise

A strong reel event is not one that looks impossible. It is one that can be surprising *inside a trusted law*.

This connects Machine Run 4 directly to Game & Reel:

`SURPRISE WITHOUT CAUSAL TRUST = SUSPICION`

`SURPRISE INSIDE A LEARNED CONTROL LAW = DISCOVERY`

## 5. Changed understanding

Previous Game & Reel model:

`meaningful STOP = action changes / reveals the visible possibility space through a stable law`

Updated model:

`trustworthy meaningful STOP = action changes / reveals the visible possibility space + the player can usually classify the source of a surprising consequence without requiring hidden-table knowledge`

The added quality dimension is **causal diagnosability**.

A reel system can be mathematically correct and still fail this quality target if Human cannot distinguish execution, route, machine-law and platform causes.

## 6. Reusable principles

- `A SURPRISING STOP NEEDS A CAUSAL HISTORY, NOT ONLY A FINAL PICTURE.`
- `FAILURE ATTRIBUTION SHOULD SEPARATE EXECUTION ERROR / ROUTE EFFECT / MACHINE-LAW EFFECT / PLATFORM ARTIFACT / INTERPRETATION ERROR.`
- `PARTIAL STOPS ARE CAUSAL BREADCRUMBS.`
- `THE PLAYER DOES NOT NEED THE HIDDEN TABLE; THEY NEED ENOUGH STABLE INVARIANTS TO TRUST THE UNKNOWN PART.`
- `LEGITIMATE UNCERTAINTY EXISTS INSIDE A LEARNED LAW; ARBITRARY UNCERTAINTY EXISTS BETWEEN COMPETING CAUSAL SOURCES.`
- `SKILL VALUE REQUIRES ATTRIBUTION PRECISION AS WELL AS TIMING PRECISION.`
- `A HARDER ROUTE, A MISSED INPUT AND A LOW-VALUE MACHINE RESULT MUST NOT COLLAPSE INTO THE SAME SUBJECTIVE FAILURE.`
- `ACTION ACKNOWLEDGEMENT IS PART OF REEL SEMANTICS WHEN PLAYER INPUT CLAIMS TO MATTER.`
- `SURPRISE WITHOUT CAUSAL TRUST BECOMES SUSPICION; SURPRISE INSIDE A TRUSTED LAW BECOMES DISCOVERY.`
- `DO NOT INVENT MISSING STOP-CONTROL TABLES TO MAKE ATTRIBUTION LOOK COMPLETE.`
- `WHEN PLATFORM HEALTH IS UNCERTAIN, DO NOT TREAT ONE ANOMALOUS STOP AS EVIDENCE ABOUT INTERNAL GAME TRUTH.`
- `REPEATABILITY SHOULD BE TESTED AT THE LEVEL OF ACTION CLASSES, NOT IDENTICAL FINAL PICTURES.`

## 7. Limits / misuse

- Sense-of-agency and motor-control studies are not pachislot studies; they support the causal-attribution model conceptually, not any specific reel timing threshold.
- 新ハナビ public material does not disclose complete manufacturer stop-control priority / all second-/third-stop tables. Those remain unresolved.
- A documented example at one press position / state must not be generalized to all positions or roles.
- Approximate aim memory is fallible; Human reports of where they pressed cannot alone prove execution error.
- Browser platform artifacts are a future implementation/QA concern. This study does not authorize a browser prototype.
- Strong feedback after STOP could increase subjective agency without proving that the reel result truly depended on the intended input; agency sensation cannot replace control-law verification.
- This work must not be used to reverse-engineer or reproduce protected third-party reel-control data.

## 8. Remaining unknowns

- How accurately can a Human report approximate press position after an unexpected result without dedicated instrumentation?
- What minimal event log would allow future QA to separate `registered input` from `rendered stop` without changing how the player watches the reels?
- How often can a legitimate uncommon stop occur before a novice attributes it to input failure?
- Which evidence is most diagnostic to players: button acknowledgement, remembered aim, slip, partial stop, payout/flash, or repeatability across trials?
- How does attribution differ between beginner safe play and expert alternate-stop-order play?
- During technical intervention, how much explicit success/failure feedback is needed before it starts replacing learning with answer-giving?
- How should re-entry after interruption temporarily lower confidence in aim memory without teaching the player to distrust the machine?
- Can a player correctly say “I do not know the hidden cause, but I trust this as a valid machine result”? This may be a stronger target than demanding exact hidden-role identification.

## 10. Possible future Chappy5 value

If Human Gate later authorizes production, this research could support a Human trial that samples surprising reel outcomes and asks, without leading category labels first:

`What do you think caused that result?`

then separately records:

- intended route;
- actual registered stop order;
- approximate aim confidence;
- partial-stop history;
- platform health / timing continuity;
- final stop/result;
- attribution confidence.

The target would not be perfect hidden-role identification. The target would be **high causal-source precision with low defensive monitoring cost**.
