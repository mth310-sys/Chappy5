# 1. Why this topic now

Sound & Experience Run 6 established:

`SAFE AUDITORY AUTOMATICITY = PREDICTABLE ROUTINE + LOW FALSE INTERRUPTION + TIMELY RESPONSIBILITY OPENING + MINIMAL DUTY HOLD + CLEAN RELEASE + REEL-PRIMACY PRESERVATION`

and handed Playtest & QA the question:

**Can a Human remain in low-monitoring reel-centered routine, spontaneously re-engage when a truly responsibility-bearing auditory deviation occurs, and then return to routine without false alarms, defensive listening or persistent post-cue vigilance?**

Playtest & QA Run 6 already separated healthy auditory offloading from defensive listening and desensitization. The remaining gap is temporal:

- detecting a meaningful cue is not enough;
- changing the next action correctly is not enough;
- the Human must also stop monitoring once the temporary responsibility has resolved.

Therefore this run studies **re-engagement and release as separate QA events**.

Core distinction:

`GOOD INTERRUPTION = CORRECT RE-ENGAGEMENT + CORRECT ACTION + CLEAN RELEASE`

not merely:

`GOOD INTERRUPTION = DETECTED CUE`

# 2. Evidence base

## 2.1 Event-based cue detection can impose an ongoing monitoring cost

### SOURCED FACT

Pereira, Albuquerque & Santos studied event-based prospective remembering embedded in task switching. Their study found slower ongoing-task responses when a prospective-memory requirement was held compared with the same ongoing task without that requirement, consistent with an interference/monitoring cost even when target-response timing was varied.

Source:
- Pereira, D. R., Albuquerque, P. B., & Santos, F. H., `Event-based prospective remembering in task switching conditions: Exploring the effects of immediate and postponed responses in cue detection`, Australian Journal of Psychology, 2020. DOI: 10.1111/ajpy.12174.

### INTERPRETATION

A Human can successfully detect a responsibility cue while paying for that success on every ordinary game through ongoing monitoring.

Therefore cue-detection accuracy alone cannot establish a low-cost attention contract.

### REUSABLE PRINCIPLE

`DETECTION CAN BE CORRECT WHILE THE MONITORING POLICY IS STILL TOO EXPENSIVE.`

`MEASURE THE COST OF HOLDING THE POSSIBILITY OF INTERRUPTION, NOT ONLY THE INTERRUPT RESPONSE.`

---

## 2.2 Cue experience changes how much monitoring people maintain

### SOURCED FACT

Loft, Kearney & Remington reported that task interference from an event-based prospective-memory intention decreased with ongoing-task experience, and decreased more when expected cues were not presented. Their experiments indicate that attention-allocation policies are not fixed; cue occurrence helps maintain the degree of monitoring allocated to a prospective event.

Source:
- Loft, S., Kearney, R., & Remington, R., `Is task interference in event-based prospective memory dependent on cue presentation?`, Memory & Cognition 36(1), 2008. PMID: 18323070. DOI: 10.3758/MC.36.1.139.

### INTERPRETATION

Repeated meaningful deviations can unintentionally train a Human to keep the interruption channel actively monitored. Conversely, extremely rare or apparently irrelevant cues can lose monitoring priority.

This creates a QA requirement beyond false alarms:

**after each responsibility-bearing event, does ordinary-play monitoring return toward its prior low-duty baseline?**

### REUSABLE PRINCIPLE

`EVERY INTERRUPTION TRAINS THE NEXT MONITORING POLICY.`

`A RESPONSIBILITY CUE SHOULD NOT LEAVE AN UNBOUNDED ATTENTIONAL AFTERTASTE.`

---

## 2.3 Responding to a cue can produce after-effects even after the cue is gone

### SOURCED FACT

Meier & Rey-Mermet reported consistent after-effects following responses to prospective-memory targets: subsequent ongoing-task performance could remain slowed, and larger task-set overlap increased the magnitude/longevity of these after-effects. They also showed that such slowing can occur without being reducible to strategic monitoring alone.

Source:
- Meier, B., & Rey-Mermet, A., `After-effects without monitoring costs: The impact of prospective memory instructions on task switching performance`, Acta Psychologica 184, 2018, 85–99. PMID: 28477841. DOI: 10.1016/j.actpsy.2017.04.010.

### INTERPRETATION

A Human may respond correctly to a reel/audio responsibility cue and still carry the prior temporary task-set into later ordinary games.

Examples of a possible residual state in a later Human trial:
- continued waiting for the same sound;
- unnecessary extra reel checks;
- repeated verbal suspicion after the relevant state ended;
- slower routine STOP handling despite no active responsibility;
- false attribution of ordinary variation to a just-resolved event.

These are not automatically “careful play.” They may indicate incomplete release.

### REUSABLE PRINCIPLE

`POST-CUE VIGILANCE IS A SEPARATE QA VARIABLE FROM CUE DETECTION.`

`A TEMPORARY TASK IS NOT COMPLETE UNTIL ITS ATTENTION SET ALSO CLOSES.`

---

## 2.4 Focal cues can be easier to detect without broad continuous monitoring

### SOURCED FACT

Research on event-based prospective memory distinguishes focal from nonfocal cues. Brewer, Knight, Marsh & Unsworth reported evidence consistent with the multiprocess view: focal cues can be detected with less dependence on controlled attentional monitoring, while nonfocal cue detection relies more strongly on attentional resources. Hicks, Cook & Marsh likewise showed that cue characteristics matter more when cues fall outside focal attention.

Sources:
- Brewer, G. A., Knight, J. B., Marsh, R. L., & Unsworth, N., `Individual differences in event-based prospective memory: Evidence for multiple processes supporting cue detection`, Memory & Cognition, 2010.
- Hicks, J. L., Cook, G. I., & Marsh, R. L., `Detecting event-based prospective memory cues occurring within and outside the focus of attention`, American Journal of Psychology 118(1), 2005, 1–11. PMID: 15822607.

### INTERPRETATION

For reel-centered play, the highest-quality responsibility cue may be one that is naturally bound to the player's existing action/evidence stream rather than requiring a second continuously monitored channel.

This does not mean “all cues should be visual” or “all cues should be focal.” It means QA should distinguish:

- **cue discovered while doing the primary reel task**;
- **cue detected only because the Human was continuously policing another channel**.

### REUSABLE PRINCIPLE

`THE BEST RESPONSIBILITY CUE CAN OFTEN BE FOUND WHILE DOING THE PRIMARY TASK, NOT WHILE GUARDING A SECOND TASK.`

`SPONTANEOUS RE-ENGAGEMENT IS STRONGER EVIDENCE THAN SUCCESS UNDER INSTRUCTED VIGILANCE.`

---

## 2.5 False or excessive alerts can produce desensitization and distrust

### SOURCED FACT

Alarm-human-factors reviews consistently report that frequent false or clinically insignificant alarms can produce distraction, mistrust, desensitization and reduced response to meaningful alarms. Cvach's integrative review and Edworthy's review both identify false alarm burden as a major factor undermining alarm usefulness.

Sources:
- Cvach, M., `Monitor Alarm Fatigue: An Integrative Review`, Biomedical Instrumentation & Technology 46(4), 2012. DOI: 10.2345/0899-8205-46.4.268.
- Edworthy, J., `Medical audible alarms: a review`, Journal of the American Medical Informatics Association 20(3), 2013, 584–589. DOI: 10.1136/amiajnl-2012-001061.

### INTERPRETATION

This evidence is not imported as a medical-alarm design specification for pachislot. It supports only a narrow cross-domain principle: high false-interruption frequency changes trust and response policy.

A reel machine whose ordinary decorative deviations repeatedly resemble responsibility-bearing cues can train either defensive listening or eventual disregard.

### REUSABLE PRINCIPLE

`FALSE INTERRUPTION DAMAGES BOTH SIDES OF THE CONTRACT: IT CAN CREATE VIGILANCE FIRST AND IGNORING LATER.`

# 4. Research-only future QA model

No Human trial is performed in Stage 1. This is acceptance-logic research only.

A later trial should treat one responsibility event as a five-part sequence:

`BASELINE -> OPEN -> ACTION -> RESOLVE -> RELEASE`

Each part needs separate evidence.

## 4.1 BASELINE

Question:
Can ordinary reel-centered play proceed without continuous suspicion?

Future observations:
- normal-game false alarms;
- extra confirmation behavior;
- spontaneous monitoring reports;
- ordinary STOP/reel interpretation pace and accuracy.

Failure:
`BASELINE IS ALREADY DEFENSIVE`.

## 4.2 OPEN

Question:
Does a legitimate deviation spontaneously reopen attention before the Human must act?

Future observations:
- detection before required action;
- free report of what felt different;
- whether attention moved toward the correct evidence/action source.

Failure:
`RESPONSIBILITY OPENS TOO LATE OR OUTSIDE THE HUMAN'S NATURAL ATTENTION PATH`.

## 4.3 ACTION

Question:
Did re-engagement actually change behavior appropriately?

Future observations:
- correct next STOP/aiming/inspection procedure where applicable;
- no unrelated compensatory hesitation;
- reel-result interpretation preserved.

Failure:
`NOTICE WITHOUT USE`.

## 4.4 RESOLVE

Question:
Can the Human recognize when the temporary duty is finished?

Future observations:
- correct understanding of state/procedure completion;
- no need for repeated confirmation after evidence has settled;
- no confusion between informational completion and decorative tail.

Failure:
`RESPONSIBILITY HAS NO LEGIBLE END CONDITION`.

## 4.5 RELEASE

Question:
Does monitoring return toward baseline after the event?

Future observations in subsequent ordinary games:
- false alarms return to baseline rather than remain elevated;
- extra checking declines;
- pace normalizes without loss of accuracy;
- Human stops mentioning/listening for the just-resolved cue unless it becomes relevant again;
- no source-trust degradation remains.

Failure:
`POST-CUE VIGILANCE OVERHANG`.

### REUSABLE PRINCIPLE

`RELEASE IS PASSED BY RECOVERY TOWARD BASELINE, NOT BY THE DISAPPEARANCE OF THE CUE ALONE.`

# 5. Re-engagement/release classification matrix

A future observation can be classified without requiring eye tracking.

## A. Healthy responsibility re-engagement
- ordinary baseline has low false alarms;
- meaningful deviation is spontaneously detected;
- next action changes correctly;
- reel evidence remains interpretable;
- after resolution, extra monitoring returns toward baseline.

`DETECT -> USE -> RESOLVE -> RELEASE`

## B. Missed responsibility
- baseline looks low-cost;
- meaningful deviation is missed or noticed only after the required action;
- next action remains ordinary when it should have changed.

`LOW MONITORING + LOW DETECTION` is not success.

## C. Defensive readiness
- meaningful deviations are detected;
- but ordinary games already contain repeated checking, waiting, false alarms or channel policing.

`HIGH DETECTION BOUGHT WITH PERMANENT VIGILANCE` is not success.

## D. Post-cue vigilance overhang
- deviation is detected;
- action is correct;
- event resolves;
- but the Human continues monitoring the prior cue class or behaves cautiously across later ordinary games.

`CORRECT RESPONSE + FAILED RELEASE` is still a QA problem.

## E. Desensitized release look-alike
- checking falls quickly after repeated exposure;
- but later meaningful deviations are also missed.

`APPARENT RELEASE + LOST DISCRIMINATION = DESENSITIZATION`.

# 6. No-leading future probe discipline

Run 6 established that naming the audio channel before observation changes the monitoring policy being measured. Run 7 extends this to release.

A future Human trial should not ask immediately after every responsibility event:
- “Are you still listening for that sound?”
- “Did you go back to normal?”
- “Were you checking more after it happened?”

Those probes may themselves keep the task-set active.

Prefer delayed/free probes after several ordinary games, such as:
- `What, if anything, were you still paying attention to after that sequence ended?`
- `Did anything from the previous sequence affect how you handled the next few games?`
- `When did it feel safe to go back to your normal routine?`

Only then classify channel/source explicitly.

### REUSABLE PRINCIPLE

`A RELEASE PROBE CAN PREVENT RELEASE.`

`DO NOT RE-ACTIVATE THE TASK SET WHILE CLAIMING TO MEASURE WHETHER IT CLOSED.`

# 7. What this changes from Run 6

Run 6 asked:

`Did audio reduce unnecessary checks without losing meaningful-deviation detection?`

Run 7 adds:

`After meaningful deviation successfully reopens attention, how quickly and cleanly does the temporary monitoring policy close?`

The complete QA target is now:

`MINIMUM NECESSARY MONITORING + PRESERVED DISCRIMINATION + TIMELY RE-ENGAGEMENT + CORRECT RESPONSIBILITY ACTION + CLEAN POST-CUE RELEASE`

This is stricter than either “player noticed the sound” or “player looked relaxed.”

# 8. Ten-part closing

## 8.1 Topic reason
Sound Run 6 made clean responsibility release the remaining QA gap after low-monitoring automaticity and legitimate auditory interruption had been separated.

## 8.2 Prior unknown
We did not yet have a model separating successful cue detection from lingering post-cue vigilance.

## 8.3 Investigation
Reviewed current Chappy5 governing/status/handoff material and cross-domain research on prospective-memory monitoring cost, cue-maintained attention policy, post-target after-effects, cue focality and false-alarm fatigue.

## 8.4 New learning
A correct interruption response can still leave a costly monitoring state behind. Cue occurrence itself can affect future attention allocation, and responding to a temporary target can produce measurable after-effects after the target is gone.

## 8.5 Changed understanding
Responsibility handling must be evaluated as a full temporal sequence, not a detection event:

`BASELINE -> OPEN -> ACTION -> RESOLVE -> RELEASE`.

## 8.6 Reusable principles
- `DETECTION CAN BE CORRECT WHILE THE MONITORING POLICY IS STILL TOO EXPENSIVE.`
- `EVERY INTERRUPTION TRAINS THE NEXT MONITORING POLICY.`
- `POST-CUE VIGILANCE IS A SEPARATE QA VARIABLE FROM CUE DETECTION.`
- `A TEMPORARY TASK IS NOT COMPLETE UNTIL ITS ATTENTION SET ALSO CLOSES.`
- `SPONTANEOUS RE-ENGAGEMENT IS STRONGER EVIDENCE THAN SUCCESS UNDER INSTRUCTED VIGILANCE.`
- `FALSE INTERRUPTION DAMAGES BOTH SIDES OF THE CONTRACT: IT CAN CREATE VIGILANCE FIRST AND IGNORING LATER.`
- `RELEASE IS PASSED BY RECOVERY TOWARD BASELINE, NOT BY THE DISAPPEARANCE OF THE CUE ALONE.`
- `CORRECT RESPONSE + FAILED RELEASE IS STILL A QA PROBLEM.`
- `A RELEASE PROBE CAN PREVENT RELEASE.`

## 8.7 Limits / misuse
- prospective-memory and medical-alarm studies are not pachislot acceptance studies;
- no experimental milliseconds, error rates or alarm thresholds are imported;
- no claim is made about exact 新ハナビ speaker mix, proprietary timing, waveform or stop-control tables;
- no eye movement is inferred from behavioral proxies;
- no Human Trial has occurred.

## 8.8 Remaining unknowns
- how many ordinary games are required for a Human's monitoring behavior to demonstrably recover after a legitimate interruption;
- how novice and expert release curves differ;
- whether successful technical intervention produces longer self-monitoring than passive state cues;
- how repeated responsibility events at different frequencies alter later false alarms;
- whether audiovisual redundancy shortens or prolongs post-cue vigilance;
- how iPhone Safari interruption/resume interacts with learned responsibility release.

## 8.9 Next worthwhile study
Machine Director should integrate **post-cue release** into the existing `MONITORING COMPRESSION / TRUSTWORTHY INVISIBILITY / RESPONSIBILITY REOPENING` model and determine whether the whole-machine attention contract has an explicit closure rule rather than only opening authority.

## 8.10 Possible future Chappy5 value
When Human Gate eventually permits real trials, this model can prevent a machine from appearing responsive and understandable in isolated events while becoming exhausting because each meaningful event leaves the player partially vigilant afterward.
