## Scope

This run studies the real 2021 新ハナビ cabinet as a physical information surface: cabinet silhouette, reel-window priority, upper/lower panels, control deck, lamps, small display, visual quiet, flash/blackout state communication, and the relationship between reel-reading and cabinet identity. No original cabinet or visual concept is proposed here.

Evidence is separated into manufacturer/strategy sourced facts, direct image observation, interpretation, reusable principles, contradictions and unresolved items. Protected art/logo details are not treated as reusable design assets.

## 1. Manufacturer-sourced facts

1. Universal lists the machine as `新ハナビ`, 6号機 A PROJECT, type `ボーナス＋RT`, manufacturer エレコ, release July 2021, cabinet `アーチャー`.
2. Universal states that the game flow inherits 5号機ハナビ, while adding RB level-based technical intervention, mini-LCD `たまやチャレンジ`, reel-arrangement changes and blackout-rule changes.
3. The official cabinet image shows a conventional three-reel physical-machine composition with a large reel window placed at the visual center of the upper/front working area, a narrow information/display zone below the reels, a projecting operation deck, and a large lower decorative panel.
4. Official/strategy material establishes that BIG is followed by `花火チャレンジ` and potentially `花火GAME`; these are play states in which the same physical reel/STOP interface remains active rather than being replaced by a separate screen-only game.
5. Blackout and reel-flash behavior are not merely decorative: published play-law references associate blackout count / flash pattern with role possibilities and contradictions. Therefore at least part of the reel illumination system is a state-information channel.

## 2. Direct cabinet observation — front view

Source: Universal official cabinet photograph.

### 2.1 Silhouette

The cabinet remains close to a vertically stacked pachislot rectangle rather than relying on a giant protruding moving ornament. The strongest silhouette deviations are the widened/angled red crown shoulders at the top, speaker-grille masses in the upper left/right, the silver/chrome side framing, and the operation deck projection.

The machine is identifiable from a distance through **color-block silhouette + luminous lower panel + upper title panel**, not by a single huge moving mechanism.

### 2.2 Visual hierarchy

Observed front hierarchy, top to bottom:

`RED CROWN / SPEAKER ZONE`
→ `BONUS REFERENCE / TITLE PANEL`
→ `LARGE HANABI IDENTITY PANEL`
→ `PHYSICAL REEL WINDOW`
→ `NARROW INFORMATION / MINI-DISPLAY BAND`
→ `OPERATION DECK`
→ `THREE STOP BUTTONS`
→ `LARGE LOWER IDENTITY PANEL`
→ `MEDAL / LOWER BODY`

The important point is not the exact licensed artwork, but that the **physical reel window occupies the central working band and is not visually subordinated to a giant LCD**.

### 2.3 Reel-window readability

The reel window presents three bright physical reel strips against a comparatively dark surrounding field. The border immediately around the reel opening is thin enough that the player's eye can stay on symbol alignment and stop position. Side-state labels and the small display are pushed outside/below the central reel-reading zone.

Interpretation: the cabinet is physically organized to allow long-duration gaze fixation on reels. Decoration frames the reel field rather than repeatedly covering it.

### 2.4 Mini display / information band

The official product description explicitly calls `たまやチャレンジ` a **mini-LCD**. In the cabinet image the screen/display content is located below the main reel window in a narrow band instead of becoming the dominant visual plane.

Interpretation: this creates a hierarchy in which the mini display can add probability/expectation information while the reels remain the primary perceptual object. This is materially different from a large central LCD architecture where the player can ignore physical stopped forms.

### 2.5 Operation deck

The deck projects forward below the information band. A large round lever sits on the player's left side; MAX BET / auxiliary controls occupy the central-left/central area; three rectangular illuminated STOP buttons are aligned horizontally on the forward face; another small round control is visible toward the right.

Direct dimensional values, switch travel and actuation force are not available from the sources opened here.

Interpretation: the hand path is compact and stable: left-hand lever action and three horizontally indexed stop positions are physically obvious without requiring the player to look away from the reel window for long. The STOP row is spatially separated from decorative light bands.

## 3. Direct cabinet observation — oblique real-machine photos

Used-machine oblique photographs show several depth planes that are flattened in the official front image.

Observed / visual only:
- the top crown and side shell project in front of the black main cabinet body;
- the speaker grilles sit inside recessed sculpted cavities;
- the reel window is behind a clear front plane / bezel and is visibly deeper than the surrounding face artwork;
- the operation deck projects further toward the seated player than the reel plane;
- the lower panel sits below and behind the STOP plane, creating a second large decorative/luminous surface rather than sharing the same depth as the controls;
- side translucent/light elements appear as separate material pieces from the chrome/silver structural trim.

Interpretation only: this produces a practical depth ladder:

`rear black cabinet body → outer red/silver shell → upper panels / transparent front plane → reel-cylinder plane → information band → projecting deck → STOP touch plane`

Exact millimetres are `UNRESOLVED`.

## 4. Materials and surface separation

### Direct visual observation

The cabinet visibly mixes:
- glossy red molded outer plastic at the crown;
- dark speaker-grille material;
- silver/chrome-look trim with high specular reflection;
- clear/gloss front surfaces over panels/reel area;
- translucent side light elements;
- printed/luminous upper and lower panels;
- dark operation deck surface;
- illuminated STOP-button inserts.

No material composition (ABS, polycarbonate, metal plating type, glass specification, etc.) is manufacturer-confirmed in the opened sources. Material names above therefore describe appearance, not chemistry.

### Reusable principle

`MATERIAL SEPARATION SUPPORTS DEPTH BEFORE ANIMATION`

A cabinet can feel physically layered even while static when shell, clear cover, reflective trim, translucent lamp, printed panel, reel cylinder and touch controls have visibly different reflection/transmission behavior.

## 5. REST VISUAL — what the player sees when nothing special is happening

The resting machine is not visually empty. It contains a stable set of always-recognizable anchors:
- red/silver cabinet identity;
- illuminated/graphic title and lower panel;
- bright physical reels;
- three distinct STOP buttons;
- small side labels / information indicators;
- narrow mini-display/information area;
- side light elements.

Crucially, the central reel field itself is comparatively regular and readable. There is no large animated character screen continuously competing for fixation.

Interpretation: **rest state is a controlled visual baseline**. Because the machine does not continuously transform the entire face, blackout, reel flash, a small display change or a changed lamp pattern can carry high informational weight.

Reusable principle:

`QUIET WORKSPACE + STRONG STATIC IDENTITY`

The working area can stay visually quiet while the cabinet remains unmistakable through static silhouette, material, panel and lamp identity.

## 6. State communication versus branding decoration

### Primarily state communication

Grounded or strongly supported:
- reel blackout / number of reel-area extinctions;
- reel flash pattern after stop/result;
- mini-LCD `たまやチャレンジ` content;
- STOP / operation illumination changes associated with playable state;
- bonus / RT information shown in the narrow display band and reel-facing states.

### Primarily identity / branding layer

Observed:
- large upper HANABI identity field;
- large lower panel artwork;
- red crown silhouette;
- silver/chrome framing;
- fireworks-themed static graphic language.

### Mixed role

Side translucent lamps and some panel illumination are visually part of identity at rest but can become event/state carriers when their behavior changes.

Reusable principle:

`IDENTITY SURFACE != STATE SURFACE, BUT THEY MAY SHARE HARDWARE`

Do not require every decorative element to communicate probability. Conversely, do not make state-critical information depend only on decorative spectacle.

## 7. Blackout and flash as visual syntax

Published analysis describes blackout count and reel-flash families as rule-bearing signals with role correspondence and contradiction. The exact full table is not recopied here because the reusable lesson is structural, not IP-specific.

Visual mechanism interpretation:
- baseline = bright readable reels;
- blackout = **subtraction** of expected light;
- flash = **post-stop temporal pattern** applied to the same physical reel field;
- contradiction = the player's knowledge compares stopped result with the visual signal.

This is a very different escalation model from `make the whole cabinet brighter`.

Reusable principles:

1. `SUBTRACTION CAN OUTWEIGH ADDITION` — removing normal reel illumination can be more legible than adding another lamp.
2. `EVENT LIGHT SHOULD OCCUPY THE PLAYER'S EXISTING GAZE` — the signal happens where the player is already reading stopped symbols.
3. `FLASH AFTER RESOLUTION EXTENDS THE STOP` — the visual event gives the completed reel result a second interpretive beat instead of replacing it with a separate full-screen animation.
4. `CONTRADICTION NEEDS A STABLE BASELINE` — rule-breaking only feels meaningful if ordinary lighting behavior is learned and reliable.

## 8. Bonus / RT visual transition

Sources establish BIG → 花火チャレンジ → 花火GAME and RB technical-intervention play. In practical play references these states still use the same physical reel window and STOP row.

Interpretation: state escalation is not achieved by physically hiding the normal game. Instead, labels, lamps, small-display information, instructions and reel responsibilities change around a stable body grammar.

Reusable principle:

`STATE CHANGE CAN REFRAME THE SAME HARDWARE`

The product can communicate a new reward/state by changing what the reels/buttons mean rather than swapping to a wholly different visual machine.

## 9. Why the machine remains readable in a hall

Direct observation suggests three simultaneous strategies:

1. **Distance identity:** saturated red crown + bright large lower panel + central title block make the cabinet recognizable from several machines away.
2. **Seated-task focus:** once seated, the largest active information surface is the physical reel window, with the mini display and indicators kept peripheral/subordinate.
3. **Event localization:** blackout/flash events occur on/around the reel field the player is already watching, reducing the need for large spatial eye movement.

Interpretation: the cabinet solves `hall visibility` and `reel concentration` with different visual layers. The whole machine can advertise identity loudly while the actual repeated work area remains comparatively calm.

Reusable principle:

`HALL READABILITY AND GAME READABILITY ARE DIFFERENT DISTANCES`

A cabinet should be evaluated at least at:
- aisle distance: what identifies the machine;
- seated neutral gaze: what holds attention;
- STOP-resolution distance: what information can be discriminated precisely.

## 10. What deliberately stays still

No source found in this run documents a major moving役物 as the core of 新ハナビ. The official cabinet presentation and long-running product grammar emphasize reels, light, flash, mini display and normal controls.

Interpretation: a substantial portion of the cabinet's physical mass remains still across normal play. This matters because **mechanical calm is not an absence of mechanism**; the physical reel cylinders, lever, buttons, light surfaces and material depth are already the machine.

Reusable principle:

`DO NOT ANIMATE THE CABINET JUST TO PROVE IT IS A MACHINE`

When reel-reading is the product, unnecessary moving geometry can reduce information quality and increase fatigue.

## 11. Connection to Game & Reel Run 1

Game & Reel Run 1 proposed:

`AIM → CLASSIFY → RESOLVE → DISCOVER → EXECUTE → EXTEND / TRANSITION → RETURN WITH MORE KNOWLEDGE`

Visual & Mechanism mapping from this run:

- `AIM` → bright, stable, central reel window; unobstructed symbol field.
- `CLASSIFY` → reel stop position remains visually available; side/minor information does not replace it.
- `RESOLVE` → three aligned physical STOP buttons and stable reel field.
- `DISCOVER` → blackout/flash/mini-display can confirm or contradict without covering the stopped result.
- `EXECUTE` → bonus/REG/RT reuse the same lever + three STOP body grammar.
- `EXTEND / TRANSITION` → state labels/lights/instructions reframe the hardware.
- `RETURN WITH MORE KNOWLEDGE` → normal cabinet returns to the same visual baseline, making learned differences reusable.

This is the central Visual & Mechanism lesson of Case Study 02 so far:

**The machine protects the visual continuity of the reel workspace because accumulated player knowledge only compounds if the same workspace remains legible.**

## 12. Reusable principles extracted

- `REEL WINDOW IS A WORKBENCH, NOT A WINDOW DRESSING`: protect its contrast, scale and continuity.
- `QUIET WORKSPACE + STRONG STATIC IDENTITY`: the cabinet can be loud as an object and calm as an interface.
- `HALL READABILITY != GAME READABILITY`: design for aisle, seated gaze and precise STOP reading separately.
- `SUBTRACTION IS A LIGHT EVENT`: blackout can communicate by removing the expected baseline.
- `EVENT LIGHT SHOULD OCCUPY EXISTING GAZE`: place important change where the player is already looking.
- `FLASH CAN EXTEND A STOP`: post-stop light can add interpretation without replacing the reel result.
- `STATE CHANGE CAN REFRAME THE SAME HARDWARE`: new states can change responsibility rather than body coordinates.
- `MATERIAL SEPARATION SUPPORTS DEPTH BEFORE ANIMATION`: static physical differentiation matters.
- `IDENTITY SURFACE != STATE SURFACE`: branding and game information need distinct responsibilities.
- `DO NOT ANIMATE THE CABINET JUST TO PROVE IT IS A MACHINE`: restraint is compatible with high physical presence.

## 13. Contradictions / cautions

1. The cabinet is visually colorful and branded, so calling it simply `minimal` would be wrong. The **working reel zone** is restrained relative to the identity surfaces; the entire cabinet is not minimal.
2. Resale photos are useful for oblique depth but may contain modifications, lighting changes, wear or nonstandard parts. They are observational only.
3. A `chrome-look` or `glass-look` surface visible in photography is not proof of its underlying material.
4. Current smart-Hanabi web pages can surface in search results. 2021 新ハナビ and later products must not be merged.
5. Flash/blackout law tables from community/strategy sources describe behavior but are not evidence for undisclosed internal lamp-control implementation.
6. No evidence in this run supports inventing physical dimensions, LED counts, PWM behavior, reel diameter, bezel depth or STOP switch mechanics.
