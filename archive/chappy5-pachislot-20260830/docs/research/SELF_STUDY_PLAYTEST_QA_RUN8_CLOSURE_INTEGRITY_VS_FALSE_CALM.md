# 1. Why this topic now

Sound & Experience Run 7 established:

`GOOD AUDITORY CLOSURE RESTORES LISTENING GRAMMAR WITHOUT ADDING A NEW THING TO LISTEN FOR.`

and handed QA a specific unresolved question:

**After a responsibility-bearing auditory deviation correctly changes the player's action, can QA distinguish genuine return to ordinary low-monitoring play from continued waiting/checking for a closure cue or false calm caused by disengagement from the audio channel?**

Playtest & QA Run 7 already showed that successful detection and correct action do not prove clean release. The remaining ambiguity is harder:

- a player may look relaxed because responsibility truly closed;
- a player may look equally relaxed because they stopped trusting/listening to the channel;
- a player may resume ordinary pace while a completed responsibility remains latent and is spuriously reactivated later.

Therefore `PACE RECOVERY` cannot be the sole acceptance signal.

Central distinction:

`TRUE RELEASE != QUIET BEHAVIOR`

New target:

`CLOSURE INTEGRITY = BASELINE RECOVERY + OBSOLETE-DUTY SUPPRESSION + PRESERVED VALID-CUE DISCRIMINATION`

# 2. Evidence base

## 2.1 Completed intentions can stop producing monitoring cost yet still reactivate later

### SOURCED FACT

A systematic review of aftereffects and deactivation of completed prospective-memory intentions reports studies in which participants no longer showed an ongoing monitoring-cost pattern after an intention had been completed, yet no-longer-relevant cues could still trigger retrieval/commission errors. The literature therefore separates **disengaging strategic monitoring** from **fully deactivating an old cue–action relation**.

Source:
- Möschl et al., `Aftereffects and Deactivation of Completed Prospective Memory Intentions: A Systematic Review`, Frontiers in Psychology, 2020. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7007322/

Related direct evidence:
- Scullin, Bugg & McDaniel, `Failing to Forget: Prospective Memory Commission Errors Can Result from Spontaneous Retrieval and Impaired Executive Control`, Journal of Experimental Psychology: Learning, Memory, and Cognition, 2012/2013 archive. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3598897/

### INTERPRETATION

A player returning to ordinary STOP speed and no longer visibly checking an audio channel does not prove the previous responsibility is cognitively dead. An old cue class may still have enough residual authority to reopen the obsolete action when something similar reappears.

### REUSABLE PRINCIPLE

`NO MONITORING COST DOES NOT GUARANTEE COMPLETE DEACTIVATION.`

`ROUTINE PACE RECOVERY IS NECESSARY EVIDENCE OF RELEASE, NOT SUFFICIENT EVIDENCE.`

---

## 2.2 Completion can deactivate an intention better than mere cancellation

### SOURCED FACT

Bugg & Scullin found that participants were better able to stop a prospective-memory intention after having actually performed/fulfilled it than when the intention had remained unfulfilled, even when the latter was explicitly cancelled. Follow-up work similarly reports more commission errors for cancelled than fulfilled/finished intentions.

Sources:
- Bugg & Scullin, `Controlling Intentions: The Surprising Ease of Stopping After Going Relative to Stopping After Never Having Gone`, Psychological Science, 2013. DOI: 10.1177/0956797613494850. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24091550/
- Schaper & Grundgeiger, `Commission errors in delay–execute prospective memory tasks`, Quarterly Journal of Experimental Psychology, 2017. DOI: 10.1080/17470218.2016.1187182. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27172855/

### INTERPRETATION

For a temporary pachislot responsibility, a legible **fulfilled end condition** may be more robust than an arbitrary “never mind” or cosmetic cancellation. This supports the current Machine/Game/Reel/Visual/Sound direction: the player should be able to see that the local action law was resolved, not merely receive an unrelated all-clear cue.

This is not a claim that pachislot cognition equals the laboratory prospective-memory task; only the deactivation distinction is reused.

### REUSABLE PRINCIPLE

`A DUTY THAT ENDS BY BEING RESOLVED IS EASIER TO TRUST THAN A DUTY THAT SIMPLY DISAPPEARS.`

`CLOSURE EVIDENCE SHOULD COMPLETE THE LOCAL QUESTION, NOT MERELY CANCEL ATTENTION.`

---

## 2.3 Deactivation can be cue/action-specific rather than global

### SOURCED FACT

Research on prospective-memory deactivation indicates that completion effects can be target-specific and that stimulus–response links matter. Later work examining deactivation found that the particular cue/context–response binding affects commission-error risk.

Sources:
- Bugg & Scullin, 2013, above.
- Bugg et al., `Deactivation of prospective memory intentions: Examining the role of the stimulus–response link`, 2020. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7523690/

### INTERPRETATION

“Back to calm” should not be treated as a global machine state. One temporary responsibility can close while another remains open. QA must identify **which duty** closed and **which cue/action relation** is no longer valid.

### REUSABLE PRINCIPLE

`CLOSURE IS DUTY-SPECIFIC; TEST THE RETIRED CUE–ACTION LINK, NOT A GLOBAL FEELING OF CALM.`

---

## 2.4 Fatigue can make obsolete actions harder to suppress

### SOURCED FACT

Scullin, Bugg & McDaniel reported that some participants made commission errors to no-longer-relevant prospective-memory cues and that fatigue-related performance patterns were associated with greater susceptibility to those errors. Separately, vigilance research consistently treats prolonged attention as vulnerable to time-on-task decline, although physiological markers are not uniform enough to import as a simple acceptance threshold.

Sources:
- Scullin, Bugg & McDaniel, above. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22799284/
- Mehrabi & Kim, `Physiological Measurements of Vigilance: A Systematic Review`, Proceedings of the Human Factors and Ergonomics Society, 2022. DOI: 10.1177/1071181322661512.

### INTERPRETATION

A closure contract that works in the first 5 minutes may fail after a long session. Three-hour QA must therefore test not only missed meaningful cues but also **obsolete cue/action resurrection** after fatigue.

### REUSABLE PRINCIPLE

`LONG-SESSION CLOSURE QA MUST TEST BOTH OMISSION AND COMMISSION.`

The completed 新ハナビ research established a useful temporal pattern:

1. did the old duty cease to bias the next ordinary STOPs?
2. did the player remain capable of detecting the next legitimate deviation?
3. did they resume routine because they trusted the closure, or because they stopped listening/reading carefully?

### REUSABLE PRINCIPLE

`SAME CONTROLS AFTER RETURN MAKE COGNITIVE CLOSURE MORE IMPORTANT, NOT LESS.`

# 4. Closure-integrity triad

A later authorized Human trial should not score release with one metric. Use three independent evidence families.

## A. Baseline recovery

Question:
**Does ordinary play recover its prior low-cost rhythm?**

Possible future observations:
- routine STOP pace returns toward the player's own pre-event baseline;
- unnecessary waits/checks decline;
- ordinary reel-reading accuracy is preserved;
- ordinary audio returns to background status in free report;
- no continuing explicit search for an all-clear cue.

Failure pattern:
`LINGERING VIGILANCE`.

## B. Obsolete-duty suppression

Question:
**When a no-longer-valid resemblance to the old cue/action appears, does the retired responsibility stay retired?**

Possible future observations:
- no unnecessary old procedure;
- no false responsibility reopening;
- no false attribution of ordinary variation to the resolved event;
- no commission-like repetition of a just-finished action law.

Failure pattern:
`LATENT OLD DUTY`.

Important: Stage 1 does not authorize fault injection or artificial cue injection. This is a future acceptance model only.

## C. Preserved valid-cue discrimination

Question:
**After release, can a later genuinely responsibility-bearing deviation still reopen attention in time?**

Possible future observations:
- later valid cue detected without advance instruction to police its channel;
- correct next action/inference follows;
- reel/result evidence remains primary;
- detection does not require permanent checking between events.

Failure pattern:
`FALSE CALM / CHANNEL DISENGAGEMENT`.

### Combined rule

`TRUE RELEASE = A PASS + B PASS + C PASS`

A player who merely looks relaxed passes none of these automatically.

# 5. Classification matrix

## 5.1 Genuine low-monitoring release

- ordinary pace/checking recovers;
- obsolete cue/action does not reassert authority;
- later legitimate deviation is still detected and used.

`RECOVER + SUPPRESS OLD + DETECT NEW = HEALTHY CLOSURE`

## 5.2 Lingering vigilance

- valid detection remains high;
- but ordinary checking/waiting remains elevated;
- false alarms remain elevated.

`DETECT NEW + FAIL RECOVERY`.

## 5.3 False calm by disengagement

- ordinary checking drops;
- apparent pace looks normal;
- later legitimate deviation detection also drops or becomes late.

`RECOVER-LOOKALIKE + LOSE DISCRIMINATION`.

## 5.4 Latent obsolete responsibility

- ordinary pace appears normal;
- no obvious strategic monitoring remains;
- an old/similar cue later reactivates an invalid action/inference.

`NO VISIBLE VIGILANCE + COMMISSION-LIKE ERROR`.

## 5.5 Global over-closure

- one local duty resolves;
- player treats the whole machine as safe-to-ignore and misses another still-open responsibility.

`LOCAL CLOSURE MISREAD AS GLOBAL CALM`.

## 5.6 Never-closed ambiguity

- neither player nor observer can identify what evidence completed the local question;
- behavior normalizes only because time passed or stimulation stopped.

`TIMEOUT != CLOSURE`.

# 6. Why hit-rate alone and false-alarm-rate alone are insufficient

A low false-alarm rate can arise from:
- good discrimination;
- conservative response policy;
- disengagement;
- fatigue;
- low trust in the channel.

A high hit rate can arise from:
- efficient cue pickup;
- permanent vigilance;
- over-sensitive response policy.

Therefore later QA should keep at least these outcomes together:
- meaningful-cue hits;
- meaningful-cue misses/late detections;
- false responsibility reopenings;
- obsolete-duty commission-like errors;
- ordinary checking/pace burden;
- primary reel-reading accuracy;
- confidence/source attribution.

Do not import a single numeric signal-detection threshold from unrelated vigilance laboratories.

### REUSABLE PRINCIPLE

`LOW FALSE ALARMS WITHOUT PRESERVED HITS CAN BE DISENGAGEMENT.`

`HIGH HITS WITHOUT LOW BASELINE COST CAN BE VIGILANCE.`

# 7. No-leading measurement discipline

Run 7 established:

`A RELEASE PROBE CAN PREVENT RELEASE.`

Run 8 adds a second contamination risk:

**testing obsolete-cue suppression too explicitly can reteach the old cue/action relation.**

Future trial discipline:
- do not announce that an old cue may reappear;
- do not repeatedly ask whether the participant is “still listening”;
- do not label the audio channel before spontaneous behavior is captured;
- do not equate a correct verbal explanation with absence of latent action bias;
- use delayed free report and naturally occurring later opportunities before explicit recognition questions.

### REUSABLE PRINCIPLE

`A TEST OF DEACTIVATION CAN REACTIVATE WHAT IT IS TRYING TO MEASURE.`

# 8. First-5-minute / 100G / 500G / 3-hour implications

This run does not perform a new play reconstruction; it extends the completed Case Study 02 repeated-play model.

## First 5 minutes

Goal:
learn one reliable baseline and at least one responsibility-opening/closing relation without teaching permanent vigilance.

Risk:
beginner confuses “there can be meaningful deviations” with “everything subtle must be monitored.”

## Normal 100G

Goal:
ordinary routine compresses; resolved local duties stop contaminating subsequent games.

Risk:
responsibility cues accumulate as a growing checklist rather than disappearing after use.

## 500G dry spell

Goal:
low reward does not convert sparse cue language into defensive scanning.

Risk:
a bored/fatigued player either over-checks every small deviation or disengages from the channel entirely.

## Three hours

Goal:
preserve both sides of discrimination under fatigue:
- meaningful new cues still reopen attention;
- obsolete duties stay closed.

Risk:
measuring only misses overlooks commission-like old-duty errors; measuring only false alarms overlooks desensitization.

### REUSABLE PRINCIPLE

`FATIGUE QA MUST ASK WHAT THE PLAYER FAILS TO NOTICE AND WHAT THEY FAIL TO STOP NOTICING.`

# 9. What changes from Run 7

Run 7 acceptance target:

`MINIMUM NECESSARY MONITORING + PRESERVED DISCRIMINATION + TIMELY RE-ENGAGEMENT + CORRECT RESPONSIBILITY ACTION + CLEAN POST-CUE RELEASE`

Run 8 makes `CLEAN POST-CUE RELEASE` falsifiable in two directions.

It now requires:

`CLEAN POST-CUE RELEASE = ROUTINE RECOVERY + RETIRED-DUTY SUPPRESSION + FUTURE VALID-CUE RESPONSIVENESS`

This prevents two false passes:
1. **quiet because disengaged**;
2. **quiet because no current cue is present, while old cue/action remains latent**.

New central rule:

`HEALTHY RELEASE PRESERVES THE ABILITY TO REOPEN.`

and:

`CLOSURE IS PROVEN BY SELECTIVITY, NOT BY SILENCE.`

# 10. Ten-part closing

## 10.1 Topic reason
Sound Run 7 required QA to distinguish genuine low-monitoring return from continued closure checking or false calm caused by audio-channel disengagement.

## 10.2 Prior unknown
Run 7 could detect post-cue vigilance overhang but still risked accepting normal pace/no-checking as proof that an old responsibility was truly deactivated.

## 10.3 Investigation
Reviewed latest main, governing gate/status material, Playtest QA Reality Baseline, prior QA Run 7, Sound Run 7 handoff, closed 新ハナビ evidence, and cross-domain research on finished-intention deactivation, commission errors, cue/action specificity and fatigue.

## 10.4 New learning
Strategic monitoring can disappear while a completed cue/action relation remains capable of spontaneous retrieval. Conversely, low checking can also reflect disengagement that damages later valid-cue detection. Closure therefore needs a three-sided test rather than one behavioral recovery metric.

## 10.5 Changed understanding
Before this run:
`release ≈ return toward baseline with preserved immediate accuracy`.

After this run:
`release = baseline recovery + obsolete-duty suppression + preserved later re-engagement`.

## 10.6 Reusable principles
- `CLOSURE IS PROVEN BY SELECTIVITY, NOT BY SILENCE.`
- `HEALTHY RELEASE PRESERVES THE ABILITY TO REOPEN.`
- `NO MONITORING COST DOES NOT GUARANTEE COMPLETE DEACTIVATION.`
- `CLOSURE IS DUTY-SPECIFIC; TEST THE RETIRED CUE–ACTION LINK.`
- `A DUTY THAT ENDS BY BEING RESOLVED IS EASIER TO TRUST THAN A DUTY THAT SIMPLY DISAPPEARS.`
- `LONG-SESSION CLOSURE QA MUST TEST BOTH OMISSION AND COMMISSION.`
- `LOW FALSE ALARMS WITHOUT PRESERVED HITS CAN BE DISENGAGEMENT.`
- `HIGH HITS WITHOUT LOW BASELINE COST CAN BE VIGILANCE.`
- `A TEST OF DEACTIVATION CAN REACTIVATE WHAT IT IS TRYING TO MEASURE.`
- `FATIGUE QA MUST ASK WHAT THE PLAYER FAILS TO NOTICE AND WHAT THEY FAIL TO STOP NOTICING.`

## 10.7 Limits / misuse
- prospective-memory laboratory tasks are not pachislot and supply no numeric acceptance thresholds;
- no Human trial, eye tracking, fault injection or browser play was performed;
- no claim is made about exact New Hanabi player error rates or auditory timing;
- “commission-like” is a QA analogy for an obsolete action rule reappearing, not a diagnosis;
- this does not authorize adding artificial decoy cues to production.

## 10.8 Remaining unknowns
- how many ordinary games are sufficient to demonstrate stable recovery without probing-induced contamination;
- how to test obsolete-duty suppression using naturally occurring real-machine sequences rather than artificial cue injection;
- whether expert and beginner closure errors differ mainly in omission, commission or monitoring burden;
- how three-hour fatigue changes valid-cue hit rate versus obsolete-duty reactivation separately;
- how to distinguish deliberate optional expert monitoring from defensive mandatory monitoring behaviorally;
- whether visual, audio and reel-source closure fail independently or as one shared task-set.

## 10.9 Next worthwhile study
Machine Director should integrate `CLOSURE INTEGRITY` into the existing Attention Contract and determine whether whole-machine closure should be represented as **selective retirement of responsibility bindings**, rather than a global state transition to calm.

Key integration question:

**How can the machine-level contract guarantee that one responsibility can close, a different one remain open, and a later legitimate deviation still regain authority without permanent cross-channel vigilance?**

## 10.10 Possible future Chappy5 value
Only after Human Gate approval, this model could prevent a future machine from falsely passing QA because testers “look relaxed.” It gives a way to separate trustworthy low-monitoring play from channel abandonment and latent old-rule interference without copying any third-party machine content.
